The $12.4bn AI-anxiety buyout day

Monday, October 19, 2026

THE LEAD

Two sponsors bought the AI dip — $12.4bn worth of it. Thoma Bravo-owned Proofpoint agreed to acquire Varonis Systems in an all-cash deal valued at $6.3bn (equity value $5.71bn), announced October 15. Varonis shareholders get $49.75 a share — a 52% premium to the June 22 pre-rumor close, though only about 5% above the pre-announcement price after months of takeover chatter. The multiple: 8.4x LTM revenue, 53x LTM free cash flow. Expected close in the first half of 2027.

The same day, CCC Intelligent Solutions agreed to a $6.1bn take-private with a consortium led by GTCR and including Elliott Investment Management. Shareholders get $7.75 a share — a 44% premium to the unaffected July 9 close and 59% above the 30-day average — with no financing condition, expected close Q1 2027. Advent, which bought CCC in 2017, SPAC'd it in 2021, and exited in 2025, is not involved.

Both targets are vertical software, and both were bought off discounts created by the same fear: that AI disrupts their economics. GTCR's own framing of CCC was the quote of the week — a business "made better by AI, not worse by AI." The read-through for alts allocators: the sponsors with the most deal-making experience in software are treating the AI selloff as a bid list, not a warning.

Sources: Proofpoint press release / Reuters (Oct 15); Insurance Journal; TipRanks 8-K summary (Oct 16)

FUND CLOSES

  • Aldaron — the new US PE firm launched by former Palantir and Carlyle executives has reportedly closed its debut fund on about $885m. (AltAssets, Oct 16)
  • OrbiMed raised $3bn across its latest US and European life sciences fund and its sixth Asian healthcare vehicle — with the Asia fund surpassing $1bn for the first time. (AltAssets, Oct 16)
  • L&G and NTR secured €330m at the first close of their fourth European energy-transition infrastructure fund, with three initial acquisitions covering around 1.5GW of clean power generation and grid-support assets. (AltAssets, Oct 15)

DEALS

  • GenNx360 × Whitsons Culinary Group — the US middle-market firm closed a $285m single-asset continuation fund for the food-services business, led by Churchill, extending an ownership in which the company has tripled in size. (AltAssets, Oct 15)
  • Fission — the tokenization infrastructure firm raised $10m in a round led by Pantera Capital, with Circle Ventures participating, to build secondary-market liquidity for qualifying tokenized private-fund interests that normally face fixed redemption windows. Announced Oct 15. (CryptoCoinShow, Oct 17)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • Issuers push back on the SEC's tokenization sandbox. CoreWeave and Broadcom are challenging tokenized versions of their shares, testing the SEC's five-year Innovation Exemption for Tokenized Securities Venues — including the issuer opt-out provision and the unresolved mechanics of shareholder lists, dividend and split handling, voting, and who controls the count of shares shown as outstanding. The exemption's symbol and volume caps also raise questions about how large a token venue can get before it hits normal stock-market rules on quotes, trade-throughs, and clearing. The first real stress test of onchain equity plumbing, and it's the issuers — not the regulators — applying it. (The Brief, Oct 16)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • Qatar keeps buying managers. Activate Capital — the US energy and infrastructure technology investor with more than $1bn across three funds — becomes the 13th manager in QIA's $3B venture programme and will open a Doha office. QIA launched the Fund of Funds in February 2024 at $1bn, added $2bn at Web Summit Qatar this February, and has since widened the remit to later-stage investments. (FwdStart, Oct 16)
  • Norway may reverse its defense ban. The world's largest sovereign wealth fund is reportedly weighing a reversal of its long-standing ethical rules preventing investment in major defense and aerospace corporations — a move that would send a signal across the sovereign-investor universe. (The Geo Chronicle, Oct 16)
  • NIIF goes long India's data centres. India's National Investment and Infrastructure Fund is preparing to invest $700–800m in data centres and related businesses, putting digital infrastructure at the centre of its second infrastructure investment cycle. (Global SWF, Oct 15)

THE NUMBER

39% — the decline in global infrastructure fundraising over the first nine months of 2026 versus last year, despite a sharp third-quarter rebound. The catch: three megafunds captured 65% of all Q3 capital raised. The rebound is real; the concentration is the story. (AltAssets, Oct 16)

MEME CLEANSER

GTCR paid a 44% premium for CCC while its own principal explained the thesis to Reuters: "a business that we think is made better by AI, not worse by AI." The AI fear trade, in both directions, in a single sentence.

The Week in Alts: Partners Group's shareholders choose the exit

Sunday, October 18, 2026

THE LEAD

Partners Group's shareholders voted to pull the plug. Investors in the Swiss manager's London-listed Partners Group Private Equity Limited backed an orderly liquidation with 99.9% of votes cast at the October 7 extraordinary general meeting — after 74.1% of eligible shareholders had already opted for a liquidity alternative, shelving a proposed restructuring. The trust trades at a roughly 40% discount to NAV, and proceeds from the portfolio sell-off are due to investors in half-yearly tranches starting March 31, 2027. The wind-down doesn't touch Partners Group Holding AG — but the same week, Bloomberg reported the firm cut or redeployed dozens of positions across its 200-person buyout team, and management proposed splitting its €6.6bn Global Value SICAV into two sub-portfolios: one for compounding, one to channel disposal proceeds back to investors. The official line is that the evergreen franchise is being reshaped. The shareholder vote says the market read it differently: this is October's real theme — the liquidity pressure that the ASIC stop orders, the evergreen redemption caps, and the Jefferies downgrade all pointed at, finally getting a 99.9% verdict.

Sources: ad-hoc-news.de (Oct 18); Reuters (Oct 6); Scottish Financial News (Oct 5)

FUND CLOSES

  • A quiet weekend on new closes — yesterday's Week in Alts covered the week's five: Sprints, Hg, StepStone, Consonance, and Aphias. The fresh money story right now is in secondaries infrastructure, not new raises. (See Edition #016)

DEALS

  • Clear Channel Outdoor → Mubadala: closed. The $6.2bn take-private completed Oct 14 after CFIUS clearance and stockholder approval; each share converted to $2.43 cash, the stock delists from the NYSE, and Wade Davis (ex-TelevisaUnivision) takes over as CEO from the departing Scott Wells. The deal closed with $1.3bn in cash to equityholders, $3.3bn of equity from Mubadala Capital-managed funds, and a $100m incremental term loan. (PR Newswire/Morningstar; Defense World, Oct 14–16)
  • Galderma → ZO Skin Health — the Swiss dermatology group agreed to acquire the Irvine, California skincare brand for $1.5bn enterprise value, all cash, from Blackstone Tactical Opportunities and the Obagi family. ZO generated $330m of sales in the twelve months to June 2026 — about a 4.5x revenue multiple. Expected close Q4 2026 or early 2027. (Premium Beauty News; AltAssets/AlphaMaven, Oct 14)
  • Benefit Street Partners × Encina — the Franklin Templeton-owned manager expanded its asset-backed finance platform with a seed portfolio of about $480m in originated commitments from Encina Lender Finance, with 14 Encina professionals joining. ABF remains the fastest-growing corner of private credit. (AlphaMaven deal roundup, Oct 15)
  • Ares → Statera Energy — structured capital investment in a 1.9GW portfolio of UK flexible-energy projects (battery storage, wind, solar) developed by Statera Energy; terms undisclosed. Institutional money keeps underwriting the UK power-buildout. (AlphaMaven deal roundup, Oct 15)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • CFTC blesses tokenized assets in customer funds. The Commission's updated FAQs tell CFTC-regulated firms that customer funds may be invested in tokenized versions of already-permitted assets — provided the token grants holders legal and economic rights "the same or functionally equivalent" to the traditional form. The guidance also extends a non-objection to blockchain-based recordkeeping. One carve-out: payment stablecoins still don't qualify. The clearest federal green light yet for onchain wrappers inside regulated vehicles. (CoinDesk, Sept 24)
  • Ondo Finance joins DTCC Fund/SERV — DTCC admitted Oasis Pro Markets, Ondo's US broker-dealer subsidiary, as the first tokenization member of Fund/SERV, the network behind roughly 85% of US mutual-fund transactions. Fund-order routing and recordkeeping on DTCC rails with a blockchain layer on top: the plumbing for tokenized fund distribution just got conventional. (DTCC press release; Ondo, Sept 16)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • MainePERS keeps buying infrastructure anyway. The pension committed $280m to a Meridiam infrastructure strategy, lifting its 2026 infrastructure commitments to $580m — even though its broader real-assets portfolio already exceeds its target allocation. When allocators overpay target and still write checks, the strategy is the priority, not the policy. (AltAssets via AlphaMaven, Oct 15)
  • Michigan writes $765m, then slows down. The $130.5bn State of Michigan Retirement System committed $765m across seven alternatives funds while rebalancing — and signaled slower future commitment pacing. Fewer relationships, larger checks, then a pause: Q2's pattern holding into Q4. (AlternativesWatch via AlphaMaven, Oct 15)
  • Illinois Police Officers' Pension approved its first allocation under a $450m non-core real estate search, with a second manager under consideration. (AlternativesWatch via AlphaMaven, Oct 16)
  • Denmark's P+ invested DKK750m in PSG Europe III, which has allocated over €4.4bn (DKK33bn) to European tech and software — with AI names like Mistral among the first investments. European pension capital keeps backing European AI. (European Pensions, Oct 9)

THE NUMBER

99.9% — the share of votes backing the orderly liquidation of Partners Group's London-listed private-equity trust. When shareholders vote almost unanimously for the exit on a fund trading 40% below NAV, it's not a verdict on one vehicle. It's the evergreen era's report card.

MEME CLEANSER

Partners Group spent the week splitting its €6.6bn flagship into "Compounding" and "Distributing" portfolios. Investors looked at the two options and chose a third: "Liquidating."

The Week in Alts: Penske caps a $37.5bn take-private week

Saturday, October 17, 2026

THE LEAD

Penske Automotive agreed to be taken private by its largest shareholders — Penske Corp and Japan's Mitsui — in a deal valuing the auto retailer at about $16.3bn including debt. Shareholders get $212 a share in cash, an 8.6% premium to the July 21 close, the day before the initial proposal went public. Penske Corp, which already owns over 52%, will hold about 64% at close; Mitsui, at over 20% today, takes the rest. Completion is expected in Q1 2027. The deal caps a week in which take-privates stopped being a theme and became the market: five signed transactions worth roughly $37.5bn, from car dealerships to insurance software to a budget airline — and nearly all of them insider-led or sponsor-backed, not hostile.

Sources: Reuters (Oct 15)

DEALS

Edition #012

Blackstone's $1bn credit continuation fund

Tuesday, October 13, 2026

THE LEAD

Blackstone just raised about $1bn for a private credit continuation vehicle — the liquidity machine has gone mainstream. Allianz Global Investors anchored the vehicle alongside StepStone, which lets existing investors exit while Blackstone keeps exposure to a portfolio of loans out of Blackstone Capital Opportunities Fund IV — a 2022-vintage fund that delivered 11% net IRR through June, per Bloomberg via Private Equity Wire (Oct 9). Evercore advised. The irony is rich: PitchBook's Q3 US PE Breakdown (Oct 5) says continuation-fund closes are tracking well behind 2025's record $97.6bn year as M&A comes back. But the industry's biggest brand just bet $1bn that the structure isn't going anywhere — it's now standard balance-sheet management for private credit, not a distress signal.

Sources: Private Equity Wire / Bloomberg (Oct 9); PitchBook (Oct 5)

FUND CLOSES

  • Conversant Capital — $845m for its debut private investment fund plus co-invests ($705m commingled, beating its $500m target by 40%+), backing real estate and real estate-adjacent businesses; SWFs, endowments, pensions in the LP roster. (Connect Money, Oct 12)
  • Apogem Capital — $472m across its real assets programme, beating target by 24%, as the NY manager expands its natural resources strategy through its fourth flagship vehicle. (AltAssets, Oct 13)
  • Gaius Capital — €50m debut fund at the hard cap plus €6m in co-invests, four months after a €40m first close; Berlin-based DACH Mittelstand succession strategy. (EU-Startups, Oct 12)

DEALS

  • One Equity Partners → Nexora — agreed to acquire the technology distribution business from DCC for $725m, per Reuters, as the Irish group keeps restructuring ahead of KKR's planned acquisition of DCC Energy. (AltAssets / Reuters, Oct 12)
  • TA Associates exits Rocscience — $535m sale, one of two anchor transactions in the Oct 12 deal roundup alongside the Nexora trade. (AltAssets, Oct 12)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • SEC's "Responsible Retailization" package (Sept 30 open meeting). The Commission proposed letting interval funds and BDCs charge performance fees up to 20% of net gains, including unrealized gains, redefining "qualified client" to include any accredited investor (opening carried interest to 3(c)(1) funds and SMAs without a separate net-worth screen), and giving interval funds more flexibility on share repurchase programs under Rule 23c-3. Five companion notices seek comment on expanding the accredited-investor definition itself. (Gibson Dunn, Oct 8)
  • SEC crypto custody proposal (Oct 1). File S7-2026-35 would let registered advisers self-custody client crypto private keys when no qualified custodian exists and add state trust companies to the permitted-custodian list — a compliant pathway, per Chair Atkins, where "none existed before." Published in the Federal Register Oct 6; comments open through Dec 7. (blockchainreporter.net, Oct 12)
  • Also on the SEC agenda: an expanded Rule 144 safe harbor for resales of restricted securities and changes to the pay-to-play two-year cooling-off period on adviser compensation from public pensions. (Hedge Fund Law Blog, Q3 2026 update)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • Danish pensions → defense PE. Danish pension funds are committing €220m to a European private equity fund to expand defense investments — institutional capital keeps finding its way into the rearmament trade. (Investment Officer, Oct 13)
  • M&G → DC savers. M&G launched a diversified private credit LTAF with £74m of initial commitments (including capital from its £138bn with-profits fund), aimed at UK DC schemes under the Mansion House Accord — 17 providers committed to ≥10% of DC defaults in private markets by 2030. (Alternative Credit Investor / IPE, Oct 12)
  • CalPERS → energy transition credit. The $640bn pension is weighing more private credit for energy-transition lending — it already holds an $800m climate fund allocation via Goldman Sachs AM, and says debt-for-green/brownfield projects is scarce enough to earn equity-like returns at debt-level risk. (Private Equity Wire / Bloomberg, Oct 6)

THE NUMBER

83% — the share of US venture investment that went to AI companies in the first nine months of 2026, per AltAssets' Oct 9 roundup of the record $515.8bn nine-month VC total. A record fundraising market that is, functionally, a single-sector market.

MEME CLEANSER

Blackstone's new vehicle lets old credit investors take liquidity while Blackstone keeps the loans — the continuation fund was invented so managers never have to sell, and the industry's biggest name just used it on its own credit book. Private markets didn't solve the liquidity problem. It figured out how to charge for the waiting.
