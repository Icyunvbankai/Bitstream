Edition #033

Lazard CL is live, and the secondaries machine gets a third global business

Tuesday, November 3, 2026

THE LEAD

$575 million. That is the base price Lazard paid to close its acquisition of Campbell Lutyens on November 2, with up to a further $85 million tied to performance targets — and the deal instantly creates Lazard CL, the bank's third global business alongside financial advisory and asset management. The combined unit pairs Lazard's in-house private capital advisory group with Campbell Lutyens' fund placement, secondary advisory and GP capital advisory franchises, co-led by Holcombe Green and Gordon Bajnai.

Why now is the number underneath it: Lazard's own Interim 2026 Secondary Market Report put first-half 2026 secondary volume at about $124 billion, up roughly 28% year on year and a first-half record. Lazard CL expects about $500 million in combined 2027 revenue — a company estimate, not a reported result — from a platform of more than 300 advisory professionals across 19 offices. When the market's scarcest commodity is liquidity, the advisers who intermediate it are consolidating. The fee pool followed the volume; now the firms are following the fee pool.

Sources: Lazard press release (Apr 30); Reuters (Apr 30); FinanceX Magazine (Nov 2)

FUND CLOSES

  • Igneo EDIF IV — €6 billion, hard cap hit in six months. Igneo Infrastructure Partners closed its fourth European Diversified Infrastructure Strategy at its €6 billion hard cap, oversubscribed against a €4 billion target, with commitments from more than 90 investors across Europe, Asia-Pacific and North America. The fund, about 11% larger than EDIF III, made its first investment in September 2026 with the acquisition of Neumann Group, a German incinerator bottom ash recycling platform. (Igneo press release, Nov 2)
  • Churchill Junior Capital Opportunities Fund III — $1.54 billion final close. Churchill Asset Management, Nuveen's private capital arm, closed its largest junior capital fund to date on November 2, beating its $1 billion target by more than 50% and more than doubling the $737 million predecessor. About $665 million came through rated feeder structures for insurance investors; roughly $600 million is already deployed across 44 portfolio companies alongside 30 private equity firms. (Churchill press release, Nov 2)
  • Spark Midas Fund II — ₹1,649 crore first close. Spark Capital's second late-stage growth fund completed its first close on November 2, above its ₹1,500 crore target after the full target was subscribed in approximately eight weeks; the fund size has been increased to ₹2,000 crore. It targets profitable, well-governed companies typically 12 to 36 months from a potential liquidity event. (Press Network of India, Nov 3)

DEALS

  • CD&R and GTCR still circling Global Payments — ~$40 billion enterprise value, no deal yet. No transaction had been announced as of Monday, November 2, per PYMNTS, and Global Payments reports Q3 earnings this week — the next hard data point before any formal offer talk firms up. The William Blair caveat from Friday still stands: nearly $22 billion in net debt puts completion odds "comfortably below" 50%. (Financial Times via TipRanks, Oct 30; PYMNTS, Nov 2)
  • Blackstone + Canadian pensions put $3.1 billion into LNG Canada. A Blackstone-led group including CPP Investments, OMERS, IMCO and PSP will invest $3.1 billion to acquire a 49.9% interest in Mitsubishi Corp's holding company for its stake in LNG Canada, the Shell-led British Columbia export project. Mitsubishi values its stake at about $1.6 billion, with the remainder supporting the Phase 2 expansion that doubles facility output to 28 million tons a year; the deal is expected to close in November. (BOE Report, Nov 2)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • Retailization countdown: 31 days to December 4, 34 to December 7. No new SEC rulemaking since the September 30 package — the comment deadlines stand at December 4 (interval fund modernization + accredited-investor pathways, including the proposed FINRA exam) and December 7 (performance fees in regulated funds). The package would let advisers charge performance fees to regulated funds and BDCs, broaden "qualified client" to include accredited investors, and modernize interval-fund liquidity — none of it is final. (JD Supra / ArentFox Schiff; Proskauer; Holland & Knight; prior editions)
  • Tokenization's numbers problem, in one chart. TokenBank's November 2 market-size roundup shows why every tokenization headline disagrees: RWA.xyz counted $38.6 billion in freely held tokens on September 28, but $358 billion once assets merely recorded on a ledger are added — same source, same day, a 9x gap. The takeaway for allocators: compare only figures with the same date and the same scope, and treat any single "market size" without a methodology footnote as marketing. (TokenBank, Nov 2; RWA.xyz via TokenBank)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • New Jersey Pension Fund, still deploying. The New Jersey Division of Investment, managing the $87.3 billion state pension fund, committed up to $400 million across two private-markets allocations announced October 28: up to $250 million to American Industrial Partners Capital Fund IX and up to $150 million to TPG Real Estate Partners V, both existing managers, both subject to legal review and negotiations. (AlphaMaven, Oct 29)
  • LGT hands the keys to a secondaries veteran. LGT Capital Partners named Sascha Gruber its next chief executive, succeeding Roberto Paganoni after almost three decades leading the $120 billion-plus alternatives business. The succession puts a secondaries hand atop a firm whose growth engine sits squarely in the market Lazard just bet $575 million on. (AltAssets, Nov 2)

THE NUMBER

$124 billion — first-half 2026 secondary transaction volume, per Lazard's Interim 2026 Secondary Market Report, up roughly 28% year on year and a first-half record. Evercore counted about $121 billion over the same period, split $65 billion GP-led and $56 billion LP-led — two advisers, nearly the same answer. Full-year forecasts point to roughly $250 billion, after a record of more than $220 billion in 2025. Liquidity is no longer private markets' side business; it is the growth business.

MEME CLEANSER

Lazard spent $575 million to advise on other people's liquidity problems; Igneo raised €6 billion in six months to buy European infrastructure; and New Jersey wrote $400 million of cheques while its lawyers still review them. Private markets this Tuesday: the money moves fast, the paperwork moves faster, and the only thing nobody can buy is more time — which is exactly why the secondaries market exists.

Edition #032

Del Vecchio's €22B Delfin play, and the $40B payments deal that isn't — yet

Monday, November 2, 2026

THE LEAD

€22 billion. That is the financing package Leonardo Maria Del Vecchio has secured from Citi to buy out the other shareholders in Delfin, the holding company that sits atop EssilorLuxottica — $24.59 billion at Monday's rate — according to a person close to the businessman cited by Reuters on Monday. Earlier the same day, Italian newspaper QN published Del Vecchio's letter to Delfin shareholders offering to acquire their stakes, without disclosing financial details.

The aim, per the source, is to acquire five or six shareholdings, giving Del Vecchio greater control over decisions that have so far largely rested with Delfin's board. Citi declined to comment. Nothing here is a done deal — no price to minorities is public, and a financing package is not a signed purchase — but a €22 billion cheque lined up before the letter even landed is the market's signal for how serious this is. The world's largest eyewear empire may be about to get a much smaller boardroom.

Sources: Reuters (Nov 2); QN via Reuters (Nov 2)

FUND CLOSES

  • One Equity Partners VIII — $2.75 billion, oversubscribed. The middle-market firm closed its eighth fund with committed capital of $2.75 billion, raised in 12 months from LPs in 25 countries, with the hard cap increased on demand. It is OEP's largest independent fund since its 2015 spin-out from JPMorgan, up from $1.75 billion for Fund VII (2019), and 12 platform investments are already closed with a 13th signed. (One Equity Partners press release)
  • Tanmiya Capital Fund II — $130 million final close, above target. The Egyptian growth-equity fund closed above its $120 million target after a $37.5 million commitment from the European Investment Bank's EIB Global arm, alongside the EBRD, IFC, the Egyptian-American Enterprise Fund and MSMEDA. The fund will target six to eight established mid-market Egyptian companies across healthcare, fintech, consumer goods, logistics and transport. (Wamda / EIB press release)

DEALS

  • CD&R and GTCR circle Global Payments — ~$40 billion enterprise value, no deal yet. The Financial Times reported Friday that Clayton, Dubilier & Rice has teamed up with GTCR, Global Payments' largest shareholder at roughly 15%, on a joint take-private approach. Shares jumped 11% Friday to close at $96.84, their best session since February. The caveats matter more than the pop: talks are ongoing, no agreement is certain, no terms are disclosed, and William Blair puts a take-private price near $101 a share with odds of completion "comfortably below" 50%, citing nearly $22 billion in net debt. Global Payments reports Q3 earnings November 4 — the next hard data point. (Financial Times via TipRanks / Stocktwits / citybiz, Oct 30)
  • Hexagon closes the $1.45 billion Waygate deal. Hexagon completed its all-cash acquisition of Waygate Technologies from Baker Hughes, consolidating it on November 1. Waygate — non-destructive testing, ~1,500 employees across 25 locations, about $630 million in fiscal 2025 revenue — dilutes Manufacturing Intelligence margins by about 300 basis points on a pro forma basis; Hexagon's plan is to lift it toward the unit's 21–24% EBITAC target. (Dealroom, Nov 2)
  • Secondaries, in the buyer's own words. Hamilton Lane has raised about $5 billion for its sixth secondary fund, part of a ~$20 billion secondary platform, co-head Keith Brittain told Calcalist. The market has grown from ~$10 billion in volume in 2010 to ~$240 billion today — and the driver is distributions: LPs historically got back ~22% of NAV a year; over the past three to four years that has fallen to 10–15%. (Calcalist, Nov 1)

The package would let advisers charge performance fees to regulated funds and BDCs, broaden "qualified client" to include accredited investors, and modernize interval-fund liquidity — none of it is final. The queue belongs to one US vehicle, not the whole group: Partners Group's private-credit evergreen feeders pulled in a combined $875 million in 2026. Fundraising and exit pressure, same house, same quarter. (ad-hoc-news.de / SEC filing, Nov 2)

Q3 filings landing in mid-November will show how much was repaid, refinanced or extended. The maturity wall is not a forecast — it is a filing deadline.

Edition #031

The Week in Alts, Sunday edition: ADT's $10-a-share terms, and the go-shop clock

Sunday, November 1, 2026

THE LEAD

$10 a share. That is the cash price Thrive Holdings agreed for ADT — $7.6 billion in equity value, $15.3 billion including debt — and the fine print, published October 31, is where the weekend's story lives. The price is a 45.6% premium to ADT's unaffected $6.87 close on October 27, and a 52.1% premium to its 30-day volume-weighted average price through that date. ADT shares closed at $9.87 on October 29, up 33.2% on the announcement — thirteen cents below the offer, the market's small but visible discount for deal risk and time.

The structure is built for certainty: financing comes solely from funds managed by affiliates of Apollo Global Management, and the deal carries no financing condition. Closing is targeted for the first quarter of 2027, subject to ADT shareholder approval and US antitrust clearance. And there is one live escape hatch: ADT may solicit competing proposals through November 28 under a 30-day go-shop. Apollo, which took ADT private in 2016, relisted it in 2018, and sold its remaining stake in May 2026, is now financing its return to private hands. The circle, closed with someone else's equity cheque.

Sources: Bitcomme (Oct 31, reproducing the ADT announcement); MLQ (Oct 30); Tekmono (Oct 30)

FUND CLOSES

  • A quiet weekend on new closes — yesterday's Week in Alts covered the week's closes, led by Advent's ~$30B complex, Ares' $8B first close, and ACORE's $1.1B opportunistic credit final close. No new close was published in Saturday's AltAssets roundup. The fresh money story right now is what happens after the close — see the deployment squeeze below. (See Edition #030)

DEALS

  • Thrive's ADT take-private, in full. All-cash, $10 per share, board unanimously approved, ADT to delist from the NYSE on closing; Thrive says ADT's existing customer data protection and privacy policies will remain unchanged, with no new data sharing without customer permission. The go-shop runs to November 28 — the only date that matters between now and the shareholder vote. (Bitcomme, Oct 31)
  • The week's other deals, recapped: Triton's $9B Trench exit to GE Vernova and the Trian-led $3.8B Wendy's proposal, both covered in Edition #030. Nothing new on either over the weekend.

REGULATION & POLICY

  • SEC staff clarify the tokenized-stock notice rule: 30 days, and issuers get their objection window. Guidance published October 20 confirms a tokenized securities venue must publish a public notice at least 30 calendar days before starting operations, and may make a tokenized stock available only once the issuer's objection period has passed without an objection. Trading in a tokenized stock must also stop when the underlying stock is halted on its primary exchange. (CoinStats, Oct 20, summarising SEC staff guidance)
  • Tokenization joins the FY2027 exam list for market infrastructure. The SEC's Division of Examinations, in priorities released October 23, named tokenized securities among the areas it will examine at clearing agencies and Regulation SCI firms, alongside default risk management, securities lending, extended trading hours and quantum computing. Five weeks after the September 17 Innovation Exemption, the exam programme is already writing the follow-up chapter. (CryptoWeek, Oct 23)
  • Retailization countdown: 33 days to December 4, 36 to December 7. No new SEC rulemaking since the September 30 package — the comment deadlines stand at December 4 (interval fund modernization + accredited-investor pathways, including the proposed FINRA exam) and December 7 (performance fees in regulated funds). (Stradley Ronon, Oct 13; Duane Morris via Mondaq, Oct 23; Greenberg Traurig via Mondaq, Oct 26; Editions #019–#030)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • New Jersey Pension Fund commits up to $400M — to managers it already knows. The $87.3 billion fund's Division of Investment announced October 28: up to $250 million to American Industrial Partners Capital Fund IX (North American capital equipment, metals, mining, transportation and logistics) and up to $150 million to TPG Real Estate Partners V. Both commitments are subject to legal review and negotiations, and both go to existing managers — consolidation, again, as the allocator strategy. (AlphaMaven / Pensions & Investments, Oct 29)
  • 84% of LPs still plan to commit at or above last year's pace — distributions are the constraint. Goldman Sachs' Alternative Investment Project survey, released October 22, found 84% of LPs plan to execute more fund commitments than last year in count terms, with 37% expecting to increase pace and 47% to maintain it; only 11% plan to reduce. But 35% cited lack of distributions as the most decisive factor in their plans, and 48% said they were under-allocated to private equity loans. Intent is holding; cash flow is the negotiation. (Goldman Sachs survey via AlphaMaven, Oct 22)
  • On-chain: tokenized private credit is now the fastest-growing major RWA category. Total on-chain real-world-asset value rose 2.92% to $34.5 billion in October, after a record $34.9 billion on October 16; private credit rose 11.8% to $5.27 billion, the strongest major category, while tokenized bonds and money market funds fell to $16.97 billion — below 50% market share for the first time since March. Tokenized stocks grew to $4.89 billion even as average daily on-chain equity trading volume fell 39.3% to $317 million. (CoinDesk Research, Oct 28)

THE NUMBER

$129.15 billion — direct-lending funds raised in the first nine months of 2026, up 35% year over year and the highest nine-month total in Preqin's data series back to 2016, per Reuters (Oct 15). Against it: third-quarter direct-lending volume fell 28% year over year to $49.77 billion (PitchBook/LCD), and $16.39 billion of direct-lending loans were refinanced into the broadly syndicated market in Q3, versus just $2.60 billion moving the other way. The industry raised a record pile and then watched borrowers walk back to the banks. Deployment, not fundraising, is now the scarce skill.

MEME CLEANSER

ADT trades thirteen cents below a $10 offer with Apollo financing the deal, a go-shop open until November 28, and a first-quarter close pencilled in. Private markets this weekend: the biggest deal of the week is already priced like a done deal, minus a rounding error for lawyers, antitrust, and anyone with a better $10.01.

Edition #029

Advent's $30 billion answer to the fundraising winter

Friday, October 30, 2026

THE LEAD

$30 billion. That is what Advent International raised across its global fund complex, announced Thursday — nearly $27 billion for its 11th flagship fund, Advent International GPE XI, past its $26 billion target, plus more than $3 billion for a new mid-market strategy, the firm's first fund dedicated to smaller deals. In a year when most managers describe fundraising as demanding, Advent closed one of the largest buyout raises on record and added a whole new lane at the same time.

The mid-market fund is the tell: it will write $100 million to $350 million in equity into companies with enterprise values up to $1.25 billion, mainly in North America and Europe, using the same sector teams as the flagship. The 2026 scorecard behind the raise — about $13 billion realized across 27 businesses, about $10 billion invested across 38 — is why LPs answered. "The trust our investors have placed in us in one of the most demanding fundraising environments reflects the consistency of our track record and our discipline in returning capital," said James Brocklebank, managing partner and co-chair of Advent's executive committee. Advent manages more than $109 billion in assets. The message to the rest of the market: the fundraising winter is real, but it is not evenly distributed.

Sources: Connect Money (Oct 29)

FUND CLOSES

  • Ares — $8B first close for its newest flagship direct lending fund, targeting $10–12B in equity. Bloomberg reports the raise, via Ares Senior Direct Lending Fund IV and Ares Senior Direct Lending Core — the firm's first open-ended senior direct lending fund for institutions, both launched in April — is running faster than its previous record-setting vintage, and would represent roughly $20 billion once leverage is factored in. Treat the figures as reported, not confirmed by the manager. (Bloomberg via TradersUnion / Dealroom, Oct 29)
  • HC Partners — €100M hard-cap close for Fund III, beating its €80M target. The Dutch firm's third vehicle nearly doubled the size of its flagship fund, with commitments from all of its existing institutional investors, following two sponsor exits. (AltAssets, Oct 29)
  • Foundation Credit — ~$540M close for its second infrastructure debt fund, beating a $500M target. The US private credit manager closed the fund a week after announcing a majority investment from fellow alternative asset manager New Holland Capital. (AltAssets, Oct 29)

DEALS

Edition #028

Disruptive's $10 billion bet: ten companies, no diversification, no apologies

Thursday, October 29, 2026

THE LEAD

$10 billion. That is what Dallas-based Disruptive raised for its first-ever blind-pool fund, announced Tuesday — a vehicle that will back roughly 10 late-stage companies with checks of up to $1 billion each, concentrated in AI, AI infrastructure, defense tech and enterprise software. The structural shift is the story: founded in 2012 by Alex Davis, Disruptive spent 14 years raising money deal-by-deal through special-purpose vehicles. Now LPs are committing capital without knowing which companies it will buy.

The trigger, Davis told Axios, was the SPV market itself — with giants like SpaceX heading toward public listings, he worried small investors who bought in through layers of intermediaries could find those intermediaries gone. The track record LPs bought into: Bloomberg reports Disruptive has returned more than $6 billion on roughly $1.9 billion invested, across names including Palantir, Airbnb, Spotify, Groq and ElevenLabs. The Dallas Business Journal calls it the largest venture fund ever raised in Texas. The concentration math is the whole thesis and the whole risk: ten positions, each near the cap, in the two hottest sectors on earth. Diversification is somebody else's strategy.

Sources: TechCrunch (Oct 27); AltAssets (Oct 28); Axios via Dealroom (Oct 28)

FUND CLOSES

  • AP Ventures — $300M final close for Fund III, targeting industrial tech VC. The London-based industrial technology investor's third fund landed just below the $316 million raised for its predecessor. Industrial tech is raising steadily while consumer VC still waits for its turn. (AltAssets, Oct 28)
  • Polus Capital — ~$600M gathered so far for second special situations fund. The Mediobanca-owned alternative credit manager is reported to have raised about $600 million to date, having returned to market after deploying almost all of its €890 million predecessor. Treat the figure as reported, not confirmed by the manager. (AltAssets, Oct 29)

DEALS

  • KKR closes its first private credit continuation vehicle — $460M+, led by AllianzGI. The vehicle holds the assets of KKR Lending Partners III, a $1.5 billion direct lending fund that finished fundraising in 2017. Existing investors could take liquidity or roll on substantially the same terms; the fund posted an 11.0% net IRR and 1.7x MOIC as of June 30, 2026, per a KKR 10-Q cited by PitchBook. Evercore advised; Debevoise acted as counsel. (PitchBook / Debevoise, Oct 28)
  • PGIM and LGT co-lead a $680M Tree Line private credit continuation vehicle. The vehicle gives existing investors across several Tree Line direct lending funds a liquidity option while the firm keeps managing the portfolio — primarily senior secured, first-lien loans to more than 60 US lower-middle-market companies. PGIM said earlier this year it plans to deploy up to $1 billion into private credit secondaries over two years. (Alternative Credit Investor, Oct 28)
  • General Catalyst and JPMorganChase lead a $2.1B financing for Re:Build. Private markets on a Thursday: concentration at the top, liquidity engineering in the middle, and a regulator taking notes at the door.

Advent's $30 billion says the exit drought is over — for the biggest names, at least

Wednesday, October 28, 2026

THE LEAD

$30 billion. That is what Advent raised across its latest fund complex, announced Tuesday — $27 billion for its 11th global buyout fund, GPE XI, the largest buyout fund closed so far in 2026, plus more than $3 billion for a new mid-market strategy launched in March. The raise lands against the exact headwind Reuters names: a prolonged exit slowdown that has starved investors of distributions and left them with less to commit to new funds.

Advent beat it anyway, with capital from pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, endowments, institutional managers, family offices and high-net-worth individuals. GPE XI will invest primarily across Europe and North America, expanding in Asia-Pacific and Latin America; the mid-market vehicle writes $100–350 million equity checks into North American and European companies with enterprise values up to $1.25 billion. The read-across is uncomfortable for everyone below the top tier: in a distribution drought, LPs are not pulling back from private markets — they are concentrating into the names they are most certain will return capital.

Sources: Reuters (Oct 27)

FUND CLOSES

  • Pantheon — $6B for Global Secondaries Fund VIII, its biggest secondaries raise ever. The final close came in 71% above the fund's $3.5 billion target and nearly twice the $3.25 billion raised for Fund VII in November 2023, with a significant share of capital from investors in Asia and the Middle East. Secondaries is no longer the sidecar — it is where the liquidity is. (PE Professional, Oct 27)
  • Town Lane — $1.9B for second real estate fund, at its hard cap. The close came in well above the initial $1.5 billion target and more than 50% larger than its predecessor fund. Real estate fundraising is not dead; it is consolidating into managers who can fill a hard cap. (AltAssets, Oct 27)
  • Tanmiya Capital — $130M for second Egypt growth fund, above its $120M target. The Egyptian manager's close was backed by a $37.5 million commitment from the European Investment Bank. Development finance remains the anchor tenant for frontier-market growth equity. (AltAssets, Oct 27)
  • Maycomb Capital — $100M final close for Community Outcomes Fund II, at target. The impact private credit fund lends to mission-aligned nonprofits and for-profits across early childhood, education, workforce development and health equity, anchored by the Ballmer Group and UnitedHealth Group, with commitments from corporations, financial institutions, foundations, family offices and individuals. (ACCESS Newswire, Oct 27)

The vault opens today, October 28, giving retail investors access to a $549 million intellectual-property royalty fund managed by Partners Group — 53 investments, more than 100,000 royalty streams — via ipUSD and an IP Vault. Yesterday's preview becomes today's retailization test. (Wisevoter, Oct 27)

Treat the discount as reported, not confirmed by the parties. (Wisevoter / We Are Main Street, Oct 27)

The Pennsylvania Public School Employees' Retirement System approved the slate on October 27 — another large public pension writing big checks to brand-name buyout while the exit drought persists.
