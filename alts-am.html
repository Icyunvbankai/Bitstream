Edition #021

Pantheon raised $6B to buy the private-markets exit queue

Thursday, October 22, 2026

THE LEAD

Pantheon just closed the largest secondaries fundraise in its history — and the market is screaming for liquidity. Pantheon Global Secondaries Fund VIII and associated vehicles held a final close at $6 billion on October 21 — 71% above its $3.5 billion target — with significant commitments from new investors across Asia and the Middle East. The raise follows ~$3.35 billion of secondaries deployment across 17 transactions in 2025, which Pantheon called a record, and caps nearly four decades in the strategy: the firm made its first private-equity secondaries investment in 1988.

Read it two ways: (1) secondaries is no longer the release valve — it IS the market, with a $6B war chest now sitting on Pantheon's ~$41 billion private-equity platform; (2) LPs are paying up for someone else to take the exit queue off their hands. "Our clients value our ongoing focus on the mid-market and our differentiated, longstanding relationships with hard-to-access, top-performing GPs," said Florence Dard, Chief Client Officer, in the announcement.

Sources: Pantheon press release via GlobeNewswire (Oct 21)

FUND CLOSES

  • Pantheon — closed Pantheon Global Secondaries Fund VIII and associated vehicles at $6B (71% above its $3.5B target), its largest-ever PE secondaries fundraise; blended LP-led/GP-led strategy targeting mid-market buyouts globally. ~$84B AUM (as of March 31, 2026). New investors from Asia and the Middle East. (GlobeNewswire, Oct 21)
  • IIFL Capital — held the first close of its maiden late-stage private equity fund at ~₹500 crore, targeting average deal sizes of ₹50–100 crore; two transactions already committed with 7–8 more in the pipeline, riding India's primary-market revival. (PR Newswire India, Oct 22)

DEALS

  • EQT takes QIAGEN private for $10.8B. Sweden's EQT agreed to acquire Dutch molecular-testing company QIAGEN for ~€9.6 billion ($10.78 billion) at €46.50 per share — an 8.6% premium to the last close — ending years of takeover speculation. Affiliates of the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority and Canada Pension Plan Investment Board are participating; QIAGEN's board approved the offer. Enterprise value ~$12.1B including ~$1.1B net debt; expected close mid-2027; EQT says equity and debt financing are secured. Context: Thermo Fisher agreed to buy QIAGEN for ~$11.5B in 2020 but walked away after failing to win enough shareholder support. (Reuters, Oct 21)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • Retailization clock keeps ticking — no new moves. No new rulemaking since the SEC's September 30 proposals; the comment deadlines still stand: December 4 (interval fund modernization + accredited-investor pathways) and December 7 (performance fees in regulated funds). About six weeks to weigh in. (Covered in #019/#020; memos from Greenberg Traurig, Simpson Thacher, Debevoise)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • The Mansion House scorecard is in — and it's a real-estate story. One year after 17 UK pension providers agreed to channel at least 10% of DC assets into global private markets by 2030 (half in the UK), the first official update shows schemes hold £11.1bn in UK unlisted assets — 6.6% of DC growth-phase assets (£22.1bn) in global private markets, 3.3% in the UK. The split: £8bn real estate, £1.3bn infrastructure, £900mn private credit, just £500mn domestic private equity — with a further £3.3bn signed but not yet deployed. UK Private Capital chief executive Michael Moore: "At the current rate of progress, the Compact target will be missed by a decade or more." Meanwhile Marsh's DC business committed £500mn to a custom long-term asset fund. (FT, Oct 20; Pensions UK/ABI data)

THE NUMBER

71% — Pantheon's oversubscription margin on yesterday's $6B secondaries fundraise, in a year when US buyout fundraising still sits ~27% below the 2023 peak (PitchBook via WSJ, Oct 13). Money isn't leaving private markets — it's rotating into the exit queue.

MEME CLEANSER

Private equity spent 2026 telling LPs the IPO window was closed, the M&A window was closed, and exits were "deferred." Pantheon just raised $6 billion to buy those deferred exits — 71% oversubscribed. The exit window isn't closed. It's just charging a toll.

Edition #020

Blackstone's energy transition bet just hit $8.7B

Wednesday, October 21, 2026

THE LEAD

Blackstone just closed the biggest energy-transition private fund of the year. Blackstone Energy Transition Partners V reached a $8.7 billion final close on October 19 — significantly oversubscribed, at its hard cap, and more than 50% larger than its predecessor. The strategy, which Blackstone says has delivered a 34% net IRR since 1997 (as of June 30, 2026), wasted no time deploying: its investment period began in June 2026 and it has already funded three platform companies (Kindle Energy, Fisterra, Viridon) and completed three acquisitions (Flow Control Holdings, Dresser Utility Solutions, DarkVision).

The raise lands alongside more than $11 billion in new equity commitments across the strategy since January 2025. Read it two ways: (1) LPs still write mega-checks for managers with a track record and a theme, and (2) the energy transition is quietly becoming one of the most fundable stories in private markets — right as capital discipline tightens everywhere else. "The momentum behind this strategy reflects both the scale of the opportunity and the confidence our investors have in our approach," said David Foley, Global Head of BETP, in the announcement.

Sources: Blackstone press release (Oct 19); AltAssets (Oct 20); ESG News (Oct 20)

FUND CLOSES

  • Ara Partners — closed its inaugural Ara Energy Fund I at its $2.5B hard cap ($1.8B fund commitments plus >$700M co-investment), ten months after launch. LPs span pensions, insurers, sovereign wealth funds, endowments, and foundations across North America and Europe; the fund launched in partnership with HF Capital and its seed portfolio already holds ~3.0 GW of thermal generation across NYISO/ISO-NE/PJM, ~400M gallons/year of US ethanol capacity, and the JET retail fuel network (~970 stations in Germany/Austria). Placement agent: Threadmark; counsel: Davis Polk. (PRNewswire, Oct 20)
  • Monomoy Capital Partners — closed its oversubscribed inaugural Monomoy Family Fund I at $589M against a $500M external hard cap, three months after launch, with the GP and affiliates committing $89M (~15%). The fund was fully allocated within two weeks and targets family-owned businesses, corporate carve-outs, and take-privates at enterprise values of $200M or less in industrial, consumer, and services. All LPs are existing Monomoy relationships. (VCA Online; AltAssets; Law360, Oct 20)

EMK Capital closed a €1.25B single-asset continuation fund for European technical installation group VDK Groep — at hard cap, with LGT Capital Partners co-leading — to fund further acquisitions. The deal fits a pattern PitchBook flagged this quarter: IT's share of global GP-led secondary exit value hit ~24.6% in 2025 versus 10.6% in 2023, more than doubling. (AltAssets, Oct 19; PitchBook)

The release cites Jefferies' 2025 Global Secondary Market Review: global secondary volume grew 53% in 2025 to ~$233B, split roughly evenly between LP-led and GP-led deals — with GP-led share rising from under 20% a decade ago to about half. (GlobeNewswire, Oct 15)
  • Permira Credit priced Menlo CLO VI at $401.9M — its sixth Menlo-platform US CLO in under two years and 21st CLO globally (fifth this year), lifting Permira Credit's global liquid credit platform to $9.5B in AUM. (Alternative Credit Investor, Oct 16)

Under seven weeks to weigh in. (Covered in #019; law-firm memos from Greenberg Traurig, Simpson Thacher, Debevoise, early-mid October)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

THE NUMBER

$222B vs. $405B — US private-equity fundraising through September 30 this year versus the 2023 peak: down ~27%, and headed for a fourth consecutive down year. Mega-funds still close at hard caps (see THE LEAD). The barbell market is the market now.

MEME CLEANSER

Private credit spent October pricing AI-infrastructure leverage at 1,200 bps. Blackstone spent the same month raising $8.7 billion for the energy transition — oversubscribed, at hard cap, in ten months. The lesson for allocators is almost insulting in its simplicity: money doesn't flow to megatrends; it flows to managers who filed the paperwork first.

Edition #019

Private credit's AI bet just hit 1,200 bps

Tuesday, October 20, 2026

THE LEAD

The AI-infrastructure credit trade just had its first real stress test — and it left a mark. CoreWeave's bonds slid into distressed territory last week, with the cost of insuring its debt against default climbing to about 1,200 basis points and yields on some of its bonds hitting 15% to 16%. Per LSEG, the neocloud carries roughly $50 billion of debt — much of it raised through private credit facilities that read like a who's-who of the asset class: a $2.3B GPU-backed facility in 2023 (Magnetar, Blackstone Tactical Opportunities), $7.5B in 2024 (Blackstone, Magnetar, Carlyle, BlackRock), and an $8.5B facility in March 2026 anchored by Blackstone Credit & Insurance.

The uncomfortable math: Carlyle said in an October paper that private credit could be called on to finance roughly $1 trillion of the AI-compute buildout — more than half the industry's current AUM — while warning that rapid lending into the sector could create concentration risks "similar to those that emerged in software lending." The good news for credit bulls: the stress is being priced borrower-by-borrower, not as sector contagion. The bad news: with billions of debt maturing across 2026–2027, refinancing happens at the new price.

CFRA's Sam Stovall summed up the read-across: "Coreweave is a proxy for other highly leveraged companies." Private credit's golden trade of 2026 now has a live quote on what the downside looks like.

Sources: Reuters (Oct 15, Oct 16); CNBC (Oct 14)

FUND CLOSES

  • Apogem Capital — closed Apogem Private Equity Fund XI at its $580m hard cap, plus $17m from employees for $597m total. The lower-middle-market fund drew new public and private pension plans, family offices, and RIAs alongside returning LPs, with a sizeable anchor from parent New York Life. (PE Professional)
  • Corre Partners — held a $220m first close for its Horizon III opportunistic credit fund against a $500m target ($650m hard cap), lifting the manager above $1.3B in AUM. Predecessor Horizon II sits at 24.7% net IRR / 2.4x MOIC. (Morningstar/Business Wire, Oct 8)
  • Janus Henderson — second close of its Shariah-compliant MENA Private Credit Fund IV at $191m toward a $300m target, backed by Saudi and UAE institutions and family offices. (ent.news/Africa Capital Digest, Oct 11)

DEALS

  • Merger Monday, Australia edition. EQT and waste-management company Cleanaway agreed to a deal valuing the latter's enterprise at A$9.3B ($6.47B); Pacific Equity Partners-owned SG Fleet won the battle to take FleetPartners private at A$1.13B equity ($789m); and the go-shop period ended on Brookfield's A$4.1B ($2.86B) bid for Reliance Worldwide, leaving it the only offer standing. Separately, TAL Daiichi Life Australia offered to buy the 79.5% of annuity provider Challenger it doesn't already own at an A$8.1B ($5.65B) equity valuation — about a 20% premium. (Reuters, Oct 20)
  • Citi's tokenization thesis. Citi Research circulated a note positioning Aave as prospective core infrastructure for tokenized finance, calling "collateral mobility" one of tokenization's most important use cases — borrowing stablecoins against tokenized Treasuries and money market funds without selling. Aave publicized the assessment on Oct 19; its Horizon market already lets qualified institutions pledge tokenized RWAs (Superstate, Centrifuge collateral) and borrow against them. No investment or commercial partnership disclosed. (CoinOTAG, TokenPost, KuCoin, Oct 20)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • Form PF wants a private credit line of its own. The SEC and CFTC's proposed streamlined Form PF amendments include a request for comment on adding a dedicated private credit reporting section — currently the Form PF glossary doesn't even define "private credit." The proposal also offers a minimum 12-month transition period, mindful that the 2024 amendments' compliance date was October 1, 2026. (Chapman, JD Supra, October)
  • Retailization comment clock. The SEC's Sept. 30 retail-access proposals (performance fees in regulated funds, interval fund modernization, new accredited-investor pathways) hit the Federal Register Oct. 5–6 — comments are due December 4 (interval fund + accredited investor notices) and December 7 (performance fee proposal). Less than seven weeks to weigh in. (Greenberg Traurig, October)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • New York keeps buying real estate. The ~$310B New York State Common Retirement Fund allocated roughly $565m to US real estate in August, per October comptroller disclosures: $300m to Artemis Real Estate Partners Fund V (closed Aug. 10), $115.9m into a 674-bed Austin student housing JV via its BlackRock-advised separate account (Aug. 13), $143m for a 520,000-sq-ft Inland Empire industrial property via its JPM-advised separate account (Aug. 18), and up to $6.3m to Hyperion I — a new manager relationship — via GCM Grosvenor (Aug. 21). (Connect Money, Oct 19)

THE NUMBER

59% vs. 0.2% — the share of the tokenized-RWA market's value held in permissioned (whitelisted) products versus their share of spot trading volume, per Pantera Capital's September 2026 report. The sector's two worlds in one line: BlackRock's BUIDL turns over about 0.12x per month while stablecoins turn over 21x — a 180-fold velocity gap. The issuance machine works; the secondary market barely does.

MEME CLEANSER

Private credit spent two years building a $1 trillion AI-infrastructure thesis on the back of GPU collateral. Last week, the market priced that thesis in real time: 9.625% coupon, 1,200-bps CDS. The new slogan writes itself — compute is the new oil, except the refinery wants 16%.

The $12.4bn AI-anxiety buyout day

Monday, October 19, 2026

THE LEAD

Two sponsors bought the AI dip — $12.4bn worth of it. Thoma Bravo-owned Proofpoint agreed to acquire Varonis Systems in an all-cash deal valued at $6.3bn (equity value $5.71bn), announced October 15. Varonis shareholders get $49.75 a share — a 52% premium to the June 22 pre-rumor close, though only about 5% above the pre-announcement price after months of takeover chatter. The multiple: 8.4x LTM revenue, 53x LTM free cash flow. Expected close in the first half of 2027.

The same day, CCC Intelligent Solutions agreed to a $6.1bn take-private with a consortium led by GTCR and including Elliott Investment Management. Shareholders get $7.75 a share — a 44% premium to the unaffected July 9 close and 59% above the 30-day average — with no financing condition, expected close Q1 2027. Advent, which bought CCC in 2017, SPAC'd it in 2021, and exited in 2025, is not involved.

Both targets are vertical software, and both were bought off discounts created by the same fear: that AI disrupts their economics. GTCR's own framing of CCC was the quote of the week — a business "made better by AI, not worse by AI." The read-through for alts allocators: the sponsors with the most deal-making experience in software are treating the AI selloff as a bid list, not a warning.

Sources: Proofpoint press release / Reuters (Oct 15); Insurance Journal; TipRanks 8-K summary (Oct 16)

FUND CLOSES

  • Aldaron — the new US PE firm launched by former Palantir and Carlyle executives has reportedly closed its debut fund on about $885m. (AltAssets, Oct 16)
  • OrbiMed raised $3bn across its latest US and European life sciences fund and its sixth Asian healthcare vehicle — with the Asia fund surpassing $1bn for the first time. (AltAssets, Oct 16)
  • L&G and NTR secured €330m at the first close of their fourth European energy-transition infrastructure fund, with three initial acquisitions covering around 1.5GW of clean power generation and grid-support assets. (AltAssets, Oct 15)

DEALS

  • Fission — the tokenization infrastructure firm raised $10m in a round led by Pantera Capital, with Circle Ventures participating, to build secondary-market liquidity for qualifying tokenized private-fund interests that normally face fixed redemption windows. Announced Oct 15. (CryptoCoinShow, Oct 17)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • Issuers push back on the SEC's tokenization sandbox. CoreWeave and Broadcom are challenging tokenized versions of their shares, testing the SEC's five-year Innovation Exemption for Tokenized Securities Venues — including the issuer opt-out provision and the unresolved mechanics of shareholder lists, dividend and split handling, voting, and who controls the count of shares shown as outstanding. The exemption's symbol and volume caps also raise questions about how large a token venue can get before it hits normal stock-market rules on quotes, trade-throughs, and clearing. The first real stress test of onchain equity plumbing, and it's the issuers — not the regulators — applying it. (The Brief, Oct 16)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • Qatar keeps buying managers. Activate Capital — the US energy and infrastructure technology investor with more than $1bn across three funds — becomes the 13th manager in QIA's $3B venture programme and will open a Doha office. QIA launched the Fund of Funds in February 2024 at $1bn, added $2bn at Web Summit Qatar this February, and has since widened the remit to later-stage investments. (FwdStart, Oct 16)
  • Norway may reverse its defense ban. The world's largest sovereign wealth fund is reportedly weighing a reversal of its long-standing ethical rules preventing investment in major defense and aerospace corporations — a move that would send a signal across the sovereign-investor universe. (The Geo Chronicle, Oct 16)
  • NIIF goes long India's data centres. India's National Investment and Infrastructure Fund is preparing to invest $700–800m in data centres and related businesses, putting digital infrastructure at the centre of its second infrastructure investment cycle. (Global SWF, Oct 15)

THE NUMBER

39% — the decline in global infrastructure fundraising over the first nine months of 2026 versus last year, despite a sharp third-quarter rebound. The catch: three megafunds captured 65% of all Q3 capital raised. The rebound is real; the concentration is the story. (AltAssets, Oct 16)

MEME CLEANSER

GTCR paid a 44% premium for CCC while its own principal explained the thesis to Reuters: "a business that we think is made better by AI, not worse by AI." The AI fear trade, in both directions, in a single sentence.

The Week in Alts: Partners Group's shareholders choose the exit

Sunday, October 18, 2026

THE LEAD

Partners Group's shareholders voted to pull the plug. Investors in the Swiss manager's London-listed Partners Group Private Equity Limited backed an orderly liquidation with 99.9% of votes cast at the October 7 extraordinary general meeting — after 74.1% of eligible shareholders had already opted for a liquidity alternative, shelving a proposed restructuring. The trust trades at a roughly 40% discount to NAV, and proceeds from the portfolio sell-off are due to investors in half-yearly tranches starting March 31, 2027. The wind-down doesn't touch Partners Group Holding AG — but the same week, Bloomberg reported the firm cut or redeployed dozens of positions across its 200-person buyout team, and management proposed splitting its €6.6bn Global Value SICAV into two sub-portfolios: one for compounding, one to channel disposal proceeds back to investors. The official line is that the evergreen franchise is being reshaped. The shareholder vote says the market read it differently: this is October's real theme — the liquidity pressure that the ASIC stop orders, the evergreen redemption caps, and the Jefferies downgrade all pointed at, finally getting a 99.9% verdict.

Sources: ad-hoc-news.de (Oct 18); Reuters (Oct 6); Scottish Financial News (Oct 5)

FUND CLOSES

  • A quiet weekend on new closes — yesterday's Week in Alts covered the week's five: Sprints, Hg, StepStone, Consonance, and Aphias. The fresh money story right now is in secondaries infrastructure, not new raises. (See Edition #016)

DEALS

  • Clear Channel Outdoor → Mubadala: closed. The $6.2bn take-private completed Oct 14 after CFIUS clearance and stockholder approval; each share converted to $2.43 cash, the stock delists from the NYSE, and Wade Davis (ex-TelevisaUnivision) takes over as CEO from the departing Scott Wells. The deal closed with $1.3bn in cash to equityholders, $3.3bn of equity from Mubadala Capital-managed funds, and a $100m incremental term loan. (PR Newswire/Morningstar; Defense World, Oct 14–16)
  • Galderma → ZO Skin Health — the Swiss dermatology group agreed to acquire the Irvine, California skincare brand for $1.5bn enterprise value, all cash, from Blackstone Tactical Opportunities and the Obagi family. ZO generated $330m of sales in the twelve months to June 2026 — about a 4.5x revenue multiple. Expected close Q4 2026 or early 2027. (Premium Beauty News; AltAssets/AlphaMaven, Oct 14)
  • Benefit Street Partners × Encina — the Franklin Templeton-owned manager expanded its asset-backed finance platform with a seed portfolio of about $480m in originated commitments from Encina Lender Finance, with 14 Encina professionals joining. ABF remains the fastest-growing corner of private credit. (AlphaMaven deal roundup, Oct 15)
  • Ares → Statera Energy — structured capital investment in a 1.9GW portfolio of UK flexible-energy projects (battery storage, wind, solar) developed by Statera Energy; terms undisclosed. Institutional money keeps underwriting the UK power-buildout. (AlphaMaven deal roundup, Oct 15)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • CFTC blesses tokenized assets in customer funds. The Commission's updated FAQs tell CFTC-regulated firms that customer funds may be invested in tokenized versions of already-permitted assets — provided the token grants holders legal and economic rights "the same or functionally equivalent" to the traditional form. The guidance also extends a non-objection to blockchain-based recordkeeping. One carve-out: payment stablecoins still don't qualify. The clearest federal green light yet for onchain wrappers inside regulated vehicles. (CoinDesk, Sept 24)
  • Ondo Finance joins DTCC Fund/SERV — DTCC admitted Oasis Pro Markets, Ondo's US broker-dealer subsidiary, as the first tokenization member of Fund/SERV, the network behind roughly 85% of US mutual-fund transactions. Fund-order routing and recordkeeping on DTCC rails with a blockchain layer on top: the plumbing for tokenized fund distribution just got conventional. (DTCC press release; Ondo, Sept 16)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • MainePERS keeps buying infrastructure anyway. The pension committed $280m to a Meridiam infrastructure strategy, lifting its 2026 infrastructure commitments to $580m — even though its broader real-assets portfolio already exceeds its target allocation. When allocators overpay target and still write checks, the strategy is the priority, not the policy. (AltAssets via AlphaMaven, Oct 15)
  • Michigan writes $765m, then slows down. The $130.5bn State of Michigan Retirement System committed $765m across seven alternatives funds while rebalancing — and signaled slower future commitment pacing. Fewer relationships, larger checks, then a pause: Q2's pattern holding into Q4. (AlternativesWatch via AlphaMaven, Oct 15)
  • Illinois Police Officers' Pension approved its first allocation under a $450m non-core real estate search, with a second manager under consideration. (AlternativesWatch via AlphaMaven, Oct 16)
  • Denmark's P+ invested DKK750m in PSG Europe III, which has allocated over €4.4bn (DKK33bn) to European tech and software — with AI names like Mistral among the first investments. European pension capital keeps backing European AI. (European Pensions, Oct 9)

THE NUMBER

99.9% — the share of votes backing the orderly liquidation of Partners Group's London-listed private-equity trust. When shareholders vote almost unanimously for the exit on a fund trading 40% below NAV, it's not a verdict on one vehicle. It's the evergreen era's report card.

MEME CLEANSER

Partners Group spent the week splitting its €6.6bn flagship into "Compounding" and "Distributing" portfolios. Investors looked at the two options and chose a third: "Liquidating."

The Week in Alts: Penske caps a $37.5bn take-private week

Saturday, October 17, 2026

THE LEAD

Penske Automotive agreed to be taken private by its largest shareholders — Penske Corp and Japan's Mitsui — in a deal valuing the auto retailer at about $16.3bn including debt. Shareholders get $212 a share in cash, an 8.6% premium to the July 21 close, the day before the initial proposal went public. GTCR and Elliott take CCC private in a $6.1bn deal

THE LEAD

CCC Intelligent Solutions agreed to be acquired by a GTCR-led consortium including Elliott Investment Management in an all-cash deal with an enterprise value of approximately $6.1bn. Stockholders get $7.75 a share in cash — a premium of about 44% to CCC's unaffected close on July 9, 2026, the last trading day before media speculation about a sale, and about 59% to the 30-day average price as of that date. The take-private pipeline keeps rewarding patience: GTCR closed its own $14.2bn flagship just last week, and now it's deploying into vertical software with a 40-year data moat.
