Securitize stops just tokenizing credit and buys the lender

Tuesday, November 10, 2026

THE LEAD

The tokenizer bought the warehouse. Securitize Corp. (NYSE: SECZ) said on November 9 it has signed a definitive agreement to acquire River Credit Labs Inc. — River One — a New York warehouse-financing platform founded in 2025 that connects stablecoin capital with traditional lending markets. Terms were not disclosed; Securitize expects the deal to close in the fourth quarter of 2026, and announced it alongside its third-quarter results and a $50 million share buyback.

River One funds lending facilities with stablecoin capital on the back end while borrowers draw and repay in ordinary dollars, financing portfolios of small-business loans, receivables and other income-generating assets. It was incubated by Framework Ventures and is backed by Framework and Maple Finance. This is Securitize's first acquisition as a public company — it began trading as SECZ on July 2, 2026 after closing its business combination with Cantor Equity Partners II.

The strategic read is simple: the firm that built the plumbing to issue tokenized funds and, since October 8, tokenized U.S. equities is now buying the plumbing that finances the assets behind them. Tokenization's front end met private credit's funding stack — and paid for the meeting.

Sources: Stablecoin Insider (Nov 9); Blockchain Reporter (Nov 9); CryptoNews (Nov 9)

Announced November 9, the dedicated semiconductor value-chain platform pairs close to €700 million of fund commitments with co-investments. Middle East and North Africa investors supplied 41% of the capital, French investors 24%, and new investors 58% of commitments. Four investments are already done: VSORA, centrotherm, Synergie CAD and Ion Beam Services. (Unite.AI, Nov 9; MLQ, Nov 9; Reuters via EIN Presswire, Nov 9)

$39.78 billion — the distributed value of tokenized real-world assets on November 9, per RWA.xyz, across 5.69 million holders. Tokenized private credit was $8.22 billion of that on November 7, the second-largest category — and its largest single product, Maple Finance's syrupUSDC, held about $909 million. The tokenized layer is still a rounding error next to the off-chain market it is financing into. That gap is the business plan behind today's lead.

Securitize spent years selling picks and shovels to tokenized credit. On Monday it bought a mine with a warehouse attached. Somewhere, a TradFi banker is explaining to a stablecoin holder what a borrowing base is — and both of them are enjoying it. Happy Tuesday.

Secondaries' price tape goes public: Texas Teachers got 94¢, BP got 84¢

Monday, November 9, 2026

THE LEAD

94.3% vs. 83.7%. Those are the prices two of the world's biggest LPs actually got for their fund stakes — and for the first time, the names and the numbers are both public. Texas Teachers transferred part of its EQT VII stake to Coller vehicles; Coller's evergreen paid $117.4 million for $124.5 million of value on March 31, or 94.3% of NAV. The BP Pension Fund sold its Livingbridge 5 and 6 stakes to Future Standard's evergreen, FS MVP, inside a 54-interest packet that cleared at 83.7% of NAV — with the two Livingbridge lines themselves at 65.5% and 70.2%.

The spread is the story. A study of 2,983 buyout fund trades since 2015, published October 5 by the Institute for Private Capital, puts the long-run average at 91.8% of NAV — and found no reliable sign that evergreen buyers have pushed prices up. The market's average price and its headline discounts are both real; which one you get depends on what you're selling. Meanwhile the buyers keep raising: StepStone's seventh venture secondaries fund counted $1.77 billion in an SEC filing Friday, from about 310 investors, and Blackstone's Jon Gray put Strategic Partners X at "over $16 billion raised to date" toward a target of at least $22 billion.

Secondaries spent a decade as the market where prices were rumored and discounts were assumed. This week, the tape exists — and it says quality clears near par while the rest still pays a real discount.

Sources: The Secondary Brief (Nov 9); Institute for Private Capital, Brown & Volckmann (Oct 5); Coller and FS MVP annual reports via SEC

FUND CLOSES

Fund VI closed at $3.3 billion in June 2024. (The Secondary Brief, Nov 9; SEC filing)

  • Blackstone Strategic Partners X — over $16 billion raised to date. Jon Gray's figure, given on the firm's earnings call Thursday, against a target of at least $22 billion. (The Secondary Brief, Nov 9; Motley Fool transcript)

DEALS

  • Nisus Finance launches its first tokenised real estate offering — $50 million. NIFCOT1, backed by the DIFC-regulated Nisus High Yield Growth Fund, comprises 100,000 digital ownership tokens and is the first asset listed on the Toyow marketplace. Nisus has set out a broader $500 million multi-tranche tokenisation roadmap; no timing or assets disclosed for later tranches. (RP Realty Plus, Nov 9)

It just got pickier.

MEME CLEANSER

Texas Teachers sold at 94 cents on the dollar. BP sold at 84. Same market, same month, same asset class — a ten-point gap between two of the most sophisticated sellers on earth. Private markets this week: the discount is dead, long live the discount. Happy Monday.

The Week in Alts: Tokenization broke out of the Treasury box

Sunday, November 8, 2026

THE LEAD

$2.38 billion. That is the size of SAFO, the tokenized cash fund Spiko launched with Amundi in March — now the world's largest tokenized fund, overtaking BlackRock's BUIDL ($2.17 billion) on October 19 and Circle's USYC ($2.37 billion) on October 27. Assets have grown about 30% a month for three months; the investor base, now more than 10,000 legal entities and individuals, is growing about 18% a month. More than two-thirds of SAFO ($1.65 billion) sits on Stellar.

It was not a one-fund week. Amundi launched a tokenized share class of its Core STOXX Europe 600 equity index fund on public Ethereum, with CACEIS — moving tokenization from cash-like products into broad equities. Schroders and Centrifuge put a tokenized share class of Schroders' emerging-market bond strategy on Solana on November 4, the manager's first deployment on the network. And Caliber went live with three more tokenized real estate offerings, up to $85 million in equity, taking its tokenized total past $110 million.

The pattern is the story: tokenization's center of gravity has shifted from tokenized Treasuries to equities, emerging-market debt, and real estate — in a single week. The plumbing is now the product. rwa.xyz counted $39.05 billion of transferable tokenized real-world assets on November 2, with Treasuries still 36.4% of the total and credit at 21.3%. The next test is whether the new wrappers draw real secondary trading, or just new packaging for the same buy-and-hold.

Sources: Chainwire via TradingView (Nov 5); KuCoin (Nov 8); MEXC blog (Nov 5); Quantisnow/GlobeNewswire (Nov 4); Sonic Wallet/rwa.xyz (Nov 2)

  • Intera Partners raises Finland's largest buyout fund — €450 million hard-cap close. Intera Fund V is the Finnish manager's fifth buyout fund. (AltAssets, Nov 5)

DEALS

  • Global Payments reports Q3 — and says nothing about the $40 billion buyout. The payments processor posted adjusted EPS of $3.58, up 10%, on $3.18 billion of adjusted net revenue, raised its Worldpay synergy target to $650 million in annual run-rate savings, and reaffirmed full-year adjusted EPS guidance of $13.60–$13.80. CEO Cameron Bready declined to comment on the reported CD&R/GTCR take-private approach. (Global Payments press release, Nov 5; Motley Fool earnings call transcript, Nov 5)

The gap between "onchain" and "tradable" is still the market's honest measure.

MEME CLEANSER

A tokenized money-market fund overtook BlackRock, a tokenized index fund went live on Ethereum, a tokenized EM bond fund launched on Solana, and a private-credit ETF liquidated in the same week. Private markets this week: everything is being tokenized except the exit — and the one wrapper that actually needed retail demand couldn't find enough of it. Happy Sunday.

The Week in Alts: CVC's $10B secondaries close caps the week liquidity won

Saturday, November 7, 2026

THE LEAD

$10 billion. That is the size of CVC Capital Partners' Secondary Opportunities Fund VI, closed this week — CVC's sixth and largest secondaries fund, more than 70% bigger than the $5.8 billion it raised in 2023 and nearly four times the $2.7 billion from 2019. More than 200 investors committed, about half of them new to CVC's secondaries platform. The firm now oversees about €20 billion ($23.2 billion) across its private equity and credit secondary strategies.

It caps a week that belonged to secondaries. Pantheon closed its $6 billion Global Secondaries Fund VIII, 71% above target. Revolut confirmed a $115 billion secondary valuation, up 53% in a year, with no new capital raised. StepStone closed a $1.5 billion infrastructure secondaries fund at its hard cap and activated a $1.8 billion venture secondaries fund. And Virginia's $135.2 billion pension said it will push private equity co-investments past $750 million a year and lean harder into continuation vehicles. The exit door is still slow; the side door is now a highway.

The catch, and it matters: the discount that made secondaries attractive is compressing. LP portfolio stakes traded around 87% of NAV in the first half of 2026, per Jefferies, and top single-asset continuation vehicles are clearing at par or above — some roughly 14% above NAV, per Evercore. Jefferies puts first-half global secondary volume at a record $118 billion, up 15% year over year, with dedicated dry powder near $290 billion heading into the second half. When $290 billion of buyer capital chases the same trophy assets, the seller wins the negotiation.

Revolut cashes out at $115 billion, and secondaries just became the exit

Friday, November 6, 2026

THE LEAD

$115 billion. That is the valuation Revolut confirmed for its latest secondary share sale on November 4 — up 53% from the $75 billion set in a share sale late last year. No new capital was raised. This is the second major Revolut secondary in roughly twelve months, and it is the cleanest illustration yet of the market's new shape: when IPOs stall and sponsor-to-sponsor exits slow, secondaries are where the liquidity lives. Jefferies puts global secondary transaction volume at a record $118 billion in the first half of 2026 alone, up 15% year over year — and GP-led deals, mostly continuation vehicles, now hold the majority share for the first time since 2021. The discount that used to define the category is compressing with it: LP portfolio stakes traded around 87% of NAV in the first half, and top single-asset continuation vehicles are clearing at par or above.

THE LEAD

One day. The pattern is now unmistakable. Lazard paid $575 million plus up to $85 million in earnouts to make private capital advisory its third global business; a day later a regional bank bought the same capability wholesale instead of building it. Placement agents and secondary advisers sit on the two things every sponsor needs most right now — distribution to LPs and a route to liquidity — and banks would rather own that toll booth than rent it. Expect more: the firms that intermediate the $124 billion first-half secondary market are becoming acquisition targets faster than they are becoming competitors.
