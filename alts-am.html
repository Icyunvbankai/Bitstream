The Week in Alts: The filing tape never sleeps

Sunday, November 22, 2026

THE LEAD

853 new Form D notices in five days — the private-markets pipeline filed, even as public stocks slid. SEC EDGAR's daily indexes for Monday, November 16 through Friday, November 20 list 853 new Form D notices (773 claiming Rule 506(b), 74 claiming Rule 506(c)) and 479 Form D/A amendments, Crowdfund Insider's weekly filings digest reported Saturday.

Read the week's biggest number carefully: the largest figure was an annual amendment — the AIP Fund F program's $14.99 billion program-wide total under Rule 506(c), up from $0 in its November 2025 notices. That is a program total on an amendment, not $15 billion of new money raised in a week. The same caution applies down the list: Ares Industrial Real Estate Fund LP's amendment reports $4.75 billion sold, and Brookfield's Catalytic Transition Fund reports $2.65 billion sold under 506(c).

The genuinely new-money signal sits elsewhere: 17 new Form C crowdfunding filings, four new Form 1-A filings, and a tokenization name in the Reg A queue — COSIMO Digital Holdings filed a $75 million Regulation A offering. The week's story is not a mega-close. It is volume: the exempt-offering machine kept filing at full speed into a soft public tape.

Sources: Crowdfund Insider, Weekly Private Securities Filings Digest, Nov 21 (SEC EDGAR daily form indexes, Nov 16–20)

FUND CLOSES

  • Plural Fund III — €1 billion raised, the firm's largest to date. Announced Monday, the London, Tallinn and Paris early-stage firm's third fund is almost four times its €260 million inaugural fund (2022) and double its €500 million second fund (2024). Two-thirds is earmarked for early-stage rounds and one-third for follow-ons; the portfolio it backs — Helsing, Proxima Fusion, OLIX, The Exploration Company — raised more than $3 billion of follow-on capital in 2026. (Plural via Business Wire, Nov 16; Crunchbase News, Nov 16)
  • The 2027 flagship queue is the real fundraising story. Dakota's tally of buyout fundraises to watch heading into 2027 counts at least $180 billion in flagship targets in market or pre-marketing, after Advent International closed GPE XI at $27 billion on October 27 — above its $26 billion target and the largest buyout close of 2026 so far. CVC's Europe/Americas Fund X (about €26 billion target) opens formal fundraising in January 2027; CD&R Fund XIII has about $20 billion raised toward a $26 billion target, with a final close targeted for Q1 2027. Targets and in-market amounts are as reported, not closes. (Dakota Marketplace, Nov 16)

DEALS

  • Multiples Equity leads about $100 million into GIVA. Reported Saturday, Multiples Alternate Asset Management led an approximately $100 million (about ₹900 crore) round in jewellery brand GIVA, operated by Indiejewels Fashion Private Limited. The round combined fresh issuance with purchases of shares from certain existing shareholders — part primary, part secondary — with Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas advising Multiples and Avendus Capital advising GIVA. (Economic Times Legal via AlphaMaven, Nov 21)
  • A quiet week for control deals. After last week's take-private tape, the week's disclosed private-markets deal flow skewed to growth rounds and filing activity rather than sponsor control transactions — the action was in the EDGAR queue, not the auction room. (Crowdfund Insider, Nov 21; AlphaMaven, Nov 21)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • Tokenization files under Reg A. COSIMO Digital Holdings' new $75 million Regulation A filing was the tokenization entry in the week's Reg A activity, which also included four qualification notices (Masterworks Vault 5 and Vault 6, Limitless X Holdings and PSFNetwork MasterSeries). A filing is an application to offer, not an approval or a raise. (Crowdfund Insider, Nov 21, from SEC EDGAR)
  • The retailization comment clock runs out in under two weeks. The SEC's September 30 package — performance-fee eligibility for accredited investors, interval fund modernization, rules-based multiple share classes — remains proposal-only, with comment periods closing December 4 and December 7, 2026. The five accredited-investor pathway notices (FINRA exam, CPA, CFA, CFP, Series 79/86/87) sit on the same December 4 clock. Current rules stay in effect unless final action is adopted; the income, net-worth and license tests have not moved. (SEC, Sept 30; Manhattan West summary; Raveum, Nov 5)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • MainePERS adds a new manager — up to $50 million to Godspeed Capital Fund IV. The $23.1 billion Maine Public Employees Retirement System approved the commitment at its November 12 meeting, subject to final due diligence, legal review and negotiations. Godspeed is a new relationship for MainePERS: a lower middle-market manager focused on defense, government services and social infrastructure. (Pensions & Investments via AlphaMaven, Nov 13)
  • New Jersey trims targets while it commits. The roughly $87 billion New Jersey Pension Fund's up to $400 million of commitments — $250 million to American Industrial Partners Capital Fund IX and $150 million to TPG Real Estate Partners V — come alongside target cuts: private equity's target falls to 11% from 13% and real estate's to 7% from 8% next year. Staff committed $2.3 billion across eight funds or fund families in fiscal 2026; the system remains underweight private markets. (Connect Money, Nov 9; Pensions & Investments via AlphaMaven)

THE NUMBER

$40.9 billion — distributed tokenized real-world assets on public chains on November 22, per rwa.xyz, down about 3.6% in 30 days and held by about 5.87 million wallets. Treasuries are the largest slice at about $14.1 billion, credit about $8.2 billion, tokenized stocks about $3.4 billion. Same discipline as always: represented value — the record-keeping layer investors cannot hold or transfer — was about $451.4 billion the same day. Quote the distributed number, or quote both, never mix them. (rwa.xyz via Blockchain Reporter, checked Nov 22)

MEME CLEANSER

The biggest private-markets number of the week was an amendment, the biggest fund story is a queue that opens in January, the tokenization headline is a $75 million application, and Maine bought defense funds while New Jersey cut its targets and committed anyway. Private markets this week: the filing cabinet did the fundraising, the calendar did the allocating, and the actual new money was a jewellery round in Mumbai. Happy Sunday.

The Week in Alts: Take-privates did the exiting

Saturday, November 21, 2026

THE LEAD

$6 billion, all cash — the week's exit was a delisting. BILL Holdings agreed Thursday to be acquired by TPG, in partnership with Corpay, in an all-cash transaction valued at about $6 billion. Shareholders get $63.25 per share, a 31% premium to the 60-day volume-weighted average price ending November 16, the last full trading day before media reports of talks surfaced.

TPG takes a majority stake through TPG Capital, its U.S. and European private equity platform. Corpay invests $1.1 billion for roughly a one-third stake and holds an option to acquire the full company by 2030. The deal has no financing condition, was approved unanimously by BILL's board, and is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to shareholder and regulatory approval. On close, BILL delists from the New York Stock Exchange.

That was the week's pattern in one deal: the cleanest exits were take-privates and sponsor-to-pension handovers, the fastest fund closes were single-close affairs, and the liquidity story stayed in the tender window. The rest of the week, below.

Sources: BILL / TPG / Corpay statement via Business Wire, Nov 19; Reuters, Nov 19; Payments Dive, Nov 20; WSJ, Nov 19–20

Fund IV is about 29% larger than Fund III's nearly $620 million. (Blue Sea announcement, Nov 17–18; PE Professional Fundraisers Roundup, updated Nov 20)
  • Hamilton Lane Venture Fund II — first close above $370 million. Disclosed on the firm's November earnings call and reported Monday, the second dedicated venture fund follows an inaugural vehicle that closed at $615 million in early 2025. The Commission is also seeking comment on additional accredited-investor pathways, including a FINRA-developed examination and credentials such as CPA, CFA and CFP. TPG and Corpay take BILL private for $6 billion

Friday, November 20, 2026

THE LEAD

$6 billion, all cash — and another public fintech leaves the board. BILL Holdings will be acquired by TPG, in partnership with Corpay, in an all-cash transaction valued at about $6 billion, the companies announced Thursday. Shareholders get $63.25 per share, a 31% premium to the 60-day volume-weighted average price ending November 16, the last full trading day before media reports of talks surfaced.

TPG takes a majority stake through TPG Capital, its U.S. and European private equity platform. Corpay invests $1.1 billion for roughly a one-third stake. The deal has no financing condition, was approved unanimously by BILL's board, and is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to shareholder and regulatory approval. The structure is the tell. This is not a sponsor buying a payments platform alone — it is a sponsor plus a strategic payments operator splitting the asset on day one, with the operator holding the larger long-term option. Private equity's biggest take-privates in 2026 keep arriving with a corporate partner already inside the tent.

CVC exits Cary at about €3 billion to the Canadians and Nordic

Thursday, November 19, 2026

THE LEAD

About €3 billion — and a full exit for CVC. CVC Capital Partners is selling its entire stake in Cary Group, the Stockholm-based vehicle glass repair and replacement company, to a consortium of the Canada Pension Plan Investment Board, the Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan Board and Nordic Capital, in a transaction valued at approximately €3 billion, Bloomberg reported. The two Canadian pensions and Nordic will each hold roughly equal ownership, and Nordic Capital is reinvesting in the business alongside them — a sponsor-to-sponsor-plus-pensions handover, not a trade sale or an IPO.

CVC has co-owned Cary since 2022 and exits completely under the deal. The structure is the story: two of Canada's largest pension funds are not writing a minority cheque or co-investing at the margin — they are taking equal ownership stakes next to the sponsor, in a European automotive aftermarket services business. Direct, equal-share pension ownership of a €3 billion asset is the allocator model the industry keeps predicting; on Tuesday it was the deal.

For CVC, the exit lands the same week the firm set out its own succession: yesterday it named TPG president Todd Sisitsky and its own president Peter Rutland as future co-CEOs, with Rob Lucas stepping back from the top role by the first quarter of 2028 (see Deals). Selling the glass company, buying the succession plan — a tidy week in Amsterdam.

Sources: Bloomberg via AlphaMaven, Nov 17; AltAssets, Nov 18

The Leeds firm closed its sixth fund oversubscribed after a six-month fundraise run in-house without a placement agent, backed mainly by existing investors plus a global base of universities, foundations, family offices, pension funds and financial institutions. Endless VI targets UK mid-market carve-outs, family-owned businesses, complex buyouts and turnarounds, typically investing £20 million to £60 million for a controlling stake. It follows Endless V, which closed at £400 million in November 2020. (Endless LLP, Nov 17; BusinessCloud via AlphaMaven, Nov 18)
  • Argonaut Private Equity Fund VI — $606 million final close. The Tulsa firm's sixth fund beat its $550 million target, backed by insurance companies, pension funds, endowments, foundations, family offices and sovereign wealth funds, and continues its industrial and manufacturing focus across underserved U.S. markets.   • Ren Life Sciences Partners II — €375 million close, above hard cap. REGULATION & POLICY

  • IOSCO sizes tokenized real-world assets at $37 billion — and calls the market "transitional and fragmented." The global securities regulators' body released its 2026 monitoring report on tokenization this month: tokenized real-world assets on public blockchains climbed from about $16 billion on July 1, 2025 to $37 billion on June 30, 2026, using RWA.xyz data. Fixed income products and money market funds remain the dominant classes; tokenized equities emerged as a new product class, growing from $330 million to $1.7 billion in twelve months; primary issuance is the most developed part of the chain while secondary trading and distribution lag. IOSCO Board Chair Jean-Paul Servais flagged classification, disclosure and conduct questions, especially for products marketed across borders or built for permissionless trading. (IOSCO 2026 Monitoring Report, via Traders Union and Genfinity, Nov 18)
  • The SEC retailization comment clock is running: December 4 and December 7. The Commission's September 30 proposals to expand "responsible retailization" of private markets — performance fees for regulated funds and a broader "qualified client" definition, interval fund modernization with multiple share classes, and new exam- and credential-based paths to accredited investor status — are still proposals, not rules. Comments on the interval fund, share class and accredited investor pieces are due December 4, 2026, and on the performance fee proposal December 7, 2026. Anyone whose fund documents assume the outcome should be writing comments, not term sheets. (SEC proposals, via Akin / AFS Law alert, Oct 29; Private Equity Law Report, Nov 12)

THE NUMBER

$37 billion — the value of tokenized real-world assets on public blockchains at June 30, 2026, per IOSCO's 2026 monitoring report using RWA.xyz data, up from about $16 billion a year earlier. The composition matters more than the growth: fixed income and money market funds dominate, tokenized U.S. government debt alone rose from about $9 billion to nearly $15 billion between November 2025 and July 2026, and the genuinely new private-markets slice — tokenized equities — is $1.7 billion of the total.
