The Week in Alts: the mega-exit barbell

Saturday, October 10, 2026

THE LEAD

~$17bn in mega-deals in a single week — and the exit logjam officially has two speeds. SpaceX agreed to buy Grain Management's nationwide 800 MHz spectrum portfolio for $8bn (AltAssets, Oct 9), while the Weston family and Fairfax Financial closed the $8.9bn Boots buyout from Sycamore Partners (Reuters, Oct 7–8), and DCC Energy sold tech unit Nexora to One Equity Partners for $725m to tidy up ahead of the £5.75bn KKR–Energy Capital Partners takeover (Reuters, Oct 9). Pair that with PitchBook's Q3 data — exit value up 65% to $481.6bn on a <10% rise in deal count (AltAssets, Oct 8) — and the week's thesis is settled: exits are back, but only at the top. One year after Walgreens went private for $24bn, its carve-up is done; meanwhile the middle market waits for trickle-down. The barbell economy isn't a metaphor this week — it's the deal log.

Sources: AltAssets / PitchBook; Reuters; AltAssets (deal roundup, Oct 9)

FUND CLOSES

  • GTCR — more than $14.2bn for its fifteenth flagship buyout fund, above the $13.5bn target and ~24% ahead of its predecessor; the single biggest close of the week and the clearest proof that brand-name fundraising is not the industry's problem. (AltAssets, Oct 9)
  • Bridgepoint — exceeded its €28bn group fundraising goal three months early, with its latest European buyout fund approaching an €8.65bn hard cap; carried-interest upgrades on Energy Capital Partners performance. (AltAssets, Oct 8)
  • Spectrum Equity — $2.5bn record close for its eleventh flagship growth equity fund, 25% larger than its predecessor, in a rough tech-fundraising market. (AltAssets, Oct 9)
  • Meridiam — $4.5bn North American infrastructure vehicle extending ownership of 15 existing assets while handing earlier-fund LPs liquidity. The continuation-vehicle machine reaches infrastructure. (AltAssets, Oct 8)
  • Consonance Capital — $1.35bn at hard cap for its third healthcare fund in under four months, heavily oversubscribed. Sector focus still raises fast. (Business Wire, Oct 9)
  • Aphias Capital — just over $1.05bn for its inaugural fund (target $900m), demand exceeding the hard cap; healthcare services + essential services, lower middle market. (Business Wire, Oct 8)
  • Niobrara Capital — more than $1.1bn for its debut buyout fund, ~50% over target, less than 2.5 years after launching the firm. (AltAssets, Oct 5)
  • Corre Partners — $220m initial close (Oct 5) for Horizon III, a $500m-target opportunistic credit fund targeting stressed middle-market companies; predecessor Horizon II posts 24.7% net IRR / 2.4x net MOIC. (Business Wire via Morningstar, Oct 8)
  • Taurus Private Markets — $301m final close (target $225m) for its third fund-of-funds vehicle, backed by public pensions, Taft-Hartley plans and family offices. (GlobeNewswire, Oct 8)
  • SEVA Growth — $160m second growth equity fund, nearly doubling its debut, raised in under a month. (AltAssets, Oct 9)

DEALS

  • SpaceX → Grain Management — the $8bn spectrum deal (see THE LEAD) is one of the largest digital-infrastructure exits of the year; a nationwide 800 MHz portfolio moving to Musk's orbit. (AltAssets, Oct 9)
  • Boots — the $8.9bn Weston/Fairfax buyout (see THE LEAD) closes the Walgreens chapter; retail carve-outs are officially the exit path of choice. (Reuters, Oct 7–8)
  • DCC → One Equity Partners — $725m EV for Nexora, with DCC shareholders pocketing an extra 42p/share on the KKR consortium's £5.75bn offer if the sale clears; regulatory backstop July 31, 2027. (Reuters, Oct 9)
  • Tinicum + Blackstone / Senior plc — all conditions satisfied on the £1.28bn takeover of the UK engineering group; scheme went effective Oct 8. Sponsor capital still buys UK industrials. (Morningstar / Alliance News, Oct 7)
  • TPG agreed to buy a controlling stake in South Korea's largest car-rental company, Lotte Rental, for ~$920m in an all-equity deal — and is now vying with IMM Private Equity for control of CGBio ($746m) after TA Associates talks collapsed. TPG's Korea season. (kedglobal, Oct 9)
  • Amundi + ICG launched an evergreen private-equity secondaries product for the wealth market — LP-secondaries in the US and Europe, ICG underwriting, Amundi distribution. The biggest institutional badge yet for the wealth channel's secondaries appetite. (caproasia, Oct 8)
  • Warburg Pincus invested in Ares and LightBay Capital-backed Awayday through its $4bn-plus Capital Solutions Founders Fund, giving vacation-rental-manager investors liquidity while Ares and LightBay stay in control — the GP-solutions playbook, running in parallel to plain exits. (AltAssets, Oct 5)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • The SEC's Sept 30 retail-access package, for allocators. Gibson Dunn's Oct 8 alert lays out the "responsible retailization" proposals: performance-fee modernization (up to 20% of net gains, including unrealized, for registered funds and BDCs; qualified-client redefined to include any accredited investor), interval-fund modernization (monthly repurchases, multi-class shares without exemptive relief), and five notices exploring accredited-investor expansion to CPA/CFA/CFP holders and FINRA exam passers. 60-day comment clock is running. (Gibson Dunn, Oct 8)
  • ASIC follow-up — the interim stop order on Australian Secure Capital Fund's three private credit funds (Premium Capital, Select Income, High Yield) stands: no offer/issue/sale until disclosure defects are fixed, with permanent orders possible. The US widens the door; Australia polices the welcome mat. (Reuters, Oct 7)
  • SEC valuation rigor. Chief accountant Kurt Hohl and IM director Brian Daly issued a joint staff statement urging greater rigor in private-asset valuations — private credit front and center: loans don't trade, Level 3 inputs dominate, management owns the fair value call even when borrower data lags. (Connect Money, Oct 2)
  • Meyer Global Management — the SEC charged the private-fund adviser and CEO Owen E.H. Meyer with fraud and misuse of client assets tied to SpaceX and other pre-IPO securities: inflated statements, misappropriated proceeds, forced investor releases, and a forfeited ~$3m SpaceX position after a missed capital call. The retailization debate's dark twin. (SEC complaint via caproasia)

  • ABP committed €250m to the EQT-managed Scaleup Europe Fund as the Dutch pension giant prepares to deploy ~€1bn into European VC over three years. (AltAssets, Oct 5)
  • P+ awarded Schroders a €200m European private equity mandate; Danish peer PKA put DKK6.4bn (€860m) into European listed companies — Nordic pensions are deploying again. (IPE, Oct)
  • CFA Institute research (Sept 21) finds modest private-market allocations can improve modeled defined-contribution outcomes — but only with precise asset-class analysis and careful default-fund design, not blanket access. The fiduciary footnote to the whole retailization push. (Business Wire / CFA Institute)

THE NUMBER

83% — the share of record $515.8bn in US venture investment through the first nine months of 2026 that went to AI companies (AltAssets, Oct 9). Venture didn't recover; AI absorbed the recovery. Every non-AI GP's fundraising deck now opens with an apology slide.

Venture capitalists raised a record half-trillion dollars and spent 83% of it on one theme. Somewhere a SaaS founder just added "AI-powered" to a deck that is, in fact, a spreadsheet with good lighting.

Edition #008

The $481.6bn exit spree (with asterisks)

Friday, October 9, 2026

THE LEAD

$481.6bn of exits. And almost nobody left the building. Global private equity exit value jumped 65% in Q3 to $481.6bn, but the number of completed exits rose by less than 10%, per new PitchBook data reported by AltAssets (Oct 8). Read that twice: the exit logjam is breaking, but only at the top — a handful of mega-transactions carrying the entire quarter's narrative. Meanwhile fundraising keeps shrinking even as the recovery concentrates in larger deals. The industry's favorite headline ("exits are back") is true in dollar terms and almost entirely a function of the deals big enough to get done. The middle market, where most managers live and most LPs need liquidity, is still waiting its turn. Yesterday's edition was the credit cops showing up; today's is the reminder that the industry's recovery is a barbell — weight at the ends, air in the middle.

Sources: AltAssets / PitchBook

FUND CLOSES

  • GTCR — raised more than $14.2bn for its fifteenth flagship PE fund, above the $13.5bn target and nearly 24% ahead of its predecessor. Brand-name buyout fundraising is not a problem; mid-tier is. (AltAssets, Oct 9)
  • Spectrum Equity — $2.5bn record close for its eleventh flagship growth equity fund, 25% larger than its predecessor, despite a rough tech-fundraising environment. (AltAssets, Oct 9)
  • Bridgepoint — exceeded its €28bn fundraising goal three months early, with its latest European buyout fund approaching an €8.65bn hard cap; strong Energy Capital Partners performance driving carried-interest upgrades. (AltAssets, Oct 8)
  • Meridiam — closed a $4.5bn North American infrastructure vehicle to extend ownership of 15 existing assets while giving earlier-fund LPs liquidity. The continuation-vehicle machine reaches infrastructure. (AltAssets, Oct 8)
  • Sheridan Capital — ~$1.35bn across two healthcare-focused PE funds, nearly doubling its flagship size, plus a new healthcare-tech vehicle. (AltAssets, Oct 8)
  • LS Power — $6bn for its sixth flagship infrastructure fund, more than doubling the $2.7bn predecessor. (AltAssets, Oct 7)
  • Forbion — record €2.3bn across latest biotech venture and growth funds, topping the €2.09bn predecessor pair. (AltAssets, Oct 7)

DEALS

  • Boots — Canada's Weston family (Wittington Investments) agreed Oct 7 to buy Boots from Sycamore Partners and the Pessina family alongside Fairfax Financial in an $8.9bn deal including assumed debt. Fairfax takes ~50% equity; Wittington gets operational control. One year after Walgreens went private for $24bn, the carve-up of noncore assets is done — and retail carve-outs are officially back as the exit path of choice. (Reuters, Oct 7–8)
  • DCC Energy / One Equity Partners — DCC will sell its technology division Nexora to One Equity Partners for $725m EV, announced this morning — a pre-closing tidy-up ahead of the £5.75bn ($7.61bn) KKR–Energy Capital Partners buyout agreed in July. DCC shareholders get an extra 42p/share on top of the offer if the sale clears. (Reuters, Oct 9)
  • Amundi + ICG — launched an evergreen private-equity secondaries product for the wealth market (Amundi ICG Global Private Equity Secondaries), investing in US and European LP-secondaries with ICG's underwriting and Amundi's distribution. The wealth channel's appetite for secondaries just got its biggest institutional badge yet. (caproasia, Oct 8)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • The SEC's Sept 30 retail-access package, for allocators. Seyfarth Shaw's Oct 8 analysis cuts through the hype: for institutional LPs, "retirement capital is coming to the private funds" — meaning terms, liquidity mechanics, and allocation practices of funds you already invest in will evolve as managers chase the 401(k) dollar. Watch the interval-fund repurchase reform and the performance-fee-on-regulated-funds proposal; both rewire fund economics for everyone, not just retail. (Seyfarth, Oct 8)
  • Meyer Global Management — the SEC charged the private-fund adviser and CEO Owen E.H. Meyer with investment fraud and misuse of client assets tied to SpaceX and other pre-IPO securities (Sept 30): inflated account statements, misappropriated proceeds, forced investor releases — and a forfeited ~$3m SpaceX investment after a missed capital call. The retailization debate's dark twin: pre-IPO access products sell scarcity, and regulators are auditing the plumbing. (caproasia / SEC complaint)
  • ASIC follow-up — yesterday's Australian stop-order story on ASCF's three credit funds stands; the interim orders bar any offer/issue/sale of interests until disclosure defects are fixed, with permanent orders possible. (Reuters, Oct 7)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • CFA Institute research (Sept 21) finds modest private-market allocations can improve modeled defined-contribution outcomes — but only with precise asset-class analysis and careful default-fund design, not blanket access. Timely evidence for the fiduciary question at the heart of the SEC's retail push: opening the door is not the same as improving outcomes. (Business Wire / CFA Institute)
  • Octagon Credit Investors hired former Antares executive John Zilko to lead investor relations and business development at the $31bn alternative credit manager — institutional fundraising benches are being reinforced across credit. (AltAssets, Oct 8)

THE NUMBER

65% — the Q3 jump in global PE exit value to $481.6bn, against a <10% rise in exit count. Every basis point of the "recovery" lives in the right tail. Price your own fund's liquidity expectations accordingly.

MEME CLEANSER

Somewhere a mid-market GP just read that exits are up 65% and felt a little richer — until the second sentence arrived. The industry has discovered the opposite of diversification: putting all your returns in one very large, very rare basket.

The credit cops show up

Thursday, October 8, 2026

THE LEAD

Three funds. One stop order. Zero more fundraising. Australia's corporate regulator hit Australian Secure Capital Fund with an interim stop order on Wednesday, halting fundraising across its Premium Capital, Select Income and High Yield private credit funds over disclosure documents that ASIC says failed to adequately explain loan-portfolio composition, diversification, and exit costs — with some statements potentially misleading (Reuters, Oct 7). This is the other side of the retailization trade. The same week the SEC moved to hand ordinary investors more access to private funds (WSJ, Sept 30), a sister regulator showed what happens when the disclosure doesn't match the pitch. Private credit's growth story — registered-fund holdings up nearly 60% to $270bn since 2020 — was built on the assumption that the asset class was now a known quantity. ASIC just demonstrated it isn't: if you sell credit products to retail, regulators will read the PDS like a loan tape. Every manager currently marketing a semi-liquid credit vehicle should be re-reading their own documents this morning.

Sources: Reuters; WSJ

FUND CLOSES

  • Oaktree Capital (Brookfield) — $2bn final close for its Asset-Backed Financial Fund, on target, with a globally diversified LP base including US public pensions and SWFs — lending across equipment leasing, transport, consumer, real estate and infrastructure; the platform has now invested $19bn+ across asset-backed. (Financial Standard, Oct 2)
  • Aspirity Partners — closed its debut fund at €890m (just over $1bn), anchored by Yale University, and immediately inked its second deal; the former Vitruvian dealmakers' debut clears the first-time-fund jitters that defined 2024–25. (WSJ Pro, Oct 2)
  • HarbourVest Partners — $2.4bn raised at first close of its first dedicated private-credit secondaries strategy, per WSJ Pro; ~$500m already deployed across five credit secondary transactions as fundraising continues into 2027. (WSJ Pro, Sept 27)

DEALS

  • Tinicum + Blackstone / Senior plc — all regulatory conditions satisfied for the £1.28bn (£1.40bn EV) takeover of the UK engineering group; the court sanction hearing was Oct 6 and the scheme becomes effective today, Oct 8. Sponsor capital still buys UK industrials. (Morningstar / Alliance News, Oct 7)
  • Sycamore Partners is nearing a deal to sell UK pharmacy chain Boots for close to $9bn including debt to the Canadian arm of the Weston family — one year after taking Walgreens private in a $24bn deal, the carve-up of the noncore assets begins. (WSJ Pro, Oct 2)
  • Aspirity Partners + EIFO (Denmark's sovereign investment fund) — combined investment of over €100m in infrastructure/connectivity company Onomondo, with EIFO increasing its own stake alongside. (WSJ Pro, Oct 2)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • ASIC's stop order (see The Lead) is the enforcement headline: interim orders bar ASCF from offering, issuing, selling or transferring interests in the three funds until disclosure defects are fixed — and ASIC warned the orders could be made permanent. The signal to retail-facing private credit is unmistakable. (Reuters, Oct 7)
  • SEC valuation rigor. SEC chief accountant Kurt Hohl and investment management director Brian Daly issued a joint staff statement urging managers, boards and auditors to apply greater rigor to private-asset valuations, with private credit front and center: loans don't trade on secondary markets, Level 3 inputs dominate, and management owns the fair value call even when borrower data is delayed. (Connect Money, Oct 2)
  • The SEC's Sept 30 retail-access package (performance-fee modernization, interval-fund repurchase flexibility, five accredited-investor notices) is now in its 60-day comment window. Read the two regulator stories together: the US is widening the door while Australia polices the welcome mat. (WSJ; Alternative Credit Investor)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • Norges Bank Investment Management advised Norway's finance ministry to cut the €2trn Government Pension Fund Global benchmark's sovereign-bond allocation from 70% to 50% of fixed income — a move implying sales of roughly €100bn in government bonds, reweighted toward spread product. The world's largest allocator rebalancing is itself an allocator trade. (IPE, Oct)
  • P+ awarded Schroders a €200m mandate for European private equity, while Danish peer PKA put DKK6.4bn (€860m) into European listed companies — Nordic pensions are deploying again after a quiet August. (IPE, Oct)
  • Germany's DTCP held a first close of its new defence-related fund with commitments from Denmark's EIFO and Danica — European defence capital keeps formalizing as a private-markets strategy. (IPE, Oct)

THE NUMBER

$270 billion — private credit held by registered funds as of December 2025, up nearly 60% from $170bn in December 2020 (SEC staff, per Connect Money). That growth is exactly why both sides of today's lead are happening at once: a $270bn retail-adjacent book invites the SEC's valuation scrutiny, the retail-access expansion, and the ASIC-style enforcement. Scale is the strategy and the target.

MEME CLEANSER

ASIC's stop order lands the same week private credit managers pitch the asset class as a "known, understood, income-producing allocation." Somewhere a compliance officer is deleting the word "attractive" from a PDS for the third time before 7am — which, to be fair, is more rigorous credit analysis than most models get.

KKR pays $5.1bn for the plumbing

Wednesday, October 7, 2026

THE LEAD

$5.1 billion. That's what KKR agreed to pay for Gen II Fund Services, the private-capital fund administrator, buying it from Hg, General Atlantic and other minority investors (AltAssets, Oct 6). Step back: the biggest story in private markets this week isn't a company being taken private — it's a buyout firm paying up for the company that administers everyone else's funds. The logic is arithmetic. When H1 2026 secondary volume runs at $118bn and GP-led deals are more than half of it, somebody has to value, transfer, report, and audit the positions changing hands. Fund admin was supposed to be boring infrastructure; KKR just priced it like a growth asset. If the industry's own back office is worth five billion dollars, the liquidity machine it serves is running very hot indeed — and every administrator still independent just became a target.

Sources: AltAssets

FUND CLOSES

  • Energy Capital Partners — $834m for a single-asset continuation vehicle holding Next Wave Energy Partners, out of its $3.3bn Fund IV — giving existing LPs a cash-out option on the energy infrastructure asset while ECP keeps managing it. GP-led liquidity is now the default plumbing of the exit market. (AltAssets, Oct 6)
  • Linse Capital — $150m raised for the Ignition strategy, moving the firm into early-stage deep tech after a decade of concentrated growth-stage investing. Even specialist managers are widening the funnel. (AltAssets, Oct 6)
  • StepStone Group + ASB Capital — launched an open-ended, Sharia-compliant private credit fund targeting senior secured lending to US middle-market businesses. Gulf capital keeps formalizing its route into American direct lending. (AltAssets, Oct 6)

DEALS

  • TDR Capital agreed to acquire OCU Group from Triton, per AltAssets' Oct 6 deal roundup — continuing the steady sponsor-to-sponsor churn in UK services assets where operational buyers are scarce and sponsor capital is not.
  • KKR / Gen II (see The Lead) — the other side of the same coin: sponsor capital flowing into the infrastructure of the market itself rather than its portfolio companies.

REGULATION & POLICY

  • The comment clock is running. The SEC's September 30 package — performance-fee modernization, interval-fund repurchase leeway, and the five accredited-investor definition notices — is now in its 60-day public-comment window from Federal Register publication. All three were approved unanimously; Chair Paul Atkins frames it as "freedom and fairness" for retail access to private funds. Critics, including the PE Stakeholder Project, call the performance-fee expansion a giveaway letting managers "charge higher fees to retirement savers." Expect the comment letters to be where this fight actually happens. (WSJ; PE Stakeholder Project, Oct 1)
  • Form PF fallout watch. The twice-extended enhanced filing rule hit its Oct 1 compliance date; funds are now filing the expanded confidential data on exposures, counterparties and currencies while the SEC develops its promised less-burdensome rewrite. First filing season, meet first rewrite. (PLANADVISER)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • CalSTRS deployed more than $9bn across private equity, venture capital, private credit and real estate in H1 2026 — nearly $4.9bn into real estate (still below its 15% target despite a -4.44% three-year return) and $4.4bn into PE/VC/private credit, including $346m to Permira IX and $400m to Apollo XI. The $300bn+ teacher fund is still buying the dip on allocations while its real estate book recovers. (Connect Money, Sept 30)
  • British Business Bank — £10m commitment to Fuel Ventures to boost the VC firm's early-stage and follow-on capacity, a small ticket with an outsized signal: the state's balance sheet keeps underwriting UK venture risk. (AltAssets, Oct 6)

THE NUMBER

$118 billion — H1 2026 secondary transaction volume, up 15% year on year, with GP-led deals accounting for 53% of activity; full-year 2025 was a record $240bn, up 48% (Jefferies via 0100 Conferences). That is the number behind KKR's $5.1bn Gen II bet: the secondary market isn't a side trade anymore, it's the market's settlement layer — and the infrastructure pricing it is now worth more than many of the funds it serves.

MEME CLEANSER

Private equity's great liquidity breakthrough of 2026 turns out to be selling each other the back office. Somewhere, a secondaries partner is explaining that the $5.1bn Gen II valuation is "actually quite reasonable on a forward EBITDA basis," while the LPs who just wanted a dividend check wait for the fund administrator to process it.

The 20% discount club: retail secondaries go mainstream

Tuesday, October 6, 2026

THE LEAD

20%. That's the discount to NAV that Cox Capital Partners is now offering retail investors holding Blue Owl Credit Income Corp. (OCIC) — a cash tender for Class I shares announced Monday (Oct 5), the fifth such tender in Cox Capital's Retail Secondaries Fund I program. The menu now covers the retail private-credit complex in full: Blackstone Private Credit Fund (12.5%), Apollo Debt Solutions BDC (14.6%), Ares Strategic Income Fund (13.4%), HPS Corporate Lending Fund (17.5%), and now OCIC at the deepest discount of the lot. The pitch is simple: when the fund's own quarterly repurchase program is oversubscribed, Cox gives you another exit — at a price. OCIC's own numbers explain why the line is forming: preliminary Q3 repurchase requests of roughly $3.1 billion (16.8% of shares outstanding) against a 5% quarterly cap, with the fund expecting to accept only ~30% of tendered shares pro rata. A dedicated liquidity vehicle buying non-traded BDC shares at 80 cents on the dollar is no longer a niche trade — it's becoming the release valve for the whole retail alts complex. Watch what the other four discounts do next; the spread between them is where the distress signal lives.

Sources: Business Wire; Morningstar

FUND CLOSES

  • Niobrara Capital Partners — more than $1.1bn for its debut buyout fund, beating target by ~50% less than two-and-a-half years after launching the firm. A tech-focused first-time manager raising hard in a crowded European queue — the bar for new firms is now nine figures minimum. (AltAssets, Oct 5)
  • LAUNCHub Ventures — €65m first close for Fund III, most of the way to a €75m+ target, keeping the Central and Southeast European early-stage investor at predecessor size. (AltAssets, Oct 5)
  • DTCP — first close of a new defence-related fund with significant commitments from Denmark's EIFO and Danica, as Nordic capital keeps flowing into defence tech. (IPE)

DEALS

  • Warburg Pincus is buying into Ares and LightBay Capital-backed Awayday, the vacation-rental manager, through its $4bn-plus Capital Solutions Founders Fund — providing liquidity to existing investors while Ares and LightBay keep control. The classic GP-led minority recap: no exit, no control change, just a liquidity window for holders who want one. (AltAssets, Oct 5)
  • Blue Owl redemptions ease. OCIC withdrawal requests fell to 16.8% in Q3 from 18.8% in Q2; across two flagship funds, requests totaled $4.2bn vs. $4.7bn the prior quarter, and the firm will repurchase the customary 5%. Blue Owl's stock is still down ~45% over the last year. Direction improving; backlog very much alive. (Reuters, Oct 2)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • The Form PF deadline just landed. The twice-extended Biden-era rule requiring private funds and advisers to file enhanced confidential data on exposures — investments, counterparties, currencies, industries — hit its compliance date of October 1, 2026, after the SEC's 3-1 vote last year to extend it while it develops a less burdensome rewrite. Funds are now filing; the rewrite isn't here yet. (PLANADVISER)
  • Comment clocks still ticking on the September 30 SEC package (performance-fee modernization, interval-fund overhaul) and the five accredited-investor definition notices — 60 days from Federal Register publication. (AIMA; catenaa)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • Connecticut pensions — Treasurer Erick Russell approved $725M in private-markets commitments, including $150M to an HgCapital junior-debt fund backing the firm's own portfolio companies and a doubling of the pensions' $75M private-equity ticket. The moves advance a plan to roughly triple the state's private-credit exposure. (Bloomberg Law)
  • ABP — €250m anchor commitment to the EQT-managed Scaleup Europe Fund, as the Dutch pension giant prepares to deploy about €1bn into European venture capital over the next three years. (AltAssets, Oct 5)
  • The great US underweight. Australia's Australian Retirement Trust, Canada's La Caisse, and the UK's People's Pension are all holding US equities below global benchmark weights (People's Pension's US share fell to 49% from 53% at end-2025; MSCI ACWI puts the US at ~64%), citing stretched AI-concentration valuations. A Marsh survey of 430 asset owners ($5.76T AUM) found 48% changed geographic positioning in the past year, and ~a third plan to cut US equity exposure in the next 12 months — double last year's share. (FT, Oct 5; Marsh via FT)
  • Norway's GPFG rethinks bonds. NBIM advised Norway's finance ministry to cut the sovereign fund's fixed-income benchmark allocation from 70% to 50% and weight government bonds by market value rather than GDP — a shift worth NOK 1.22 trillion in bonds. (IPE)

THE NUMBER

20% — the discount Cox Capital set on Blue Owl's OCIC, the steepest of its five retail secondaries tenders. The number that matters isn't the discount itself; it's that a retail exit lane at 80 cents on the dollar has enough demand to warrant a dedicated fund, a fifth tender, and a Business Wire rollout. The bid for liquidity is the market.

MEME CLEANSER

Cox Capital's official rationale for buying your non-traded BDC shares at a 20% discount: retail investors "deserve an additional path to liquidity … just as institutional investors have long relied on the secondaries market." Institutional investors relied on the secondaries market to sell at discounts, too. The difference was they never had to put it in a press release.

Advent's 85% premium: the take-private bid sheet is back

Monday, October 5, 2026

THE LEAD

85%. That's the premium Advent International put on the table Monday for Spectris Plc — a £3.7 billion (~$5 billion) takeover proposal valuing the UK precision-instrumentation and testing-equipment maker at £37.63 per share (£37.35 cash plus a £0.28 interim dividend). The bid follows a string of earlier Advent approaches; Bloomberg reports the Spectris board would likely recommend a deal at that level (Bloomberg Law; Bloomberg News). This lands 72 hours after Long Lake closed its 65%-premium Amex GBT take-private — the message to every public-company board in the industrial and instrumentation space is now unmistakable: if your multiple doesn't reflect scarcity value, a buyout firm will bring one that does. The premium era that defined 2024–25 isn't winding down; it's repricing upward.

Sources: Bloomberg Law; Bloomberg News

FUND CLOSES

  • Oaktree / Brookfield — $2B final close of the inaugural Oaktree Asset-Backed Finance Fund (ABF I), hitting its target with commitments from U.S. public pension plans and sovereign wealth funds. The strategy lends to originators across equipment leasing, transportation, consumer, real estate and infrastructure; across its wider ABF platform Oaktree has invested $19B+, and Brookfield says the combined asset-based finance platform now exceeds $60B. Asset-backed finance just got its flagship institutional vehicle — on top of a separate Oaktree vehicle aimed at private wealth. (GlobeNewswire, Oct 1; Alternative Credit Investor)
  • Apax Partners — set to launch Apax XII targeting €12B (~$13.5B), per WSJ Pro. The predecessor vintage closed with $12B+ across related vehicles in March 2024; Apax now joins CVC, Permira and Bridgepoint hunting European megafund commitments at the same time. The fundraising queue in Europe is officially a traffic jam. (WSJ Pro / Private Equity News, Oct 2)
  • Investcorp — $1.22B for its second North American private equity fund, beating its $1.1B target; targets growing middle-market services businesses. (AltAssets, Oct 2)
  • Headline — $400M for its eighth European early-stage fund as the Mistral and Black Forest Labs backer doubles down on the continent's AI sector. (AltAssets, Oct 2)

DEALS

  • Advent → Spectris — £3.7B proposal at an 85% premium; see THE LEAD.

Private credit as the pivot player in a UK telecom restructuring.

REGULATION & POLICY

  • The September 30 SEC package keeps ripening. AIMA's October 2 summary of the unanimous open-meeting vote: two rulemakings — Investment Adviser Performance-Based Compensation Modernization (amending Rule 205-3 so registered advisers can charge performance fees to registered funds and BDCs; the fulcrum-fee option exists today but only ~1% of registered funds use one) and Interval Fund Modernization (S7-2026-34: extended deferral of first repurchase offers, monthly intervals, deferred sales loads, principles-based liquidity, and — the quiet bomb — multiple share classes for closed-end funds and BDCs without exemptive relief). Private fund assets grew from $11.9T to $36.8T in the ten years to 2025, per the release. Comment period: 60 days after Federal Register publication. (AIMA; securities.io)
  • Five accompanying notices seek comment on expanding the accredited-investor definition to anyone passing a FINRA-developed exam, plus holders of the CPA, CFA, CFP, and Series 79/86/87 credentials. Chairman Atkins: thresholds are "not the sole indicators of ability to assess the merits and risks" of private-market exposure. (plutux.ai; AIMA)

THE NUMBER

85% — the premium Advent offered for Spectris, following Long Lake's 65.1%-premium close of Amex GBT last week. When public-market shareholders won't pay industrial multiples, private buyers will pay them in cash. The take-private arb is the market's last reliable alpha — and it's getting more expensive by the deal.

MEME CLEANSER

Fortress bought Majestic Wine's stores for £95M in 2019, grew sales by a third, watched profits halve on wage and tax costs — and may now exit by selling the whole thing back to Tesco for "hundreds of millions." The moral: in UK private equity, the cleanest exit from a wine retailer is the supermarket that undercut it on price for a decade.

Edition #003

The Week in Alts: the exit window is wide open

Sunday, October 4, 2026

THE LEAD

26 times EBITDA. That's what GE Aerospace paid for a castings supplier — and it just handed every private-markets model a new comp. GE's $11.75 billion purchase of Consolidated Precision Products from Warburg Pincus and Berkshire Partners ($7B cash, the rest debt; expected to close H2 2027) values the Cleveland manufacturer at roughly 26x projected 2027 EBITDA — 18x including expected synergies (Zacks; The Deal). The deal is nearly a month old (announced September 8), but this week's commentary is the story: analysts are now using CPP to reprice Warren Buffett's Precision Castparts toward $100 billion — a business Buffett admitted he overpaid for at $32B in 2016 (fortuneherald). Warburg bought CPP in 2011 and held it for 15 years; Berkshire Partners joined in a 2019 recap. The patient capital got paid like it was a growth company, because it turned out to be one: GE says its airfoil demand will grow 30%+ through 2030, and the supplier nobody can replace names the price. Meanwhile Long Lake closed its $6.3 billion Amex GBT buyout on October 2 at $9.50/share — a 65.1% premium to the 30-day VWAP — and delisted the stock from the NYSE (SEC 8-K via americanbankingnews). Aggressive entry multiples, premium take-privates closing: the exit window isn't just open, it's wide.

Sources: Zacks; The Deal; GE Aerospace press release (GlobeNewswire); fortuneherald; SEC 8-K via americanbankingnews; Bradford Stanley / Substack

FUND CLOSES

  • HarbourVest — $2.4B first close of its first dedicated private credit secondaries strategy (WSJ Pro, Sep 25). The firm has already deployed ~$500M across five credit secondary transactions and will fundraise into 2027. Credit secondaries — a market that barely existed 18 months ago — just got a flagship institutional vehicle. (WSJ Pro via markets-edge)
  • Coller Capital — $1.3B continuation vehicle for Ares's 2018-vintage U.S. direct lending fund, a portfolio of first-lien, floating-rate loans to PE-backed middle-market companies. Existing LPs get a liquidity option; Ares keeps managing. (Coller Capital announcement, Oct 2, via Caproasia)
  • Tokenized credit wave: Centrifuge launched three tokenized funds — JAAA (AAA CLOs), JTRSY (short-term Treasuries), HYB (high-yield corporates) — on Circle's Arc blockchain, with strategies from Janus Henderson and New York Life Investment Management; subscribe in USDC, minimums $500K / 100K USDC. Securitize + Neuberger Berman launched HINC, a tokenized private credit fund (high-yield, leveraged loans, CLOs) across Sui, Solana, Avalanche and Ethereum, accredited investors only. Securitize is also bringing Apollo's ACRED diversified credit fund to the Sei network via Wormhole. (SignalPlus, Oct 3; adbytes.media; BitRss)

DEALS

  • GE Aerospace → CPP — $11.75B; see THE LEAD.
  • Long Lake → Amex GBT — $6.3B closed; 65.1% premium; NYSE delisting. (SEC 8-K via americanbankingnews)
  • GTCR → Tactacam — the Chicago buyout firm acquired the cellular-connected camera maker (1M+ subscribers, expanding from trail cameras to home/commercial security) from Bertram Capital for "well north of $1B," out of GTCR Fund XIV ($11.5B, 2023 vintage). Sponsor-to-sponsor, subscription-revenue consumer tech. (WSJ Pro)
  • Nuveen + Schroders — TIAA-owned Nuveen completed its takeover of Schroders on October 1, creating a ~$2.6 trillion group (Schroders equity ~£9.9B). The combined firm claims a global top-10 ranking across active equities, active fixed income, and private markets. Schroders runs separately for 12–18 months; London becomes the non-U.S. HQ. (Catenaa, Oct 2)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • FERC becomes a private-equity battleground. Senator Elizabeth Warren, ranking member of the Senate Banking Committee, plus Reps. Carson (D-IN), Spartz (R-IN), Tlaib and Pressley wrote FERC Chair Laura Swett on September 28 urging her to reject BlackRock's GIP + EQT $33.4 billion acquisition of AES Corporation. Their argument is math, not politics: GIP's PE funds target 15–20% IRRs over five years, while regulated utilities have historically returned ~10% — and the gap, they argue, gets closed on the backs of ratepayers in Indiana and Ohio. AES says rates and transaction costs won't touch customers. FERC is still reviewing; the commission's "public interest" standard forces it to weigh exactly this. (Reuters, Sep 29; banking.senate.gov)
  • Ohio's PUC has already approved the AES deal with conditions — but state AGs and consumer advocates are also pushing FERC to halt the NextEra–Dominion merger. The PE-takes-public-utilities trend is now a multi-front regulatory fight. (EnergyCentral)
  • The September 30 SEC vote (covered in Saturday's edition) keeps ripening: Alternative Credit Investor's read is that interval-fund modernization plus the new 20%-of-gains performance-fee framework makes interval funds "the default wrapper" for retail private credit. Expect every large manager to have a retail shelf by Q2. (Alternative Credit Investor, Oct 1)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • Washington State Investment Board approved $950M in new PE commitments — $650M to GTCR Fund XV and $300M to CD&R Associates XIII. Texas Teachers (TRS) added $200M each to Artemis Healthcare III, BDT & MSD Bluebonnet, and GTCR XV/A&B. Virginia Retirement System disclosed $300M each to GTCR XV and Blue Owl Real Estate VII, $200M each to Apollo Investment Fund XI, General Atlantic SMA II and ValueAct Strategic Global II. GTCR XV is the week’s allocator consensus trade. (Dakota September 2026 Public Pension Brief)
  • The fee squeeze is now quantifiable. Dakota's 2026 public pension fee report (Oct 1): fund-of-funds and secondaries programs price at 0.5–1.0%; direct buyout at 1.5–2.0% plus 20% carry. In credit, middle-market senior direct lending base fees sit at 0.45–0.75%, and some evergreen direct lending structures price at 0.65% with no carry at all — while special situations/opportunistic credit commands 1.5–1.75% plus 15–20% carry. The basis matters as much as the rate: a fee on committed capital vs. invested capital can diverge sharply by year three. (Dakota, Oct 1)
  • Oregon Investment Council is dialing PE back: target cut from 20% to 19%, with a new standalone credit portfolio at 7.5% — an explicit move "away from private equity and toward more liquid public market investments," per State Treasurer Elizabeth Steiner. (Markets Group)

THE NUMBER

0.65% with no carry — the fee some allocators now pay for evergreen direct lending exposure, per Dakota's 2026 pension fee report. When the wrapper is a commodity and the basis is invested capital, the manager's entire business is origination scale. That's why everyone's fundraising into credit secondaries.

MEME CLEANSER

Warburg Pincus bought a castings company in 2011, held it for 15 years, and sold it to the customer at 26x EBITDA. The moral of the week: in private equity, the best exit multiple comes from being the bottleneck in someone else's earnings call.
