Edition #049

CVC exits Cary at about €3 billion to the Canadians and Nordic

Thursday, November 19, 2026

THE LEAD

About €3 billion — and a full exit for CVC. CVC Capital Partners is selling its entire stake in Cary Group, the Stockholm-based vehicle glass repair and replacement company, to a consortium of the Canada Pension Plan Investment Board, the Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan Board and Nordic Capital, in a transaction valued at approximately €3 billion, Bloomberg reported. The two Canadian pensions and Nordic will each hold roughly equal ownership, and Nordic Capital is reinvesting in the business alongside them — a sponsor-to-sponsor-plus-pensions handover, not a trade sale or an IPO.

CVC has co-owned Cary since 2022 and exits completely under the deal. The structure is the story: two of Canada's largest pension funds are not writing a minority cheque or co-investing at the margin — they are taking equal ownership stakes next to the sponsor, in a European automotive aftermarket services business. Direct, equal-share pension ownership of a €3 billion asset is the allocator model the industry keeps predicting; on Tuesday it was the deal.

For CVC, the exit lands the same week the firm set out its own succession: yesterday it named TPG president Todd Sisitsky and its own president Peter Rutland as future co-CEOs, with Rob Lucas stepping back from the top role by the first quarter of 2028 (see Deals). Selling the glass company, buying the succession plan — a tidy week in Amsterdam.

Sources: Bloomberg via AlphaMaven, Nov 17; AltAssets, Nov 18

FUND CLOSES

  • Endless VI — £425 million final close. The Leeds firm closed its sixth fund oversubscribed after a six-month fundraise run in-house without a placement agent, backed mainly by existing investors plus a global base of universities, foundations, family offices, pension funds and financial institutions. Endless VI targets UK mid-market carve-outs, family-owned businesses, complex buyouts and turnarounds, typically investing £20 million to £60 million for a controlling stake. It follows Endless V, which closed at £400 million in November 2020. (Endless LLP, Nov 17; BusinessCloud via AlphaMaven, Nov 18)
  • Argonaut Private Equity Fund VI — $606 million final close. The Tulsa firm's sixth fund beat its $550 million target, backed by insurance companies, pension funds, endowments, foundations, family offices and sovereign wealth funds, and continues its industrial and manufacturing focus across underserved U.S. markets. Since closing Fund V in 2023, Argonaut has fully deployed that fund and completed exits including American Well Services, Crimson Steel Supply and VoidForm Products. (Argonaut via Business Wire, Nov 17; AltAssets, Nov 18)
  • Ren Life Sciences Partners II — €375 million close, above hard cap. The specialist European life sciences firm's second fund closed oversubscribed at €375 million (about $411 million), above its €350 million hard cap and €300 million target, in a raise reported to have taken just over four weeks from launch. The Guernsey-domiciled fund had Langham Hall as administrator, with Pacenote Capital as exclusive placement agent. (Dealroom, Nov 18; Langham Hall via InservGlobal, Nov 18)

DEALS

  • Permira and Freeman Spogli take a majority of Brunt Workwear at a reported $800 million-plus valuation. The two firms will acquire a majority stake in the founder-owned work boot and apparel brand in a deal valuing Brunt at more than $800 million, Reuters reported Wednesday, citing three people familiar with the matter. Brunt confirmed Permira and Freeman Spogli are investing but disclosed no financial details; founder Eric Girouard stays on as CEO with the existing leadership team. Brunt had been exploring options including a sale with JPMorgan's help, per earlier Reuters reporting. Valuation is reported, not confirmed by the parties. (Reuters, Nov 18)
  • CVC sets its succession: a rival's president as co-CEO. CVC Capital Partners named TPG president Todd Sisitsky and its own president Peter Rutland as its next co-chief executives, a leadership handover that will see Rob Lucas step back from the top role by the first quarter of 2028. Poaching the sitting president of a listed rival to co-run the firm is about as direct as succession signalling gets. (AltAssets, Nov 18)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • IOSCO sizes tokenized real-world assets at $37 billion — and calls the market "transitional and fragmented." The global securities regulators' body released its 2026 monitoring report on tokenization this month: tokenized real-world assets on public blockchains climbed from about $16 billion on July 1, 2025 to $37 billion on June 30, 2026, using RWA.xyz data. Fixed income products and money market funds remain the dominant classes; tokenized equities emerged as a new product class, growing from $330 million to $1.7 billion in twelve months; primary issuance is the most developed part of the chain while secondary trading and distribution lag. IOSCO Board Chair Jean-Paul Servais flagged classification, disclosure and conduct questions, especially for products marketed across borders or built for permissionless trading. (IOSCO 2026 Monitoring Report, via Traders Union and Genfinity, Nov 18)
  • The SEC retailization comment clock is running: December 4 and December 7. The Commission's September 30 proposals to expand "responsible retailization" of private markets — performance fees for regulated funds and a broader "qualified client" definition, interval fund modernization with multiple share classes, and new exam- and credential-based paths to accredited investor status — are still proposals, not rules. Comments on the interval fund, share class and accredited investor pieces are due December 4, 2026, and on the performance fee proposal December 7, 2026. Anyone whose fund documents assume the outcome should be writing comments, not term sheets. (SEC proposals, via Akin / AFS Law alert, Oct 29; Private Equity Law Report, Nov 12)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • MassPRIM approves up to $250 million of private equity inside a $1.9 billion meeting. The Massachusetts Pension Reserves Investment Management Board approved $1.9 billion in investments and commitments across public equity, fixed income, private equity and hedge funds at its November 17 meeting. The private equity slice is up to $175 million to American Industrial Partners Capital Fund IX and up to $75 million to lower middle-market buyout fund Vance Street Capital Fund V. The pension fund totals $131.3 billion; its actual private equity allocation was 16.6% at June 30. (Pensions & Investments via AlphaMaven, Nov 18)
  • CalPERS nearly doubled its diverse-manager allocations — and its private equity book returned 17%. CalPERS allocated $13.4 billion to diverse managers in the fiscal year ended June 2026, up from $7.3 billion the prior year, with commitments to emerging managers rising to $2.6 billion from $2.2 billion; since 2022 it has committed about $8.8 billion to 35 emerging managers and $35 billion to 60 diverse managers. The system's private equity portfolio returned 17% over the 12 months to June 30, 2026, helping a 14.8% total fund return, and its current PE strategy generated a 36% return over that year against 4% for the prior strategy, per the fund's own reporting to its board this week. (CalPERS, Nov 18)

THE NUMBER

$37 billion — the value of tokenized real-world assets on public blockchains at June 30, 2026, per IOSCO's 2026 monitoring report using RWA.xyz data, up from about $16 billion a year earlier. The composition matters more than the growth: fixed income and money market funds dominate, tokenized U.S. government debt alone rose from about $9 billion to nearly $15 billion between November 2025 and July 2026, and the genuinely new private-markets slice — tokenized equities — is $1.7 billion of the total. Tokenization is real, growing, and still mostly a wrapper for the safest assets in the world. (IOSCO via Genfinity, Nov 18)

MEME CLEANSER

A glass repair company just changed hands for €3 billion without ever troubling the public markets, the pensions bought it in equal shares like a syndicate of very polite sponsors, a work-boot brand is worth more than $800 million, and the world's securities regulators spent 59 pages concluding that tokenization is "transitional and fragmented" — which is also a fair review of most fund docs. Private markets on a Thursday: the exit is another fund, the buyer is a pension, and the windshield, at least, is fully transparent. Happy Thursday.

Edition #048

Blue Sea beats its own hard cap at $800 million

Wednesday, November 18, 2026

THE LEAD

$800 million — past the target, past the original hard cap, in four months. Blue Sea Capital announced Tuesday the close of Blue Sea Capital Fund IV LP and its executive fund at $800 million of aggregate commitments — significantly oversubscribed, above its $550 million target and its original $700 million hard cap. Launched in May 2026, the fund secured commitments above the revised hard cap in four months. Fund IV is about 29% larger than Fund III, which raised nearly $620 million including its executive fund.

The West Palm Beach firm now manages more than $2.5 billion, and Fund IV is its first vehicle dedicated exclusively to healthcare — healthcare products, technology-enabled services, outsourced services and multi-site services, in companies valued up to $500 million. Lazard acted as global placement agent and Kirkland & Ellis as fund counsel. Managing Partner J.R. Davis tied the raise to investor conviction in the healthcare-only focus.

The context is the exits: Blue Sea completed four in 2026 — SignatureMD, WillowWood, One Physics and Spectrum Vision Partners — representing about $1.5 billion in total enterprise value, including its first continuation vehicle, for One Physics. Yesterday's edition led with Gemspring raising $3.5 billion in under 90 days; today a healthcare specialist lifts its own cap to get to $800 million. The pattern is the same: managers with fresh distributions are not struggling to raise — everyone else is reading about them.

Sources: Blue Sea Capital via GlobeNewswire, Nov 17; AltAssets, Nov 18; Dealroom, Nov 17

REGULATION & POLICY

  • BlackRock asks the SEC for a new wrapper: the exchange-traded interval fund. BlackRock affiliates filed November 16 for exemptive relief to create exchange-traded interval funds (ETIFs) — exchange-listed, intraday-traded shares, daily holdings disclosure, but repurchases only monthly, at least 2% of outstanding shares, which would let the vehicles hold proportionally more private assets than an ETF's 15% illiquid limit allows. The filing pairs BlackRock's private markets platform — $335.994 billion at September 30, per the application coverage — with an exchange-traded products business above $6 trillion. A filing is a request, not an approval, and no fund name or strategy has been disclosed. (Bloomberg via WealthManagement, Nov 16; Private Equity Wire via AlphaMaven, Nov 17)
  • Australia's ASIC puts private credit on notice for a third survey. ASIC Commissioner Simone Constant urged private credit lenders to make sure their information on valuations, liquidity and conduct is up to scratch ahead of a third survey of the sector, speaking at the regulator's annual forum in Sydney on Tuesday, Law360 Australia reported. The U.S. debate is about interconnectedness; the Australian one is about whether the files match the marketing. (Law360 Australia, Nov 17 — reported)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

The Maryland State Retirement and Pension System is phasing out its $4 billion Absolute Return hedge fund portfolio — 5.5% of assets at June 2025 — with a full exit expected by October 2028, and redirecting capital toward private credit and infrastructure after years of lackluster hedge fund returns, Bloomberg reported from public pension documents.   • CalPERS put $4.15 billion into private debt in Q2. CalPERS manages $653.7 billion for about 2.4 million members. Two published tallies of the quarter's total commitments differ, so this item uses only the private debt breakdown, which is consistent across the reporting. (Alternative Credit Investor via AlphaMaven, Nov 10)

THE NUMBER

$12 billion — the volume of direct lending that rotated back into the broadly syndicated loan market in the third quarter, the highest level since Moody's began tracking the metric, Moody's CFO Noémie Heuland told the J.P. Morgan Ultimate Services Investor Conference on November 17. Private credit and the syndicated market are usually framed as rivals taking each other's deals — the Fullscript takeout above is exactly that story. The rotation number is the counterweight: at the top of the market, the two pipes now run in both directions. (Moody's at J.P. Morgan conference via Investing.com, Nov 17)

MEME CLEANSER

A healthcare fund raised its own hard cap to let more money in, a bank syndicate handed its hung loan to the private lenders it was competing with, BlackRock asked permission to put private assets in a wrapper that trades every second but redeems every month, and Maryland fired its hedge funds to hire private credit with the proceeds. Private markets on a Wednesday: the exits are other funds, the liquidity is monthly, and the revolution will be interval-based. Happy Wednesday.

Gemspring closes $3.5 billion in under 90 days

Tuesday, November 17, 2026

THE LEAD

$3.5 billion, one close, less than three months. Gemspring Capital announced Monday the close of Gemspring Capital Fund IV, LP at its $3.5 billion hard cap — oversubscribed, and raised in a single close on all institutional limited partner commitments in under three months. The fund was targeted at a reported $3 billion, and AltAssets notes the close more than doubles the size of Gemspring's flagship buyout fund.

The Westport, Connecticut firm now manages about $9 billion. Fund IV continues its middle-market buyout strategy, targeting companies with annual revenue of up to $2 billion across sectors including aerospace and defense, healthcare, software and financial services. Sidley Austin served as legal counsel on the fundraise. Gemspring also runs Gemspring Growth Solutions, a non-control strategy that closed its second fund at $1.1 billion in 2025.

The signal is the speed. In a fundraising market defined by 18-to-24-month raises and LPs pleading the denominator effect, a mid-market manager took the whole fund, at the cap, in one sitting. Scarcity is not the market's problem — conviction is, and Gemspring's LPs apparently had it pre-loaded.

Sources: Gemspring Capital via Business Wire, Nov 16; AltAssets, Nov 16; Dealroom, Nov 16

Hamilton Lane raises a second venture fund to chase AI

Monday, November 16, 2026

THE LEAD

More than $370 million at first close — for venture, from a private-markets house. Hamilton Lane has held the first close of its second dedicated venture fund at more than $370 million in commitments, executives said on the firm's November earnings call, WSJ Pro reported today. The inaugural venture fund closed at $615 million in early 2025, and the firm is going back because it sees what it considers a generational opportunity in artificial intelligence.

The shape of the fund, per a document reviewed by WSJ Pro: 50% to 60% of capital to early-stage investments, 25% to 35% to expansion stage, and 10% to 15% to late stage, with a $2.5 million minimum commitment. The target size could not be learned, and Hamilton Lane declined to comment on the fundraise. The firm reported $1.1 trillion in assets under management and supervision as of September 30.

The tell is in the secondaries commentary: an executive said the firm sees increased demand from sellers on the secondary market and "very few people buying," creating attractive discounts for venture secondary transactions — part of the first fund's capital was reserved for exactly that. A storied secondaries buyer is raising a venture fund partly to buy other venture investors' stakes. That is the 2026 market in one sentence.

Sources: WSJ Pro, Nov 16
