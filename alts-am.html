The Week in Alts: Penske caps a $37.5bn take-private week

Saturday, October 17, 2026

THE LEAD

Penske Automotive agreed to be taken private by its largest shareholders — Penske Corp and Japan's Mitsui — in a deal valuing the auto retailer at about $16.3bn including debt. Shareholders get $212 a share in cash, an 8.6% premium to the July 21 close, the day before the initial proposal went public. Penske Corp, which already owns over 52%, will hold about 64% at close; Mitsui, at over 20% today, takes the rest. Completion is expected in Q1 2027. The deal caps a week in which take-privates stopped being a theme and became the market: five signed transactions worth roughly $37.5bn, from car dealerships to insurance software to a budget airline — and nearly all of them insider-led or sponsor-backed, not hostile.

Sources: Reuters (Oct 15)

FUND CLOSES

  • Sprints — closed its fifth fund at its €725m hard cap, roughly 40% larger than its €525m predecessor, with more than half the capital already deployed. (Tech Funding News; AltAssets, Oct 15)
  • Hg — raised approximately €1bn at the first close of Orion 1, its debut GP-led secondaries fund, past the halfway mark of its €1.75bn target after around six months in the market. (Private Equity Wire / WSJ; AltAssets, Oct 15)
  • StepStone — raised more than $3.1bn for its sixth flagship PE secondaries fund, past the halfway mark of its reported $6bn target, per amended US regulatory filings. (AltAssets, Oct 14)
  • Consonance Capital Partners — $1.35bn for its third PE fund at the hard cap, +58% on its flagship, oversubscribed in under four months. (AltAssets, Oct 12)
  • Aphias Capital — more than $1.05bn for its inaugural PE fund, beating the debut target; Rob Wolfson's (ex-HIG Capital healthcare head) new lower mid-market buyout platform. (AltAssets, Oct 12)

DEALS

  • Proofpoint → Varonis — Thoma Bravo-owned Proofpoint agreed to buy data-security firm Varonis in an all-cash deal valued at about $6.3bn, at $49.75 a share — a 52% premium to the June 22 close, the day before sale speculation surfaced. (Reuters, Oct 15)
  • GTCR + Elliott → CCC Intelligent Solutions — the consortium agreed to acquire the insurance-software company in an all-cash deal with an enterprise value of approximately $6.1bn, at $7.75 a share — about a 44% premium to the unaffected July 9 close. Expected close Q1 2027. (GlobeNewswire, Oct 15)
  • Apollo → EasyJet — the £5.7bn ($7.54bn) takeover's scheme document was published Thursday, setting a shareholder vote for November and reaffirming a close before the end of Q1 2027. (Reuters, Oct 15)
  • Ithaca Holdings → Bumble — the dating app agreed to go private in a $1.3bn all-cash deal at $6.05 a share, a 138% premium to Friday's close — and an 84% haircut from its $8bn 2021 IPO valuation. (TipRanks; Barron's, Oct 13)
  • Vista Equity ← ASSA ABLOY — Vista agreed to acquire HID's public key infrastructure business (IdenTrust, HydrantID, ZeroSSL), which generated about $58m in 2025 revenue. Purchase price not disclosed; expected close Q4 2026. (Business-News-Today, Oct 16)
  • LSEG ↔ Permira's Octus — LSEG confirmed talks to acquire the credit-intelligence provider for ~$3.5bn; no final agreement yet. (LSEG announcement via Caproasia, Oct 13)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • Australia's private-credit crackdown reaches Ares. ASIC imposed a 21-day interim stop order preventing Fidante Partners from issuing Class A units in the A$799.9m Ares Global Credit Income Fund, finding Fidante had not met its design-and-distribution obligations. The order follows similar stop orders the prior week on three Australian Secure Capital Fund mortgage funds overseeing about A$250m. (Alternative Credit Investor; Financial Standard, Oct 13)
  • SEC retailization package: comment deadlines set. Comments are due Dec 4 on the interval-fund proposal and the five accredited-investor notices, and Dec 7 on the performance-fee proposal — the Sept 30 package that would widen retail access to private-market strategies through regulated funds. None of the proposals are final. (Greenberg Traurig alert, Oct 14)
  • Form PF's enhanced disclosure regime is now live. The compliance deadline for amendments requiring private funds to file enhanced confidential disclosures on portfolio investments, counterparties, and currency/country/industry exposures arrived Oct 1. (PLANADVISER, Oct 14)
  • IMF flags tokenized markets. The Fund's October 2026 report measures tokenized real-world assets at ~$65bn (with $300–$350bn/day in tokenized repos) and warns that near-instant settlement can remove time buffers and speed up fire sales. (IMF GFSR, Oct 8)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • Milwaukee CMERS eyes a private-equity pause. The ~$6.7bn pension fund's consultant proposed no new PE commitments in 2027 — following no new PE reinvestments in 2026 — as the fund de-risks and lets its PE allocation drift down toward target as existing investments mature. (Markets Group, Oct 13)
  • Family offices now average 48.2% in private markets — and 54% of respondents plan to increase exposure over the next five years, rising to 68.1% in Spain, per the IE Family Offices Knowledge Hub report. (FamCap, Oct 15)
  • Lærernes Pension eyes DKK100bn in private markets — the Danish teachers' pension fund's current private-markets allocation stands at 27%, and it has committed close to DKK26bn so far this year across PE, private debt, infrastructure, and real estate. (IPE, Oct 14)
  • EQT hits €354bn in AUM. The Swedish manager reported €41.6bn in Q3 gross inflows, with total AUM up from €267bn a year earlier — including a €34bn contribution from Coller Capital, acquired August 31 and rebranded Coller EQT. Its tenth Coller International Partners fund launches in early 2027. (Alternative Credit Investor, Oct 16)
  • M&G launches a private-credit LTAF for DC pensions — the diversified private credit feeder long-term asset fund launches with £74m, including capital from M&G's £138bn With Profits Fund, as UK defined-contribution schemes broaden beyond public markets under the Mansion House Accord. (IPE, Oct 14)

THE NUMBER

$37.5bn — the combined value of this week's five signed take-privates: Penske ($16.3bn), EasyJet ($7.54bn), Varonis ($6.3bn), CCC ($6.1bn), and Bumble ($1.3bn). Five deals, five sectors, one message: public-market pessimism is private equity's inventory.

MEME CLEANSER

Private equity bought your car dealer, your flight, your dating app, your fender-bender software, and the cybersecurity firm guarding your passwords — all in one week. At this rate the only thing left to take private is the weekend — and someone is already modelling the take-private of Saturday.

GTCR and Elliott take CCC private in a $6.1bn deal

Friday, October 16, 2026

THE LEAD

CCC Intelligent Solutions agreed to be acquired by a GTCR-led consortium including Elliott Investment Management in an all-cash deal with an enterprise value of approximately $6.1bn. Stockholders get $7.75 a share in cash — a premium of about 44% to CCC's unaffected close on July 9, 2026, the last trading day before media speculation about a sale, and about 59% to the 30-day average price as of that date. The Chicago company connects more than 35,000 businesses across the insurance economy, and the deal is expected to close in Q1 2027, subject to stockholder and regulatory approvals. The take-private pipeline keeps rewarding patience: GTCR closed its own $14.2bn flagship just last week, and now it's deploying into vertical software with a 40-year data moat.

Sources: CCC Intelligent Solutions / GlobeNewswire (Oct 15)

FUND CLOSES

  • Sprints — closed its fifth fund at its €725m hard cap, roughly 40% larger than its €525m predecessor, with more than half the capital already deployed. Backers include endowments, foundations, pension funds, and family offices. (Tech Funding News; AltAssets, Oct 15)

DEALS

  • StepStone launches the Total Portfolio Allocator — a tool letting financial professionals, institutions, and DC plan sponsors test allocation tradeoffs and model how private markets fit a broader portfolio. StepStone reported about $913bn in total capital, including $245bn of AUM, as of June 30, 2026. (MarketNewsDesk, Oct 14)

THE NUMBER

48.2% — the average share of family-office portfolios now allocated to private markets. When the family office is nearly half-alts, the "alternative" label is doing a lot of work.

MEME CLEANSER

GTCR and Elliott just paid $6.1bn for the software that prices your fender-bender. Private equity now owns the claim estimate, the repair network, and the AI that decides whether your bumper is totaled. The only thing left to take private is the tow truck — and someone is already modelling it.

Bumble's $1.3bn take-private

Wednesday, October 14, 2026

THE LEAD

Bumble agreed to go private in a $1.3bn all-cash deal with Ithaca Holdings — a 138% premium to Friday's close, and an 84% haircut from its $8bn 2021 IPO valuation. Class A holders get $6.05 a share; Blackstone affiliates and founder Whitney Wolfe Herd consented, the board's special committee approved unanimously, and Bumble will skip its Q3 earnings call entirely while the deal closes (TipRanks; Barron's, Oct 13). New co-CEOs Ravi Gupta and John Hegeman take over as the company retreats to Austin to run a "women-first" turnaround away from quarterly scrutiny. The read-through for private markets: the consumer-tech take-private pipeline keeps humming, and PE is happily buying public-company pessimism at single-digit revenue multiples — the IPO price was $43 a share in 2021.

Sources: TipRanks (Oct 13); Barron's (Oct 13); Reuters (Oct 13)

FUND CLOSES

  • Corre Partners — $220m first close on Horizon III, a $500m-target opportunistic credit fund (hard cap $650m); predecessor Horizon II delivered a 24.7% net IRR and 2.4x net MOIC, and Corre now manages over $1.3bn. (Morningstar / BusinessWire, Oct 8)
  • PennantPark — closed a $298.6m debt securitization for its Credit Secondary Fund with a one-year reinvestment period, AAA notes clearing at 3-mo SOFR + 140bps — CLO-format financing for secondary credit positions keeps printing. (GlobeNewswire, Oct 9)
  • Bridgepoint + AIP Capital + GFH — teamed up on a $1bn aircraft engine leasing platform, per Alternative Credit Investor (Oct 13) — asset-backed origination stays the hottest corner of private credit.
  • CVC — raised $1bn for a captive CLO equity fund, per Creditflux (Oct 13) — managers keep building balance-sheet vehicles to warehouse their own issuance.

DEALS

  • Humana ↔ BlackRock — Humana's new JV with a BlackRock investment unit will buy Welsh Carson's ~$2.8bn stake in its primary care clinic business, per Reuters (Oct 13) — one of the biggest healthcare-services secondaries-style moves of the year, asset manager replacing PE sponsor.
  • LSEG ↔ Permira's Octus — LSEG confirmed talks to acquire the New York financial-data provider for ~$3.5bn (Oct 13); Permira's credit-data asset has become the strategic trophy in the private-markets data arms race. Shares fell 4.7% on valuation concerns.
  • Citation Capital's Gallo Mechanical → Cherokee Mechanical — add-on acquisition of the Georgia HVAC contractor (announced Oct 13), another tuck-in in PE's relentless roll-up of Southeast commercial services.

REGULATION & POLICY

  • Form PF's enhanced disclosure regime is now live. The compliance deadline for Biden-era amendments requiring private funds to file enhanced confidential disclosures on portfolio investments, counterparties, and currency/country/industry exposures arrived Oct 1 — after a 3–1 SEC vote pushed it back from 2025 while the Atkins commission weighs softer replacement rules. (PLANADVISER)
  • IMF flags tokenized markets. The Fund's October 2026 report measures tokenized real-world assets at ~$65bn (with $300–$350bn/day in tokenized repos) and warns that near-instant settlement can remove time buffers and speed up fire sales — calling for technology-neutral rules, clearer token-holder rights, and accepted settlement assets before the market scales. (Particle News, Oct 10)
  • SEC accredited-investor exam takes shape. FINRA is developing the competency exam (modeled on the SIE, ~$100.50 fee, in-person, valid 10 years) that would let non-wealthy investors bypass the $200k income / $1m net-worth tests — the most concrete plank of the Sept 30 "responsible retailization" package. (Catenaa; WSNext, Oct 1)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • Washington + Louisiana systems approve large distressed-debt commitments — new LP capital is moving toward stressed/distressed sleeves, per Creditflux's funds desk (Oct 13).
  • Pemberton opens a Tokyo office as Japanese investors eye European credit (Oct 13) — the latest manager planting a flag to chase Japan's under-allocated insurance and pension capital.
  • LPs are questioning the asset-allocation model itself in pursuit of uncorrelated returns, per Creditflux (Oct 13) — the strategic-asset-allocation framework that governed pension portfolios for decades is under live review.
  • NBIM vs. sovereign bonds. Norway's €2trn oil fund advised its finance ministry to cut the benchmark's sovereign fixed-income allocation from 70% to 50% — a NOK1.22trn (~€100bn) bond sale — the latest signal that the world's biggest allocator sees more value elsewhere. (IPE, Oct 5)

THE NUMBER

138% — the premium Ithaca Holdings is paying for Bumble ($6.05 vs. the $2.54 unaffected close). It's also a measure of how far public-market confidence in dating apps has fallen: a 138% premium and you're still worth $6.7bn less than the IPO.

MEME CLEANSER

Bumble's press release calls $6.05 a share a 138% premium. The IPO prospectus called $43 a starting price. Private markets didn't invent the phrase "buy the dip" — but they're the only ones left with the balance sheet to say it at scale.

Blackstone's $1bn credit continuation fund

Tuesday, October 13, 2026

THE LEAD

Blackstone just raised about $1bn for a private credit continuation vehicle — the liquidity machine has gone mainstream. Allianz Global Investors anchored the vehicle alongside StepStone, which lets existing investors exit while Blackstone keeps exposure to a portfolio of loans out of Blackstone Capital Opportunities Fund IV — a 2022-vintage fund that delivered 11% net IRR through June, per Bloomberg via Private Equity Wire (Oct 9). Evercore advised. The irony is rich: PitchBook's Q3 US PE Breakdown (Oct 5) says continuation-fund closes are tracking well behind 2025's record $97.6bn year as M&A comes back. But the industry's biggest brand just bet $1bn that the structure isn't going anywhere — it's now standard balance-sheet management for private credit, not a distress signal.

Sources: Private Equity Wire / Bloomberg (Oct 9); PitchBook (Oct 5)

FUND CLOSES

  • Conversant Capital — $845m for its debut private investment fund plus co-invests ($705m commingled, beating its $500m target by 40%+), backing real estate and real estate-adjacent businesses; SWFs, endowments, pensions in the LP roster. (Connect Money, Oct 12)
  • Apogem Capital — $472m across its real assets programme, beating target by 24%, as the NY manager expands its natural resources strategy through its fourth flagship vehicle. (AltAssets, Oct 13)
  • Gaius Capital — €50m debut fund at the hard cap plus €6m in co-invests, four months after a €40m first close; Berlin-based DACH Mittelstand succession strategy. (EU-Startups, Oct 12)

DEALS

  • One Equity Partners → Nexora — agreed to acquire the technology distribution business from DCC for $725m, per Reuters, as the Irish group keeps restructuring ahead of KKR's planned acquisition of DCC Energy. (AltAssets / Reuters, Oct 12)
  • TA Associates exits Rocscience — $535m sale, one of two anchor transactions in the Oct 12 deal roundup alongside the Nexora trade. (AltAssets, Oct 12)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • SEC's "Responsible Retailization" package (Sept 30 open meeting). The Commission proposed letting interval funds and BDCs charge performance fees up to 20% of net gains, including unrealized gains, redefining "qualified client" to include any accredited investor (opening carried interest to 3(c)(1) funds and SMAs without a separate net-worth screen), and giving interval funds more flexibility on share repurchase programs under Rule 23c-3. Five companion notices seek comment on expanding the accredited-investor definition itself. (Gibson Dunn, Oct 8)
  • SEC crypto custody proposal (Oct 1). File S7-2026-35 would let registered advisers self-custody client crypto private keys when no qualified custodian exists and add state trust companies to the permitted-custodian list — a compliant pathway, per Chair Atkins, where "none existed before." Published in the Federal Register Oct 6; comments open through Dec 7. (blockchainreporter.net, Oct 12)
  • Also on the SEC agenda: an expanded Rule 144 safe harbor for resales of restricted securities and changes to the pay-to-play two-year cooling-off period on adviser compensation from public pensions. (Hedge Fund Law Blog, Q3 2026 update)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • Danish pensions → defense PE. Danish pension funds are committing €220m to a European private equity fund to expand defense investments — institutional capital keeps finding its way into the rearmament trade. (Investment Officer, Oct 13)
  • M&G → DC savers. M&G launched a diversified private credit LTAF with £74m of initial commitments (including capital from its £138bn with-profits fund), aimed at UK DC schemes under the Mansion House Accord — 17 providers committed to ≥10% of DC defaults in private markets by 2030. (Alternative Credit Investor / IPE, Oct 12)
  • CalPERS → energy transition credit. The $640bn pension is weighing more private credit for energy-transition lending — it already holds an $800m climate fund allocation via Goldman Sachs AM, and says debt-for-green/brownfield projects is scarce enough to earn equity-like returns at debt-level risk. (Private Equity Wire / Bloomberg, Oct 6)

THE NUMBER

83% — the share of US venture investment that went to AI companies in the first nine months of 2026, per AltAssets' Oct 9 roundup of the record $515.8bn nine-month VC total. A record fundraising market that is, functionally, a single-sector market.

MEME CLEANSER

Blackstone's new vehicle lets old credit investors take liquidity while Blackstone keeps the loans — the continuation fund was invented so managers never have to sell, and the industry's biggest name just used it on its own credit book. Private markets didn't solve the liquidity problem. It figured out how to charge for the waiting.

Healthcare fundraising runs the table

Monday, October 12, 2026

THE LEAD

Two healthcare fundraises closed oversubscribed on the same morning — and the rest of the market is still waiting for deals. Aphias Capital closed its inaugural private equity fund at more than $1.05bn, beating its debut target as former HIG Capital healthcare chief Rob Wolfson launches a new lower mid-market buyout platform (AltAssets, Oct 12). Hours earlier, Consonance Capital Partners closed its third flagship at the $1.35bn hard cap — nearly 58% larger than its predecessor — after an oversubscribed process that ran less than four months. The context matters: Schroders Capital puts global PE deal count at its lowest since Q3 2021 (Oct 10), and exits are stuck in the mega-deal barbell. But LP dollars are not stuck — they are concentrating, fast, into managers with healthcare pedigrees and track records they already trust. Fundraising is not the industry's problem. Everything after the close is.

Sources: AltAssets (Oct 12, ×2); Schroders Capital via Catenaa (Oct 10)

FUND CLOSES

  • Aphias Capital — more than $1.05bn for its inaugural PE fund, beating the debut target; Rob Wolfson's (ex-HIG Capital healthcare head) new lower mid-market buyout platform. (AltAssets, Oct 12)
  • Consonance Capital Partners — $1.35bn for its third PE fund at the hard cap, +58% on its flagship, oversubscribed in under four months. (AltAssets, Oct 12)
  • SEVA Growth — $160m for its second growth equity fund, nearly double its debut vehicle, oversubscribed in under a month. (AltAssets, Oct 9)
  • Corre Partners — $220m first close for Horizon III opportunistic credit fund, targeting $500m with a $650m hard cap; predecessor Horizon II at 24.7% net IRR / 2.4x MOIC. (Business Wire, Oct 8)

DEALS

  • INWIT take-private — Ardian (32%) and Oak Holdings 1 — the GIP/Vodafone/KKR vehicle (39%) — are arranging bank financing for a bid to buy out INWIT's minority shareholders; Mubadala expected to take a stake. Watch Milan today for the price reaction: $7.1bn market value, down 27% in a year. (Reuters, Oct 11)
  • TruGolf → Polymath — the golf-tech company completed its acquisition of Polymath, the blockchain built for regulated real-world assets, now a wholly owned subsidiary of a Nasdaq-listed company. Tokenization infrastructure just got a public-market funding runway. (GlobeNewswire, Oct 9)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • IMF: tokenization is real, and so is the risk. The Fund's October 2026 Global Financial Stability Report puts tokenized real-world assets at roughly $65bn, with tokenized repos trading $300–350bn daily — but tokenized equities run about 1.5x more volatile than the same shares on traditional exchanges, over half of trading happens outside market hours, and ~80% of trades are fractional. The warning: instant settlement removes the time buffers that slow fire sales, liquidity withdrawals, and contagion — so scale it only with technology-neutral rules, clearer token-holder rights, and agreed settlement assets. (IMF GFSR, Oct 8)
  • Securitize went live on tokenized U.S. stocks (Oct 8), trading national-market stocks on-chain under the SEC's September five-year conditional innovation exemption for onchain securities markets — limited trading with traditional investor rights preserved. (rwasignal.io, Oct 8)

The Week in Alts: Sunday edition — towers over takeovers

Sunday, October 11, 2026

THE LEAD

Italy's biggest tower landlord is about to go private. Ardian (32% owner) and Oak Holdings 1 — the GIP/Vodafone/KKR vehicle holding 39% of INWIT — are arranging financing for a bid to buy out INWIT's minority shareholders, with Mubadala expected to take a stake in the deal (Reuters, Oct 11). The numbers: INWIT's market value sits at $7.1bn (€6.3bn) after falling 27% in a year — and the discount has a clear cause: its two anchor customers, Telecom Italia and Swisscom's Fastweb, have both moved to terminate and renegotiate their tower contracts. Two sponsors already control 71% of the company and are now willing to pay full price to finish the job — the customer dispute is the sellers' headache and the buyers' thesis. Deliberations are still ongoing; the financing talks with banks are live now.
