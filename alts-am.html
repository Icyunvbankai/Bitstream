The Week in Alts: Secondaries ate the week

Saturday, November 14, 2026

THE LEAD

$6 billion, 71% above target. Pantheon held the final close of Pantheon Global Secondaries Fund VIII (PGSF VIII) and its associated vehicles at $6 billion of commitments, against a $3.5 billion target — the biggest private equity secondaries fundraise in the firm's history, and nearly twice the $3.25 billion raised for Fund VII in November 2023.

It was the capstone of a secondaries week, not a one-off. Ares raised $3.6 billion for a credit continuation vehicle shifting 24 assets from Ares Capital Europe Fund IV, in what Bloomberg reported as one of the largest credit secondaries transactions to date. Private-credit secondaries volume hit $20 billion in the first half of 2026 alone — already above the full-year 2025 total, per NEPC.

The wider tape explains why. Total secondaries volume hit a record $118 billion in the first half of 2026, according to Jefferies, with GP-led deals at $62 billion, up 32% year on year. When the traditional exit stays shut, the market does not wait — it builds a second market for the first one.

Sources: PE Professional — Pantheon PGSF VIII close (Nov 12); Morgan Lewis, Nov 12; Bloomberg via Dealroom, Nov 12; Jefferies via PE Professional; NEPC via Dealroom

FUND CLOSES

  • ICG Europe IX — €12 billion final close, oversubscribed. Announced with ICG's interim results on November 11, the firm — citing Preqin — calls it the largest structured capital fund ever raised globally. ICG reported total AUM of $145 billion and $7.6 billion raised in six months. (GlobeNewswire, Nov 11; Preqin via ICG)
  • Norwest 18 — $4 billion close, the largest fund in the firm's 65-year history. Announced November 12, the multi-stage fund brings Norwest's total capital under management to $18 billion and will back venture and growth equity across technology, healthcare and consumer in the U.S., India and Israel. It follows Norwest 17, launched in April 2024 at $3 billion. (Business Wire, Nov 12, via AlphaMaven)
  • Just Climate Natural Climate Solutions I — $540 million final close. Announced November 12, the debut fund for the strategy is anchored by CalSTRS as institutional anchor and Microsoft's Climate Innovation Fund as corporate anchor, and has already made five investments across the land-use value chain. (ESG Today, Nov 12)
  • Phoenix Equity Partners Insight Fund III — £570 million final close, through its hard cap. Announced November 12, the UK lower-midmarket fund beat its £500 million target and £550 million hard cap, with a re-up rate above 90%. (Real Deals, Nov 12; AltAssets, Nov 12)

DEALS

  • Ares credit continuation — $3.6 billion, 24 assets moved. Reported November 12, the vehicle shifts a loan portfolio from Ares Capital Europe Fund IV, which closed with €6.5 billion in 2018, into a new continuation fund. It is a GP-led deal, the structure that now drives most credit secondaries volume. (Bloomberg via Dealroom, Nov 12; TradingView / Bloomberg News, Nov 12)
  • Blackstone Life Sciences commits up to $1.5 billion to Pfizer's obesity programme. Announced November 12, Blackstone will provide up to $1.5 billion through 2028 to support development of Pfizer's experimental obesity treatment berobenatide, in return for milestone-linked payments. (AltAssets, Nov 12)
  • CD&R and GTCR halt Global Payments take-private talks — reported. Global Payments shares fell 12.03% on November 12 to close at $88.18, following reports the two firms had halted a take-private reported at $40 billion given the deal's complexities, though a revival remained possible. (Reuters via Insider Monkey, Nov 12)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • The SEC's "responsible retailization" comment clock is now about three weeks out. The SEC's September 30 proposals to expand retail access to private markets — performance fees for regulated funds, a modernised interval fund rule, multiple share classes for regulated closed-end funds without exemptive relief, and new pathways to accredited investor status including professional credentials and a FINRA-developed exam — remain proposed, not final. Comments on the interval fund, multiple share class and accredited investor proposals are due December 4, 2026, and comments on the performance fee proposal are due December 7, 2026. Current rules remain in effect unless and until final amendments are adopted. (SEC, Sept 30; ArentFox Schiff, Oct 29)
  • Europe's exit problem, in official numbers. Invest Europe reported November 13 that European private equity and venture funds raised €67.6 billion in the first half of 2026, up 6% year on year, while investment fell 35% to €43.1 billion and divestments at original cost fell 40% to €13.2 billion — their lowest half-year level since 2020. Buyout funds accounted for almost four-fifths of fundraising. Divestments are measured at cost, not sale proceeds, and the figures are preliminary. (Invest Europe via AltAssets, Nov 13)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • CalPERS kept writing — $2.7 billion in real assets on top of $4.15 billion in private debt. Per a board document reported November 10, the real-assets tape included $500 million to Global Infrastructure Partners' Transition Fund, $500 million to a Macquarie Sapphire Quail vehicle, $500 million to Greystar Equity Partners XII plus a $250 million co-invest sidecar, and $250 million to a Brookfield Infrastructure co-invest, among others. (IPE Real Assets, Nov 10)
  • Most LPs say they are still underweight private credit. Goldman Sachs Alternatives' 2026 Global Alternatives Survey, published November 10, found 56% of LPs consider themselves under-allocated to private credit — up from 43% in 2025 — across 225 LPs and 36 GPs surveyed. Asset-backed finance was the favourite strategy, with 58% of LPs planning to increase commitments. (Alternative Credit Investor, Nov 10; Goldman Sachs Alternatives)
  • Form D tape: $7.28 billion reported across the 50 largest issuers in the week to November 13. AltStreet's tally of SEC Form D filings from November 6 to November 13 counted 531 issuers in total, with the 50 largest reporting $7.28 billion combined sold to date as filed. Form D amounts are what the issuer reported sold to date on the filing, not cash that moved that week. (AltStreet, Nov 13; SEC EDGAR filings)

THE NUMBER

€13.2 billion — European private equity divestments at original cost in the first half of 2026, down 40% from €22 billion a year earlier and the lowest half-year level since 2020, per Invest Europe. Fundraising in the same half rose to €67.6 billion. That gap — more money in, less money out — is the entire secondaries thesis in two numbers, and it is why a $6 billion secondaries fund can close 71% above target in the same week.

MEME CLEANSER

Pantheon raised $6 billion to buy other people's fund stakes, Ares moved 24 loans into a new fund to keep holding them, ICG closed the largest structured capital fund ever, and Europe exited at a six-year low. Private markets this week: nobody is selling companies anymore — they are selling the funds that own the companies, to newer funds, at record size. Happy Saturday.

Edition #043

Pantheon closes its biggest secondaries fund ever at $6 billion

Friday, November 13, 2026

THE LEAD

$6 billion, 71% above target. Pantheon has held the final close of Pantheon Global Secondaries Fund VIII (PGSF VIII) and its associated vehicles at $6 billion of commitments, against a $3.5 billion target. It is the biggest private equity secondaries fundraise in Pantheon's history, and nearly twice the $3.25 billion raised for Fund VII in November 2023.

The new money is not just bigger — it is broader. A significant share came from investors in Asia and the Middle East who had not previously backed the program. PGSF VIII will invest across both LP-led deals, buying fund interests from LPs who want out early, and GP-led deals, typically continuation vehicles, with a focus on mid-market buyout funds and companies globally, diversified across vintages, sponsors and geographies.

The backdrop explains the appetite. Total secondaries volume hit a record $118 billion in the first half of 2026, according to Jefferies, with GP-led transactions at $62 billion, up 32% from $47 billion a year earlier. Pantheon deployed about $3.3 billion across 17 secondaries transactions in 2025, a record for the firm, and in May 2026 closed its first private equity collateralised fund obligation at $1 billion, above a $750 million target.

When exits stay slow, liquidity itself becomes the asset class. Pantheon just raised $6 billion to sell it.

Sources: PE Professional — Pantheon PGSF VIII close; Jefferies via PE Professional

ICG closes Europe IX at €12 billion, the largest structured capital fund ever raised

Thursday, November 12, 2026

THE LEAD

€12 billion, oversubscribed, and a record. ICG held the final close of Europe IX at €12 billion, which the firm — citing Preqin — calls the largest structured capital fund ever raised globally. The close headlined ICG's interim results on November 11: total AUM of $145 billion, fee-earning AUM of $90 billion (up 9% in twelve months), and $7.6 billion raised in six months alone.

The context makes the number louder. CEO Benoît Durteste noted this could be the fifth consecutive year of declining private capital fundraising globally — and that ICG has now closed seven funds at or above target in two years. Management fees rose 12% to £374 million and fee-related earnings 18% to £199 million, with Europe IX's final close contributing £41 million of catch-up fees.

In a fundraising market that keeps shrinking, the biggest structured capital fund in history did not just clear. It was materially oversubscribed. Scale is not a strategy everywhere in private markets right now — but at the top of structured capital, it is the whole game.

Sources: GlobeNewswire — ICG interim results (Nov 11); Preqin via ICG

FUND CLOSES

  • Phoenix Equity Partners Insight Fund III — £570 million final close, through its hard cap. Announced November 12, the UK lower-midmarket fund beat its £500 million target and £550 million hard cap in about seven months, roughly 40% above the £415 million Fund II closed in 2022. The re-up rate topped 90%, and 15 new LPs contributed about £200 million. The fund will make 10–15 platform investments of £20 million to £200 million in founder-led UK and Ireland businesses valued at £50 million to £150 million. (Real Deals, Nov 12; AltAssets, Nov 12)
  • JLL Partners Fund IX — approximately $1.4 billion final close. Announced November 10, the New York middle-market fund's capital raised meaningfully exceeded its target and the approximately $866 million size of its predecessor. JLL invests across healthcare, industrials and business services. (Weil, Nov 10)
  • Harwood Private Capital UK Fund II — first close at approximately £120 million including co-investment vehicles. Announced November 12, the British Business Bank added £40 million, taking its total commitment to Harwood to £75 million after the £35 million it put into the inaugural fund in 2020. Harwood provides flexible private credit to UK smaller and lower mid-market companies. (British Business Bank, Nov 12)
  • Proximal Ventures debut fund — $27.5 million close against a $25 million target. Announced November 11, the U.S. venture firm's first fund, focused on AI-driven life sciences, drew backing from investors including Kleiner Perkins, SignalFire and Bertelsmann Healthcare Investments. (AltAssets, Nov 11)

DEALS

  • KKR and Diamondback Energy agree to buy Five Point's Deep Blue stake — reported at $2.1 billion. Announced November 11, KKR and Diamondback agreed to acquire Five Point Infrastructure's 70% stake in Deep Blue Midland Basin, a water infrastructure platform serving oil and gas producers in West Texas. The $2.1 billion figure is as reported; deal terms were not fully disclosed. (AltAssets, Nov 11)
  • Investcorp's BDC merges into BCP Investment Corp — at 90% of NAV. Announced November 11, Investcorp Credit Management BDC (NASDAQ: ICMB) entered a definitive agreement to merge into BCP Investment Corporation (NASDAQ: BCIC), managed by Sierra Crest, an affiliate of BC Partners. ICMB stockholders will receive aggregate consideration equal to 90% of ICMB's net asset value at closing, paid in BCIC shares — about $2.49 per share on September 30 NAVs (ICMB $2.76; BCIC $13.00), adjusted for estimated transaction expenses. The deal needs ICMB stockholder approval and is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027. (GlobeNewswire, Nov 11)
  • Fairbridge winds down two private credit funds — the stress case of the week. Fairbridge Asset Management told investors it will close Fairbridge Partners LP and Fairbridge Partners Cayman LP after investors sought to withdraw nearly 25% of the funds' approximately $400 million in combined assets. The funds fell about 4% in September, absorbing $11 million in writedowns and roughly $7 million in additional losses, with real estate losses cited in the investor letter. (Crain's New York Business, Nov 10, via EIN Presswire, Nov 11; The Real Deal, Nov 10)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • The IPO exit window is reopening — and the SEC wants to widen it. U.S. IPOs raised $137.9 billion in the first half of 2026 across 208 deals, versus $27.7 billion across 180 deals a year earlier, per SEC figures cited by Debevoise on November 10. Chairman Paul Atkins' "Make IPOs Great Again" agenda would let issuers use Form S-3 shelf registration immediately after an IPO — the SEC estimates about 74% of reporting issuers would then be eligible for automatically effective shelf filings, versus 36% today — and extend reduced post-IPO disclosure accommodations to more companies. All of it remains proposed, not final. For PE sponsors weighing IPO exits against secondaries and continuation vehicles, the calculus is shifting for the first time in years. (Debevoise & Plimpton, Nov 10; SEC figures)
  • India weighs giving private credit funds real recovery teeth. A high-level panel headed by Niti Aayog member Rajiv Gauba is considering granting regulated private credit funds secured-creditor status under India's SARFAESI loan-recovery law, which would require an amendment to the Act, according to reports on November 12. The proposal is at discussion stage, not decided. India's private credit market holds about $25 billion in AUM (Moody's), with $3.5 billion invested in the first half of 2026 (EY). (TradingView via Mint, Nov 12; Moody's; EY)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • CalPERS adds $2.7 billion in real assets — the second big tape in two days. Following yesterday's $4.15 billion private debt disclosure, CalPERS approved $2.7 billion across real estate and infrastructure, per a board document reported November 10: $500 million to the Pan European Student Living Partnership I (Global Student Accommodation); $500 million to Greystar Equity Partners XII plus $250 million to a co-investment sidecar; $500 million to Global Infrastructure Partners' Transition Fund A/B; $500 million to Sapphire Quail Infrastructure Partners (Macquarie); $250 million to a Brookfield Infrastructure co-invest; $125 million to Vesta Co-Invest (Harbert); and $77 million to Concession Investment Holdings (IFM Investors). (IPE Real Assets, Nov 10)
  • Most LPs say they are still underweight private credit. Goldman Sachs Alternatives' 2026 Global Alternatives Survey: The Private Credit View, published November 10, found 56% of LPs consider themselves under-allocated to private credit — up from 43% in 2025 — across 225 LPs and 36 GPs surveyed. Asset-backed finance was the favourite strategy, with 58% of LPs planning to increase commitments, and GPs pointed to the wealth channel and evergreen vehicles as the growth engines (84% of GP responses combined). (Alternative Credit Investor, Nov 10)
  • Carlyle and SEI take the multi-strategy fund to U.S. wealth. Announced November 11, Carlyle and SEI launched a single vehicle giving U.S. wealth investors access to private equity, secondaries, private credit and real estate — the latest flagship-manager move to package the whole private-markets stack for the channel the Goldman survey says everyone is chasing. (AltAssets, Nov 11)

THE NUMBER

$137.9 billion — the proceeds raised by U.S. IPOs in the first half of 2026, across 208 deals, per SEC figures cited by Debevoise. A year earlier the same half produced $27.7 billion across 180 deals: 16% more IPOs, roughly five times the money. Private equity's exit problem was never a shortage of portfolio companies. It was a closed window. At $137.9 billion in six months, the window is open — and every continuation vehicle priced this year now has to compete with it.

MEME CLEANSER

ICG raised the largest structured capital fund in history in a year when fundraising fell for the fifth straight time. Fairbridge, with $400 million, is handing the keys back. Private markets in one day: the top of the market is setting records, the middle is consolidating, and the bottom is a wind-down letter. Happy Thursday.

CalPERS writes $4.15 billion into private debt in one quarter

Wednesday, November 11, 2026

THE LEAD

$4.15 billion in one quarter, from one pension. CalPERS committed $4.15 billion to private debt in the second quarter of 2026, according to figures published November 10 ahead of its November 16 investment committee meeting. The private debt total was part of $21.2 billion committed across private equity, private debt, real assets, systematic equity and insurance-linked strategies in the three months to June 30, including top-ups to existing investments.

The concentration is the story. Sixth Street took $3.35 billion of the private debt total alone — $2.35 billion to Sixth Street TAO Partners and $1 billion to Sixth Street Cottonwood Strategic Holdings I. Wellington took the rest: $500 million to Wellington Commercial Real Estate Debt Fund I and $300 million to a co-investment vehicle alongside it.

CalPERS manages $653.7 billion for around 2.4 million members, and in June it promoted Anton Orlich to deputy chief investment officer for private markets, overseeing private equity, private credit, real estate and infrastructure. The largest U.S. public pension is not dabbling in private debt. It is consolidating around a handful of large managers, at scale.

Sources: Alternative Credit Investor (Nov 10)

FUND CLOSES

  • redalpine Capital VIII — $250 million final close, oversubscribed. Announced November 10, the European early-stage fund is redalpine's largest to date, 25% above RAC VII's $200 million, and lifts firm AUM above $1.4 billion. Backers include Aviva Investors, Flexstone Partners and KfW; the fund will back about 20 companies, mainly Seed and Series A, with initial tickets of $1 million to $15 million. (GlobeNewswire, Nov 10; AltAssets, Nov 10)
  • Zacua Ventures Fund II — $135 million close, 35% oversubscribed. Announced November 10, the built-environment and physical-industries fund more than doubles Zacua's $56 million debut, drawing 30+ institutional and corporate LPs including Volvo Group Venture Capital, Holcim Maqer Ventures, Hilti Venture, Trimble Ventures and DPR Construction. (Business Wire, Nov 10; WSJ Pro, Nov 10)
  • Aldaron Fund I — over $950 million at its hard cap. Pharos said assets aggregated across its RWA platform now exceed $241.64 million. (CryptoDaily, Nov 10)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • The SEC's retailization comment clock is now the story. The Commission's September 30 proposals to expand retail access to private markets — performance fees for regulated funds, interval-fund modernization and multiple share classes, and new accredited-investor pathways via credentials or a FINRA exam — are still proposals, not rules. A November 9 law-firm update puts the deadlines plainly: comments on the interval-fund / share-class proposal and the accredited-investor notices are due December 4, 2026, and comments on the performance-fee proposal are due December 7, 2026. ALLOCATOR INTEL

(Business Wire, Nov 10; WSJ Pro, Nov 10; Business Wire, Nov 9)

THE NUMBER

$33.4 billion — what startups in the physical industries raised in the first half of 2026 alone, more than the $28.7 billion they collected in all of last year, per PitchBook data cited by the WSJ in its Zacua coverage. Construction, mining and energy technology used to be venture's side quest. At that pace, it is a main allocation — and Zacua's oversubscribed $135 million Fund II is priced off exactly that shift.

MEME CLEANSER

CalPERS wrote $3.35 billion to one manager and called it a quarter. Somewhere a mid-market GP is polishing a 90-page deck to win a $25 million ticket — while Sixth Street's allocation arrived the size of a small fund, twice. Scale is its own strategy. Happy Wednesday.

Edition #040

Securitize stops just tokenizing credit and buys the lender

Tuesday, November 10, 2026

THE LEAD

The tokenizer bought the warehouse. Securitize Corp. (NYSE: SECZ) said on November 9 it has signed a definitive agreement to acquire River Credit Labs Inc. — River One — a New York warehouse-financing platform founded in 2025 that connects stablecoin capital with traditional lending markets. Terms were not disclosed; Securitize expects the deal to close in the fourth quarter of 2026, and announced it alongside its third-quarter results and a $50 million share buyback.

River One funds lending facilities with stablecoin capital on the back end while borrowers draw and repay in ordinary dollars, financing portfolios of small-business loans, receivables and other income-generating assets. It was incubated by Framework Ventures and is backed by Framework and Maple Finance. This is Securitize's first acquisition as a public company — it began trading as SECZ on July 2, 2026 after closing its business combination with Cantor Equity Partners II.

The strategic read is simple: the firm that built the plumbing to issue tokenized funds and, since October 8, tokenized U.S. equities is now buying the plumbing that finances the assets behind them. Tokenization's front end met private credit's funding stack — and paid for the meeting.

Edition #039

Secondaries' price tape goes public: Texas Teachers got 94¢, BP got 84¢

Monday, November 9, 2026

THE LEAD

94.3% vs. 83.7%. Those are the prices two of the world's biggest LPs actually got for their fund stakes — and for the first time, the names and the numbers are both public. Texas Teachers transferred part of its EQT VII stake to Coller vehicles; Coller's evergreen paid $117.4 million for $124.5 million of value on March 31, or 94.3% of NAV. The BP Pension Fund sold its Livingbridge 5 and 6 stakes to Future Standard's evergreen, FS MVP, inside a 54-interest packet that cleared at 83.7% of NAV — with the two Livingbridge lines themselves at 65.5% and 70.2%.

The spread is the story. A study of 2,983 buyout fund trades since 2015, published October 5 by the Institute for Private Capital, puts the long-run average at 91.8% of NAV — and found no reliable sign that evergreen buyers have pushed prices up. The market's average price and its headline discounts are both real; which one you get depends on what you're selling. Meanwhile the buyers keep raising: StepStone's seventh venture secondaries fund counted $1.77 billion in an SEC filing Friday, from about 310 investors, and Blackstone's Jon Gray put Strategic Partners X at "over $16 billion raised to date" toward a target of at least $22 billion.

Secondaries spent a decade as the market where prices were rumored and discounts were assumed. This week, the tape exists — and it says quality clears near par while the rest still pays a real discount.

Edition #038

The Week in Alts: Tokenization broke out of the Treasury box

Sunday, November 8, 2026

THE LEAD

$2.38 billion. That is the size of SAFO, the tokenized cash fund Spiko launched with Amundi in March — now the world's largest tokenized fund, overtaking BlackRock's BUIDL ($2.17 billion) on October 19 and Circle's USYC ($2.37 billion) on October 27. Assets have grown about 30% a month for three months; the investor base, now more than 10,000 legal entities and individuals, is growing about 18% a month. More than two-thirds of SAFO ($1.65 billion) sits on Stellar.

It was not a one-fund week. Amundi launched a tokenized share class of its Core STOXX Europe 600 equity index fund on public Ethereum, with CACEIS — moving tokenization from cash-like products into broad equities. Schroders and Centrifuge put a tokenized share class of Schroders' emerging-market bond strategy on Solana on November 4, the manager's first deployment on the network. And Caliber went live with three more tokenized real estate offerings, up to $85 million in equity, taking its tokenized total past $110 million.

The pattern is the story: tokenization's center of gravity has shifted from tokenized Treasuries to equities, emerging-market debt, and real estate — in a single week. The plumbing is now the product. rwa.xyz counted $39.05 billion of transferable tokenized real-world assets on November 2, with Treasuries still 36.4% of the total and credit at 21.3%. The next test is whether the new wrappers draw real secondary trading, or just new packaging for the same buy-and-hold.

FUND CLOSES

  • AE Industrial closes ~$850 million single-asset continuation fund for Blue Raven. Lexington Partners led the vehicle for the defence components distributor, seven years after AE Industrial first backed the business. (AltAssets, Nov 4)
  • Intera Partners raises Finland's largest buyout fund — €450 million hard-cap close. Intera Fund V is the Finnish manager's fifth buyout fund. (AltAssets, Nov 5)
  • Evertrue Capital raises oversubscribed $17 million debut fund. Former Phoenix Court partner Yvonne Bajela's new firm targets pre-seed technology investments across the UK and Europe. (AltAssets, Nov 4)

DEALS

  • Global Payments reports Q3 — and says nothing about the $40 billion buyout. The payments processor posted adjusted EPS of $3.58, up 10%, on $3.18 billion of adjusted net revenue, raised its Worldpay synergy target to $650 million in annual run-rate savings, and reaffirmed full-year adjusted EPS guidance of $13.60–$13.80. CEO Cameron Bready declined to comment on the reported CD&R/GTCR take-private approach. (Global Payments press release, Nov 5; Motley Fool earnings call transcript, Nov 5)
  • Caliber tokenizes three more real estate offerings — up to $85 million in equity. The Caliber-Hyatt Studios Steamboat, Caliber-Hyatt Studios Phoenix/TSMC and Caliber-HS Feeder Fund offerings take Caliber's tokenized total past $110 million. Offerings are limited to accredited investors; the release is explicit that tokenization does not guarantee liquidity. (GlobeNewswire via Quantisnow, Nov 4)
  • Schroders goes onchain on Solana. Centrifuge and Schroders launched tokenized exposure to the Schroder International Selection Fund Emerging Market Bond strategy on November 4, an onchain share class of a feeder fund, with about $200,000 in assets at launch and a $100,000 minimum. (MEXC blog, Nov 5; CoinDesk via MEXC)

  • Retailization countdown: 26 days to December 4, 29 to December 7. No new SEC rulemaking since the September 30 package — comment deadlines stand at December 4 (interval fund modernization + accredited-investor pathways, including the proposed FINRA exam) and December 7 (performance fees in regulated funds). None of it is final. (SEC; Greenberg Traurig; prior editions)
  • A private-credit ETF dies quietly. The Simplify VettaFi Private Credit Strategy ETF (PCR) stopped accepting creation orders after October 30, delisted, and paid its liquidation proceeds on or about November 6. A reminder that retail wrappers for private credit still have to survive their own economics. (Simplify prospectus supplement, Oct 8)
  • Transfer-agent modernization, still pending. The SEC's proposed rule — which would allow shareholder registers to be kept on blockchain — saw its comment window close November 3. No final rule yet. (Legal 500; prior edition)

  • BlackRock's non-traded BDC paid its September tender in full. BlackRock Private Credit Fund (BDEBT) accepted 100% of the 3,193,088 shares tendered by September 4 at the September 30 NAV of $23.53, paying $75.1 million on or about October 30, per the final Schedule TO-I/A filed November 5. The fund's net assets stood at $1.60 billion at September 30. Full acceptance, no proration — a small but real liquidity data point for the retail private-credit complex. (CrowdfundedWealth/SEC EDGAR, Nov 7)
  • Tokenized funds are finding their allocators. SAFO's investor base passed 10,000 entities and individuals, growing about 18% a month, with nine in ten holders on Stellar via the Spiko app — no wallet or keys required. The distribution story is now as important as the wrapper. (Chainwire via TradingView, Nov 5)
  • Virginia's $135.2 billion pension stays the allocator story of the week. The Virginia Retirement System's plan to push private equity co-investments past $750 million a year and lean into continuation vehicles was covered in Edition #037.

$39.05 billion — transferable tokenized real-world assets counted by rwa.xyz on November 2, excluding stablecoins. US Treasury products are 36.4% ($14.21 billion), credit 21.3% ($8.33 billion), commodities 13.0% and stocks 9.2%. Another $397.98 billion sits recorded on blockchains but bound to the issuing platform — ten times the transferable total. The gap between "onchain" and "tradable" is still the market's honest measure.

A tokenized money-market fund overtook BlackRock, a tokenized index fund went live on Ethereum, a tokenized EM bond fund launched on Solana, and a private-credit ETF liquidated in the same week. Private markets this week: everything is being tokenized except the exit — and the one wrapper that actually needed retail demand couldn't find enough of it. Happy Sunday.
