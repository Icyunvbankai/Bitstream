Arrow closes €5.5 billion for distressed credit

Friday, November 27, 2026

THE LEAD

€5.5 billion, final close, no extension. Arrow Global announced on November 26 the final close of ACO III and associated strategies at €5.5 billion in investor commitments, lifting the firm's funds under management to €15.9 billion — up 16.9% year over year. The vehicle family is focused on opportunistic and distressed credit across Europe, the strategy that just earned Arrow Distressed Debt Manager of the Year at the 2026 Alternative Credit Investor Awards for the second consecutive year.

The timing is the story. Arrow deployed €2.8 billion in the first nine months of 2026, up 47% from €1.9 billion in the same period of 2025 — deployment is accelerating into the close, not trailing it. A pan-European distressed specialist raising its largest pool while deployment runs nearly half again faster than last year is a clean read on where allocators think the European credit cycle is heading: more stress, more forced sellers, and a premium on managers who can work assets, not just buy them.

A $10 billion share swap for Lukoil's world outside Russia

Thursday, November 26, 2026

THE LEAD

$10 billion in frozen shares — no cash changes hands. A U.S.-led consortium led by Todd Boehly's Eldridge signed a definitive agreement on November 25 to acquire 100% of LUKOIL International GmbH, the Vienna-based unit holding the sanctioned Russian oil major's business outside Russia, according to the consortium's Business Wire announcement and Reuters reporting. The consideration is not money: the buyers will hand over restricted shares in Lukoil and other Russian companies — securities frozen since 2022, bought up from Western funds and depositary banks — which Reuters values at $10 billion, while people familiar with the terms put the assets themselves at about $16 billion. The swapped shares sit in a restricted custodial account for five years or until sanctions are lifted, a structure built to meet Treasury's demand that Lukoil not profit from the sale.

The package is large: production in 11 countries at roughly 350,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day, three refineries (Burgas, Zeeland, Petrotel), the LITASCO trading arm, pipelines, and retail stations in 16 countries. The U.S. International Development Finance Corporation takes an ownership interest at no upfront cost to taxpayers; Citi is lead financial adviser. None of it closes without OFAC — and OFAC has form here: a Gunvor deal collapsed in 2025 and a Carlyle agreement announced in January 2026 expired in July without approval. Boehly has said closing could take up to a year. It is the third attempt to move Lukoil's ex-Russia empire into Western hands, and the first structured so the seller gets paper it cannot touch.

GTCR lines up a roughly $50 billion take-private of Global Payments

Wednesday, November 25, 2026

THE LEAD

Roughly $50 billion — and it needs friends to get there. GTCR, already Global Payments' largest shareholder with a 16.35% stake, disclosed in a regulatory filing on November 24 that it intends to pursue a take-private of the payments processor at a roughly $50 billion valuation, according to Reuters reporting summarized by AlphaMaven. The financing is the story: Blackstone, Warburg Pincus and Apollo are in talks to help fund the bid through equity or preferred equity investments, specifically to reduce GTCR's reliance on debt. The split between equity and debt has not been disclosed.

The path here matters. Reuters reported that GTCR and Clayton, Dubilier & Rice first tried a consortium effort, dropped it, and GTCR moved forward on its own — now apparently rebuilding the consortium on the financing side instead. If it lands, it ranks among the largest leveraged buyouts in the payments sector in recent years, and it is the second mega payments take-private thread in two days, after yesterday's TPG/Corpay deal for BILL. Payments is where the big checks are going.

TPG and Corpay take BILL private for $6.0 billion

Tuesday, November 24, 2026

THE LEAD

$6.0 billion of equity value — $6.3 billion enterprise value — takes BILL private. TPG has agreed to acquire BILL (NYSE: BILL), the software company that pays and collects bills for small and midsize businesses and processes more than 1% of U.S. GDP, according to PE Professional (Nov 23). TPG will hold a majority interest through TPG Capital, its U.S. and European private equity platform, and payments company Corpay (NYSE: CPAY) will take a minority interest. The enterprise value includes about $260 million of net debt.

The structure is a repeat, on purpose: TPG and Corpay are the pair that took AvidXchange private last year. Read it as the playbook consolidating — a sponsor with the platform, a strategic payments operator with the distribution, and a public-market payments asset repriced in private hands.

Edition #053

$720 million of private credit settles a family buyout in India

Monday, November 23, 2026

THE LEAD

About $720 million (₹6,700 crore) of private credit is financing one of India's largest buyouts of the year — a family one. P.V. Krishna Reddy, managing director of Megha Engineering & Infrastructures Ltd. (MEIL), has secured the financing from global private credit investors to buy out the 43% stake held by his uncle, executive chairman Pamireddy Pitchi Reddy, according to people familiar with the matter cited by The Hindu BusinessLine (Nov 21, via Bloomberg News reporting).

The lender consortium, per those sources, includes Davidson Kempner Capital Management, Värde Partners and Elham Credit Partners. The structure runs through a special purpose vehicle, Provantage Infra Pvt.: two tranches of three-year secured bonds of ₹1,750 crore each at a 17.75% base return plus an equity-linked upside payment, ₹2,700 crore of 18-month bonds at an 18% coupon, and a ₹500 crore tranche of under three years — secured partly by a pledge over 19.99% of MEIL. Reddy already owns 57% of the company.

Read it as the market signal it is: when a transaction is this large, this layered and this personal, the banks are not in the room. Private credit is — at 17.75–18% base pricing. Figures are as reported by people familiar; the parties have not published a closing release.

Sources: The Hindu BusinessLine, Nov 21 (people familiar with the matter; Bloomberg News); corroborating summary, Rift Valley News, Nov 21

FUND CLOSES

  • Blue Sea Capital Fund IV — $800 million, closed in four months. The healthcare-only firm's fourth fund came in well above its $550 million target and its original $700 million hard cap, which was raised; Fund IV (Blue Sea Capital Fund IV LP plus its Executive Fund) is about 29% larger than Fund III, which closed above target in September 2023 at $618 million. (PE Professional, Nov 19)
  • Argonaut Private Equity Fund VI — $606 million, above its $550 million target. The Tulsa firm's sixth buyout fund backs industrial and manufacturing businesses in parts of the U.S. other firms overlook, with a lower-leverage approach; the predecessor closed in 2023 at $500 million. (WSJ Pro Private Equity, Nov 17; PE Professional, Nov 19)
  • Gemspring Capital Fund IV — $3.5 billion hard cap, single close, under three months. The Westport middle-market firm's fourth buyout fund was oversubscribed and took all institutional LP commitments in one closing — more than double Fund III's $1.7 billion (2023). The Gemspring team is the fund's largest single investor; the firm now manages about $9 billion. (Gemspring Capital via Business Wire, Nov 16)

DEALS

  • Monroe Capital finances Arcapita's acquisition of MC Sign Company — announced today. Monroe acted as sole lead arranger and administrative agent on a senior credit facility supporting Arcapita Investment Management US's acquisition of Mentor, Ohio-based MC Sign, a national sign and lighting services provider to retail, telecom, hospitality, healthcare and restaurant customers. Facility size was not disclosed. (Monroe Capital press release, Nov 23)
  • Private credit steps into the $1.3 billion Fullscript financing. A consortium of direct lenders — reported to include Antares Capital, Blue Owl Capital, Carlyle and Golub Capital — committed $1.3 billion to back L Catterton and Altas Partners' majority acquisition of healthcare platform Fullscript from HGGC and Snapdragon Capital Partners, replacing an earlier bank-syndicated arrangement underwritten by Morgan Stanley; pricing was reported at 400 basis points over SOFR, issued at 96, alongside a $230 million revolver. (InforCapital, Nov 18; Krugman Insights desk note)
  • Apollo lines up banks for ADT — with private credit as the backstop. Bloomberg reported Nov 20 that Apollo added 13 banks to a nearly $9 billion debt package financing Thrive Holdings' acquisition of ADT (about $15.3 billion enterprise value, $7.6 billion equity value, $10 cash per share, expected to close Q1 2027 subject to approvals). The syndicated route is Thrive's preference; an Apollo-led private credit package stands behind it if the bank book does not fill. (Bloomberg via EL7 summary of Octus/SEC filing details, Nov 20–21)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • IOSCO's tokenization verdict: issuance works, the exit doesn't. The IOSCO Board's Tokenization of Financial Assets: 2026 Monitoring Report (FR/06/26, published Nov 18) records tokenized real-world assets growing from about $16 billion on July 1, 2025 to about $37 billion on June 30, 2026 (rwa.xyz data, IOSCO's cut-off) — and flags secondary-market liquidity as the immature stage of the value chain: thin market-making, prices that don't refresh without a trade, and property tokens at just 0.53% of the market with 872 monthly active addresses across 96 instruments. A monitoring report, not a rulebook — but it is the global regulators' shared map of where tokenization actually stands. (IOSCO, Nov 18; 82Shops Institutional Desk analysis, Nov 22)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • Virginia Retirement System allocates $825 million across Oct 16–Nov 12. The $135.5 billion system committed $150 million each to AE Industrial Partners Fund IV and EnCap Energy Capital Fund XIII in private equity — actual PE allocation 14.7% against a 15% target as of Nov 9 — plus $150 million to Sprott Natural Resource IV and $125 million to Artemis Real Estate Partners Fund V in real assets (13.6% actual vs 14% target). The balance of the $825 million went to commitments and investments outside those two buckets, per its emailed update. (Pensions & Investments via AlphaMaven, Nov 19)

THE NUMBER

$250 billion — PJT Partners' forecast for full-year 2026 secondaries volume, against $225 billion in 2025. Q3 volume was roughly $56 billion and year-to-date about $175 billion, ~6% ahead of the first three quarters of 2025 — which means hitting the forecast needs roughly $75 billion in Q4 alone, the busiest quarter of the year, in exactly the quarter when PJT estimates more than 125 continuation vehicles will be competing for the same pool of capital. Buyers noticed: LP-led deals took back the lead in Q3 at about 54% of volume. (PJT Partners Q3 2026 Secondary Market Insight via Secondary Scoop)

MEME CLEANSER

An uncle's 43% stake now clears at 17.75% plus an equity kicker, a $3.5 billion fund raises in one sitting, 125 continuation vehicles queue for the same buyers, and the global regulators' report on tokenization concludes the exit is the part nobody built. Private markets on a Monday: everyone can get in, the pricing is honest about it, and getting out remains a group project. Happy Monday.

Edition #052

The Week in Alts: The filing tape never sleeps

Sunday, November 22, 2026

THE LEAD

853 new Form D notices in five days — the private-markets pipeline filed, even as public stocks slid. SEC EDGAR's daily indexes for Monday, November 16 through Friday, November 20 list 853 new Form D notices (773 claiming Rule 506(b), 74 claiming Rule 506(c)) and 479 Form D/A amendments, Crowdfund Insider's weekly filings digest reported Saturday.

Read the week's biggest number carefully: the largest figure was an annual amendment — the AIP Fund F program's $14.99 billion program-wide total under Rule 506(c), up from $0 in its November 2025 notices. That is a program total on an amendment, not $15 billion of new money raised in a week. The same caution applies down the list: Ares Industrial Real Estate Fund LP's amendment reports $4.75 billion sold, and Brookfield's Catalytic Transition Fund reports $2.65 billion sold under 506(c).

The genuinely new-money signal sits elsewhere: 17 new Form C crowdfunding filings, four new Form 1-A filings, and a tokenization name in the Reg A queue — COSIMO Digital Holdings filed a $75 million Regulation A offering. The week's story is not a mega-close. It is volume: the exempt-offering machine kept filing at full speed into a soft public tape.

Sources: Crowdfund Insider, Weekly Private Securities Filings Digest, Nov 21 (SEC EDGAR daily form indexes, Nov 16–20)

FUND CLOSES

  • Plural Fund III — €1 billion raised, the firm's largest to date. Announced Monday, the London, Tallinn and Paris early-stage firm's third fund is almost four times its €260 million inaugural fund (2022) and double its €500 million second fund (2024). Two-thirds is earmarked for early-stage rounds and one-third for follow-ons; the portfolio it backs — Helsing, Proxima Fusion, OLIX, The Exploration Company — raised more than $3 billion of follow-on capital in 2026. (Plural via Business Wire, Nov 16; Crunchbase News, Nov 16)
  • The 2027 flagship queue is the real fundraising story. Dakota's tally of buyout fundraises to watch heading into 2027 counts at least $180 billion in flagship targets in market or pre-marketing, after Advent International closed GPE XI at $27 billion on October 27 — above its $26 billion target and the largest buyout close of 2026 so far. CVC's Europe/Americas Fund X (about €26 billion target) opens formal fundraising in January 2027; CD&R Fund XIII has about $20 billion raised toward a $26 billion target, with a final close targeted for Q1 2027. Targets and in-market amounts are as reported, not closes. (Dakota Marketplace, Nov 16)

DEALS

  • Multiples Equity leads about $100 million into GIVA. Reported Saturday, Multiples Alternate Asset Management led an approximately $100 million (about ₹900 crore) round in jewellery brand GIVA, operated by Indiejewels Fashion Private Limited. The round combined fresh issuance with purchases of shares from certain existing shareholders — part primary, part secondary — with Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas advising Multiples and Avendus Capital advising GIVA. (Economic Times Legal via AlphaMaven, Nov 21)
  • A quiet week for control deals. After last week's take-private tape, the week's disclosed private-markets deal flow skewed to growth rounds and filing activity rather than sponsor control transactions — the action was in the EDGAR queue, not the auction room. (Crowdfund Insider, Nov 21; AlphaMaven, Nov 21)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • Tokenization files under Reg A. COSIMO Digital Holdings' new $75 million Regulation A filing was the tokenization entry in the week's Reg A activity, which also included four qualification notices (Masterworks Vault 5 and Vault 6, Limitless X Holdings and PSFNetwork MasterSeries). A filing is an application to offer, not an approval or a raise. (Crowdfund Insider, Nov 21, from SEC EDGAR)
  • The retailization comment clock runs out in under two weeks. The SEC's September 30 package — performance-fee eligibility for accredited investors, interval fund modernization, rules-based multiple share classes — remains proposal-only, with comment periods closing December 4 and December 7, 2026. The five accredited-investor pathway notices (FINRA exam, CPA, CFA, CFP, Series 79/86/87) sit on the same December 4 clock. Current rules stay in effect unless final action is adopted; the income, net-worth and license tests have not moved. (SEC, Sept 30; Manhattan West summary; Raveum, Nov 5)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • MainePERS adds a new manager — up to $50 million to Godspeed Capital Fund IV. The $23.1 billion Maine Public Employees Retirement System approved the commitment at its November 12 meeting, subject to final due diligence, legal review and negotiations. Godspeed is a new relationship for MainePERS: a lower middle-market manager focused on defense, government services and social infrastructure. (Pensions & Investments via AlphaMaven, Nov 13)
  • New Jersey trims targets while it commits. The roughly $87 billion New Jersey Pension Fund's up to $400 million of commitments — $250 million to American Industrial Partners Capital Fund IX and $150 million to TPG Real Estate Partners V — come alongside target cuts: private equity's target falls to 11% from 13% and real estate's to 7% from 8% next year. Staff committed $2.3 billion across eight funds or fund families in fiscal 2026; the system remains underweight private markets. (Connect Money, Nov 9; Pensions & Investments via AlphaMaven)

$40.9 billion — distributed tokenized real-world assets on public chains on November 22, per rwa.xyz, down about 3.6% in 30 days and held by about 5.87 million wallets. Treasuries are the largest slice at about $14.1 billion, credit about $8.2 billion, tokenized stocks about $3.4 billion. Same discipline as always: represented value — the record-keeping layer investors cannot hold or transfer — was about $451.4 billion the same day. Quote the distributed number, or quote both, never mix them. (rwa.xyz via Blockchain Reporter, checked Nov 22)

The biggest private-markets number of the week was an amendment, the biggest fund story is a queue that opens in January, the tokenization headline is a $75 million application, and Maine bought defense funds while New Jersey cut its targets and committed anyway. Private markets this week: the filing cabinet did the fundraising, the calendar did the allocating, and the actual new money was a jewellery round in Mumbai. Happy Sunday.

Edition #051

The Week in Alts: Take-privates did the exiting

Saturday, November 21, 2026

THE LEAD

$6 billion, all cash — the week's exit was a delisting. BILL Holdings agreed Thursday to be acquired by TPG, in partnership with Corpay, in an all-cash transaction valued at about $6 billion. Shareholders get $63.25 per share, a 31% premium to the 60-day volume-weighted average price ending November 16, the last full trading day before media reports of talks surfaced.

TPG takes a majority stake through TPG Capital, its U.S. and European private equity platform. Corpay invests $1.1 billion for roughly a one-third stake and holds an option to acquire the full company by 2030. The deal has no financing condition, was approved unanimously by BILL's board, and is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to shareholder and regulatory approval. On close, BILL delists from the New York Stock Exchange.

That was the week's pattern in one deal: the cleanest exits were take-privates and sponsor-to-pension handovers, the fastest fund closes were single-close affairs, and the liquidity story stayed in the tender window. The rest of the week, below.

Sources: BILL / TPG / Corpay statement via Business Wire, Nov 19; Reuters, Nov 19; Payments Dive, Nov 20; WSJ, Nov 19–20

FUND CLOSES

  • Gemspring Capital Fund IV — $3.5 billion final close at hard cap. Announced Monday, the Westport middle-market firm's fourth buyout fund was oversubscribed and reached its $3.5 billion hard cap in less than three months, holding a single close on all institutional limited partner commitments. The Gemspring team is the fund's largest single investor; the firm now manages about $9 billion. (Gemspring via PR Newswire, Nov 16)
  • Blue Sea Capital Fund IV — $800 million final close. Announced Tuesday, Fund IV and its executive fund closed at $800 million of aggregate commitments — above the $550 million target and the original $700 million hard cap, secured in four months from a May 2026 launch. Fund IV is about 29% larger than Fund III's nearly $620 million. (Blue Sea announcement, Nov 17–18; PE Professional Fundraisers Roundup, updated Nov 20)
  • Hamilton Lane Venture Fund II — first close above $370 million. Disclosed on the firm's November earnings call and reported Monday, the second dedicated venture fund follows an inaugural vehicle that closed at $615 million in early 2025. The target size was not disclosed; Hamilton Lane reported $1.1 trillion in assets under management and supervision as of September 30. (WSJ Pro, Nov 16)
  • Argonaut Private Equity Fund VI — $606 million final close. The Tulsa industrial buyout firm closed Fund VI at $606 million, above its $550 million target; its predecessor closed in 2023 at $500 million. (WSJ Pro, Nov 17–18)

DEALS

  • TPG and Corpay take BILL private — $6 billion. See The Lead. $63.25 per share in cash, TPG majority via TPG Capital, Corpay $1.1 billion for roughly a third with an option to acquire 100% by 2030, no financing condition, close expected in the first half of 2027. (BILL / TPG / Corpay via Business Wire, Nov 19; Payments Dive, Nov 20)
  • CVC exits Cary Group at about €3 billion to the Canadians and Nordic. CVC Capital Partners agreed Tuesday to sell its entire stake in Stockholm-based vehicle glass repair company Cary Group to a consortium of CPP Investments, Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan Board and Nordic Capital, in a transaction Bloomberg valued at approximately €3 billion. Ontario Teachers' expects to invest about €545 million and CPP Investments about €500 million; each consortium member will hold roughly equal co-control, Nordic is reinvesting, and CVC — a co-owner since 2022 — fully exits. Cary runs about 1,800 workshops in 13 European countries and posted SEK 9 billion (about €820 million) of net revenue in the twelve months to October 2026. Close is expected in the first half of 2027. (Consortium announcement via Pulse2, Nov 17; Bloomberg via AlphaMaven, Nov 17)
  • Partners Group takes a majority of Rawlings Sporting Goods at about $2 billion enterprise value, reported. Partners Group agreed Thursday to acquire a majority stake in Rawlings, the official Major League Baseball equipment provider. Existing shareholders and Major League Baseball retain meaningful ownership. The roughly $2 billion enterprise value comes from a person familiar with the deal and was not confirmed by the parties. (Bloomberg Law, Nov 19)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • The retailization comment clock is the calendar now. The SEC's September 30 package — voted 3-0 — would expand performance-fee eligibility to accredited investors, modernize the interval fund framework, and replace exemptive orders with a rules-based framework for multiple share classes in regulated closed-end funds. The Commission is also seeking comment on additional accredited-investor pathways, including a FINRA-developed examination and credentials such as CPA, CFA and CFP. These remain proposals only: comment periods close December 4 and December 7, 2026, and current rules stay in effect unless final amendments are adopted. (SEC press release, Sept 30; Manhattan West summary)
  • Form PF compliance date sits at July 1, 2027. The SEC and CFTC's August 31 joint final rule further extended the compliance date for the February 2024 Form PF amendments from October 1, 2026 to July 1, 2027. Private fund advisers should build reporting systems to the 2027 date. (SEC / CFTC joint rule, Aug 31, via Manhattan West summary)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • MassPRIM approves $1.9 billion, including up to $250 million to private equity. At its November 17 meeting, the $131.3 billion Massachusetts Pension Reserves Investment Management Board approved up to $175 million to American Industrial Partners Capital Fund IX and up to $75 million to Vance Street Capital Fund V within a $1.9 billion slate spanning public equity, fixed income, private equity and hedge funds. MassPRIM's actual private equity allocation was 16.6% as of June 30. (Pensions & Investments via AlphaMaven, Nov 18)
  • Ohio PERS cuts private equity, adds private real estate — unanimously. The board voted unanimously on November 18 to cut its private equity target to 13% from 14% and raise its private real estate target to 12% from 11% in its $120.6 billion defined benefit plan, with the increase going entirely to value-added and opportunistic strategies. As of September 30, OPERS held about $15.85 billion in private equity (13.2%) and $12.95 billion in private real estate (10.8%). (Pensions & Investments via AlphaMaven, Nov 19)
  • Virginia Retirement System allocates $825 million, including $300 million to private equity. VRS allocated $825 million across commitments and investments made between October 16 and November 12, including $150 million each to AE Industrial Partners Fund IV and EnCap Energy Capital Fund XIII. The $135.5 billion system's actual private equity allocation stood at 14.7% against a 15% target as of November 9. (Pensions & Investments via AlphaMaven, Nov 19)
  • LA Fire and Police Pensions adds $30 million to a real estate secondaries fund. Los Angeles Fire and Police Pensions approved a $30 million follow-on commitment to Townsend Group's Real Estate Capital Solutions IV, on top of the $60 million it committed in April 2025. The fund is near the end of a $3 billion raise and is expected to hold a final close before the end of this year; it makes secondary investments in existing funds, with some recapitalisations. (IPE Real Assets, Nov 20)

$42.1 billion — distributed tokenized real-world assets on public chains on November 18, per rwa.xyz, held by about 5.8 million wallets. Same caution as always with this series: rwa.xyz's represented value was $430.4 billion the same day — a record-keeping layer most investors cannot hold or transfer — so quote the distributed number, or quote both, never mix them. Treasuries and credit remain the bulk of the distributed total. (rwa.xyz via Metacodebox, checked Nov 19–20)

A $6 billion fintech left the public markets with a payments company already holding a third of it and an option on the rest, two Canadian pensions bought a Swedish windshield business from one sponsor alongside another sponsor who sold and bought back in on the same day, a baseball-glove maker is worth about $2 billion in private hands, and Ohio's pension moved exactly one percent — slowly. Private markets this week: the exit is a take-private, the buyer is a pension, and the fund close is a single close. Happy Saturday.
