Advent's $30 billion answer to the fundraising winter

Friday, October 30, 2026

THE LEAD

$30 billion. That is what Advent International raised across its global fund complex, announced Thursday — nearly $27 billion for its 11th flagship fund, Advent International GPE XI, past its $26 billion target, plus more than $3 billion for a new mid-market strategy, the firm's first fund dedicated to smaller deals. In a year when most managers describe fundraising as demanding, Advent closed one of the largest buyout raises on record and added a whole new lane at the same time.

The mid-market fund is the tell: it will write $100 million to $350 million in equity into companies with enterprise values up to $1.25 billion, mainly in North America and Europe, using the same sector teams as the flagship. The 2026 scorecard behind the raise — about $13 billion realized across 27 businesses, about $10 billion invested across 38 — is why LPs answered. "The trust our investors have placed in us in one of the most demanding fundraising environments reflects the consistency of our track record and our discipline in returning capital," said James Brocklebank, managing partner and co-chair of Advent's executive committee. Advent manages more than $109 billion in assets. The message to the rest of the market: the fundraising winter is real, but it is not evenly distributed.

Sources: Connect Money (Oct 29)

FUND CLOSES

  • Ares — $8B first close for its newest flagship direct lending fund, targeting $10–12B in equity. Bloomberg reports the raise, via Ares Senior Direct Lending Fund IV and Ares Senior Direct Lending Core — the firm's first open-ended senior direct lending fund for institutions, both launched in April — is running faster than its previous record-setting vintage, and would represent roughly $20 billion once leverage is factored in. Treat the figures as reported, not confirmed by the manager. (Bloomberg via TradersUnion / Dealroom, Oct 29)
  • HC Partners — €100M hard-cap close for Fund III, beating its €80M target. The Dutch firm's third vehicle nearly doubled the size of its flagship fund, with commitments from all of its existing institutional investors, following two sponsor exits. (AltAssets, Oct 29)
  • Foundation Credit — ~$540M close for its second infrastructure debt fund, beating a $500M target. The US private credit manager closed the fund a week after announcing a majority investment from fellow alternative asset manager New Holland Capital. (AltAssets, Oct 29)

DEALS

  • Triton agrees a $9B Trench exit to GE Vernova. Triton Partners has agreed to sell high-voltage electrical equipment manufacturer Trench Group to GE Vernova for $9 billion, in one of the largest European private equity exits of the year. (AltAssets, Oct 29)
  • Trian-led group makes a $3.8B Wendy's take-private proposal. A Trian-led group has proposed taking Wendy's private at $3.8 billion, putting one of fast food's most recognizable names back in play. (AltAssets, Oct 29)
  • Cohere opens a Series E targeting up to CAD $7B at a CAD $35.7B valuation. The Toronto sovereign-AI company announced the initial close of a round it calls the largest private financing ever undertaken by a Canadian technology company, led by the companies of Schwarz Group, with new investors including Bell Ventures, Bullhound Capital and the Government of Canada, and returning investors Inovia Capital, NVIDIA and Prosperity7 Ventures. (PR Newswire via Morningstar, Oct 29)
  • On-chain: Solana's tokenized real-world assets hit a record $5.19B. Solana's RWA market reached the record on October 29, per data shared that day — and tokenized private credit on the network reached $1.44 billion as of October 26, up from close to zero at the start of 2025, with credit tokens from platforms such as Securitize and Maple accepted as collateral on Solana lending markets. (KuCoin / Solana, Oct 29; The Block, Oct 29)

(Stradley Ronon, Oct 13; Duane Morris via Mondaq, Oct 23; Greenberg Traurig via Mondaq, Oct 26; Editions #019–#028)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • Three-quarters of investors wait longer for distributions — and 84% still plan to increase exposure. Three-quarters of private markets investors say the time required to receive distributions has increased over the past year, yet 84% still intend to increase their exposure over the next two years, according to new research from Brown Brothers Harriman. The liquidity complaint and the allocation intent now coexist, and managers who return capital are the ones getting the new money — see Advent, above. (AltAssets / Brown Brothers Harriman, Oct 29)
  • Blue Owl's institutional engine: ~70% of its last-12-month equity raise. Institutional and insurance investors drove about 70% of Blue Owl's last-12-month equity capital raise, committing roughly 20% more than a year earlier, per co-CEO Marc Lipschultz on the firm's October 29 earnings call. Assets under management rose 12% year over year to $330 billion as of September 30, with credit at $162.8 billion. (Pensions & Investments via AlphaMaven, Oct 29)

THE NUMBER

84% — the share of private markets investors who intend to increase their exposure over the next two years, even as three-quarters report longer waits for distributions (Brown Brothers Harriman). LPs are not leaving private markets. They are re-underwriting who gets their next dollar — and Advent's $30 billion is what the winners' circle looks like.

MEME CLEANSER

Advent raised $30 billion while everyone else complained about fundraising, Ares pulled in $8 billion before its fund even finishes, and three-quarters of investors say getting their money back takes longer than ever — yet 84% want in for more. Private markets on a Friday: the exit door is slow, the entry door is crowded, and the bouncer only knows a few names.

Edition #028

Disruptive's $10 billion bet: ten companies, no diversification, no apologies

Thursday, October 29, 2026

THE LEAD

$10 billion. That is what Dallas-based Disruptive raised for its first-ever blind-pool fund, announced Tuesday — a vehicle that will back roughly 10 late-stage companies with checks of up to $1 billion each, concentrated in AI, AI infrastructure, defense tech and enterprise software. The structural shift is the story: founded in 2012 by Alex Davis, Disruptive spent 14 years raising money deal-by-deal through special-purpose vehicles. Now LPs are committing capital without knowing which companies it will buy.

The trigger, Davis told Axios, was the SPV market itself — with giants like SpaceX heading toward public listings, he worried small investors who bought in through layers of intermediaries could find those intermediaries gone. The track record LPs bought into: Bloomberg reports Disruptive has returned more than $6 billion on roughly $1.9 billion invested, across names including Palantir, Airbnb, Spotify, Groq and ElevenLabs. The Dallas Business Journal calls it the largest venture fund ever raised in Texas. The concentration math is the whole thesis and the whole risk: ten positions, each near the cap, in the two hottest sectors on earth. Diversification is somebody else's strategy.

Sources: TechCrunch (Oct 27); AltAssets (Oct 28); Axios via Dealroom (Oct 28)

FUND CLOSES

The London-based industrial technology investor's third fund landed just below the $316 million raised for its predecessor. Industrial tech is raising steadily while consumer VC still waits for its turn. (AltAssets, Oct 28)
  • Polus Capital — ~$600M gathered so far for second special situations fund. The Mediobanca-owned alternative credit manager is reported to have raised about $600 million to date, having returned to market after deploying almost all of its €890 million predecessor. Treat the figure as reported, not confirmed by the manager. (AltAssets, Oct 29)

DEALS

  • KKR closes its first private credit continuation vehicle — $460M+, led by AllianzGI. The vehicle holds the assets of KKR Lending Partners III, a $1.5 billion direct lending fund that finished fundraising in 2017. Existing investors could take liquidity or roll on substantially the same terms; the fund posted an 11.0% net IRR and 1.7x MOIC as of June 30, 2026, per a KKR 10-Q cited by PitchBook. Evercore advised; Debevoise acted as counsel. (PitchBook / Debevoise, Oct 28)
  • PGIM and LGT co-lead a $680M Tree Line private credit continuation vehicle. The vehicle gives existing investors across several Tree Line direct lending funds a liquidity option while the firm keeps managing the portfolio — primarily senior secured, first-lien loans to more than 60 US lower-middle-market companies. PGIM said earlier this year it plans to deploy up to $1 billion into private credit secondaries over two years. (Alternative Credit Investor, Oct 28)
  • General Catalyst and JPMorganChase lead a $2.1B financing for Re:Build. The round for the US advanced manufacturing business, led by General Catalyst and JPMorganChase's Strategic Investment Group, is the week's reminder that industrial capacity is still a growth-equity story, not just a policy slogan. ALLOCATOR INTEL

The programme is designed with incentives for pension funds and other institutional investors to back domestic venture and growth capital — a government explicitly engineering the allocator bid rather than waiting for it. Retail private credit, in one filing, with the numbers to watch. (Kalkine Media, Oct 27)

THE NUMBER

More than $20 billion — credit secondaries volume in the first half of 2026 alone, already surpassing full-year 2025 volume, per Evercore data cited in the KKR coverage, with continuation vehicles making up most of it. Yesterday's two CV closes ($460 million-plus at KKR, $680 million at Tree Line) are not outliers. They are the market's new plumbing.

MEME CLEANSER

Disruptive raised $10 billion to buy ten companies, KKR and Tree Line raised $1.14 billion to let investors leave funds without selling anything, and the SEC announced it will examine all of it more closely next year. Private markets on a Thursday: concentration at the top, liquidity engineering in the middle, and a regulator taking notes at the door.

Advent's $30 billion says the exit drought is over — for the biggest names, at least

Wednesday, October 28, 2026

THE LEAD

$30 billion. That is what Advent raised across its latest fund complex, announced Tuesday — $27 billion for its 11th global buyout fund, GPE XI, the largest buyout fund closed so far in 2026, plus more than $3 billion for a new mid-market strategy launched in March. The raise lands against the exact headwind Reuters names: a prolonged exit slowdown that has starved investors of distributions and left them with less to commit to new funds.

Advent beat it anyway, with capital from pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, endowments, institutional managers, family offices and high-net-worth individuals. GPE XI will invest primarily across Europe and North America, expanding in Asia-Pacific and Latin America; the mid-market vehicle writes $100–350 million equity checks into North American and European companies with enterprise values up to $1.25 billion. The read-across is uncomfortable for everyone below the top tier: in a distribution drought, LPs are not pulling back from private markets — they are concentrating into the names they are most certain will return capital.

The final close came in 71% above the fund's $3.5 billion target and nearly twice the $3.25 billion raised for Fund VII in November 2023, with a significant share of capital from investors in Asia and the Middle East. Secondaries is no longer the sidecar — it is where the liquidity is. (PE Professional, Oct 27)

Private equity is heading for $12 trillion — and Monday's closes are already pricing it in

Tuesday, October 27, 2026

THE LEAD

$12 trillion. That is where global private equity assets under management are headed by the end of 2031, nearly double today's level, according to new Preqin research reported Monday — with private credit passing $5 trillion to become one of the fastest-growing major alternative asset classes. The forecast landed on a day that read like a preview: a venture fund-of-funds raising $920 million to buy into a concentrating market, an Asian private credit fund crossing $700 million toward a $1 billion target, and insurers pouring another $510 million into rated private credit feeders.

The through-line is concentration. Vintage's raise is explicitly a bet that venture's value now accrues to a handful of managers — three firms took nearly half of all venture capital raised in the first half of 2026, per the PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor it cites. If Preqin's $12 trillion is right, the next five years belong less to whoever finds the most deals and more to whoever can get into the rooms where the deals already are.

Sources: AltAssets (Oct 26), citing Preqin; CTech (Oct 21); Globes (Oct 22)

FUND CLOSES

  • Vintage Investment Partners — $920M across venture fund-of-funds and secondaries. The Israeli-founded firm raised $920 million in new capital, lifting assets under management above $4.8 billion. Per Globes, the split is $580 million for a new fund-of-funds and $340 million for a secondary fund buying stakes from existing investors and employees. It plans to back 30 venture funds — down from 35 in its prior vehicle and ~60 at peak — investing $7–30 million per fund, typically in funds under $1 billion. (CTech, Oct 21; Globes, Oct 22; AltAssets, Oct 26)
  • Ascertis Credit — past $700M for Fund IV, targeting $1B. The Singapore-based, India- and Southeast Asia-focused private credit manager (formerly BPEA Credit) passed $700 million in commitments at its second close for its fourth fund, up from a $520 million first close in November 2025 — one of the largest ever first closes for a private credit fund in India. Head Kanchan Jain told The Economic Times the fund targets mid-teens returns, has begun deploying, and aims for a portfolio of 35–40 companies. (Dealroom; The Economic Times via alpha-maven, Oct 26)
  • Bison Ventures — $160M for Fund II. The frontier-technology venture firm raised $160 million for its second fund, approximately 19% more than its debut vehicle, increasing exposure to early-stage frontier-tech companies. (AltAssets, Oct 26)
  • Partners Group — $875M via rated private credit feeders in 2026. The Swiss manager raised $875 million through two rated feeder vehicles for its evergreen private credit strategy this year, after a second closing that secured $510 million from US insurance companies. Insurers keep buying the wrapper, not just the fund. (AltAssets, Oct 26; ad-hoc-news, Oct 21)

DEALS

  • Truelink Capital signs to buy Johnson Controls' global intrusion business. The Los Angeles private equity firm entered a definitive agreement on October 26 to acquire Johnson Controls' Global Intrusion Products business — panels, sensors and detectors sold under brands including DSC, Qolsys, PowerG, Visonic and Bentel — which will operate as an independent standalone company. Financial terms were not disclosed; closing is expected in the first half of calendar 2027. (PR Newswire via Morningstar, Oct 26)
  • Surgery Partners goes private at $5.7B. Surgery Partners agreed to be taken private in an all-cash deal at $23.50 per share, an enterprise value of approximately $5.7 billion, by HealthSpan Partners, an affiliate of non-profit health system Bon Secours Mercy Health. The agreement was signed October 23 and announced October 26; Bain Capital, which holds roughly 39% of the stock, will retain a stake in the combined entity. Closing is expected in the first quarter of 2027. (Market Flux; Motley Fool, Oct 26)
  • Goldman Sachs Alternatives takes a majority stake in Celnor — reported at ~€1B. Goldman Sachs Alternatives' reported majority investment in the testing, inspection and compliance platform — which generates more than £300 million in annual revenue — was valued at approximately €1 billion, per M&A Daily's October 26 roundup. Treat the valuation as reported, not confirmed by the parties. (M&A Daily, Oct 26)
  • Tokenization watch: Soneium's IP Royalties Vault launches tomorrow. Soneium will launch its IP Royalties Vault on October 28, opening a $549 million intellectual-property royalty fund managed by Partners Group — 53 investments and more than 100,000 royalty streams — to retail investors via ipUSD and an IP Vault. Tokenized private-market income, not just tokenized Treasuries, is the next retailization test. (Wisevoter, Oct 27)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • ASIC halts a $573M mortgage fund — and warns the sector is "not where we need [it] to be." Australia's corporate regulator issued an interim stop order against the Murdoch Clarke Mortgage Fund, barring offers, issuance, sales or transfers of interests. The fund held A$573.2 million in net assets at June 30, 2026, mostly in interest-only loans secured by mortgages over Tasmanian land.   • Retailization countdown: 38 days to December 4, 41 to December 7. No new SEC rulemaking since the September 30 package — the comment deadlines stand at December 4 (interval fund modernization + accredited-investor pathways, including the proposed FINRA exam) and December 7 (performance fees in regulated funds). ALLOCATOR INTEL

Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada opened a $700 million fund-of-funds stream — the largest of three under the $1 billion Venture and Growth Capital Catalyst Initiative — courting pension funds and other institutions as limited partners. THE NUMBER

$12 trillion — Preqin's forecast for global private equity AUM by the end of 2031, nearly double today's level, with private credit passing $5 trillion. Monday's sweep — $920 million, $700 million-plus, $510 million from insurers alone — is not the forecast. It is the down payment.

MEME CLEANSER

Preqin says private equity doubles to $12 trillion, ASIC halted a $573 million fund for a disclosure statement that forgot to disclose things, and a $549 million royalty fund is about to become a retail token. Private markets on a Tuesday: the future is enormous, the paperwork is still catching up, and somewhere a pension fund is filling out a form to invest in songs.

Edition #025

LSEG pays $3.535B for the private-credit data underneath the AI trade

Monday, October 26, 2026

THE LEAD

The private-credit data layer just got a $3.535 billion price tag. London Stock Exchange Group agreed on October 25 to acquire credit intelligence provider Octus for $3.535 billion, expanding its footprint in private, illiquid, and distressed debt intelligence as financial firms wire AI into credit research. The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory approval.

Why it matters beyond the multiple: Octus turns hard-to-access credit records — loan covenants, deal terms — into searchable data, with 750 customers and more than 40,000 users, and is expected to generate more than $300 million in revenue this year. LSEG says the acquisition should be neutral to adjusted earnings per share in its first full year before turning accretive, and that 80% of the AI contracts it has signed involve buying additional datasets. Translation: the AI credit trade runs on covenants and terms, not just models — and the exchange just bought the filing cabinet.

Sources: SiliconReport (Oct 25–26), citing LSEG's announcement; ad-hoc-news (Oct 26)

FUND CLOSES

  • A quiet start to the week on new closes — no new private-markets fund close was announced in the last 24 hours in our sweep. The fresh capital story today is commitments, not closes (see Allocator Intel). For the week's tally — $19.9B across seven closes — see Edition #023.

DEALS

  • AVID tops Warburg Pincus for Ingenia with a $1.54B bid. AVID Property Group offered A$2.2 billion ($1.54 billion), or A$5.40 per share, for Australian land-lease operator Ingenia Communities — a 13.4% premium to Friday's close and above Warburg Pincus' latest A$5.25 offer after three rounds of bids. Ingenia's board granted AVID soft exclusivity of up to six weeks and terminated Warburg Pincus' data-room access. The bid hinges on Ingenia terminating its planned A$992.5 million acquisition of Peet. (Reuters via Devdiscourse/KLSE Screener, Oct 26)
  • Mutares fields an irrevocable offer for Stuart. Mutares received an irrevocable offer from Quiver to acquire Stuart (SRT Group), its urban and last-mile logistics platform, with closing expected in Q4 2026, subject to informing employee representatives. (EQS News via Sharewise/Finanzwire, Oct 26)

Edition #024

The Week in Alts: QIAGEN's €1.49 spread is the market's verdict on the EQT deal

Sunday, October 25, 2026

THE LEAD

The market is pricing EQT's QIAGEN deal at a discount — literally. QIAGEN traded at €45.01 in weekend trading on October 24, against EQT's agreed €46.50 per-share all-cash offer — a €1.49 spread, about 3.2% below the offer price. The gap is the market's working estimate of deal risk and time value between now and the expected mid-2027 close, not a verdict that the deal fails: QIAGEN's board and supervisory board have recommended acceptance, and the company reported preliminary third-quarter sales of $548 million ahead of full results on November 4.

Read it alongside the week's other EQT move — buying a US real estate manager outright (below) — and the picture is a manager deploying on two fronts at once: taking a listed diagnostics company private while buying distribution and real-estate capability in the US. The spread says traders want paying for the wait. The strategy says EQT doesn't mind waiting.

Sources: ad-hoc-news.de (Oct 24); Reuters (Oct 21, via Edition #021)

FUND CLOSES

  • A quiet weekend on new closes — yesterday's Week in Alts covered the week's closes, led by Blackstone's $8.7B energy-transition fund and Pantheon's $6B secondaries fund. The fresh money story right now is what happens after the close. (See Edition #023)
  • Partners Group's next test is a November fee vote. After banking $510M from US insurers for its private-credit feeder (2026 rated-feeder fundraising now $875M), Partners Group faces a November vote on a fee overhaul for its Global Value SICAV — the same vehicle management proposed splitting into compounding and distributing sub-portfolios. Raising capital and repricing it are two different votes. (ad-hoc-news, Oct 24; Edition #022)

DEALS

  • EQT to buy Stockbridge for $550M. EQT agreed to acquire the US real estate investment manager for $550 million all cash — $375 million upfront plus $175 million contingent — gaining roughly $16.5 billion in fee-generating assets under management, about 185 professionals across six US offices, and roughly 180 institutional clients. Stockbridge, founded in 2003 and headquartered in San Francisco, brings specialist depth in industrial outdoor storage and alternative living, including manufactured housing. The combination is pitched as broadening EQT Real Estate into a more thematically diversified global platform. (EQT announcement, Oct 15, via Caproasia, Oct 24)
  • Bregal Milestone takes a majority stake in Italy's Nethive, backs JoyComm buyout. Terms were not disclosed. Nethive, founded in 2014, serves more than 250 clients across telecoms, energy, healthcare, and public administration with managed security services and carrier-grade software; revenue reached about €25 million in 2026, a roughly 30% compound annual growth rate since 2020, with headcount rising from about 20 to more than 80 pro forma including JoyComm. The bet: Italian cybersecurity procurement — Consip tenders worth a combined €1.255 billion in 2026 — keeps consolidating around scaled providers. (Dealroom, October 2026)
  • W1M sale advances to a second round. The UK wealth manager — £28.7 billion in assets under management, about 440 employees, formed in 2024 from the merger of Waverton Investment Management and London and Capital — is reported to be in a second round of sale talks, with bidders including Bain Capital, Bridgepoint, and Aquiline. No deal yet; the process itself is the signal that private equity still wants wealth-management distribution. (Caproasia, Oct 24)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • SIFMA wants issuer consent, not opt-out, for tokenized shares. The securities industry group asked the SEC on October 19 to require affirmative issuer consent rather than an opt-out for tokenized versions of shares, and to give companies a universal way to exclude their shares from every tokenization venue. This is the industry association joining the issuer pushback already seen from CoreWeave and Broadcom against tokenized versions of their shares — the tokenization sandbox's consent question is now a lobbying fight, not just a legal memo. (SIFMA via BlockchainReporter, October 2026; The Brief, Oct 16; Edition #018)
  • Retailization countdown: still December 4 and December 7. No new rulemaking since the SEC's September 30 package — comment deadlines stand at December 4 (interval fund modernization + accredited-investor pathways, including the proposed FINRA exam) and December 7 (performance fees in regulated funds). Six weeks out, the comment file is where the retail-access fight will be won. (Stradley Ronon, Oct 13; Intelligize, Oct 7; Editions #019–#023)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • A Texas pension weighs handing staff the pen. A Texas public pension system (unnamed in the report) is considering delegating some private-markets investment discretion to its staff, rather than requiring every commitment to pass through board-level approval — alongside a potential private-equity pacing plan of $80–$90 million per year to reach an 11% private-equity target. Governance reform is allocator intel too: faster decisions are how smaller systems keep pace when mega-funds close in weeks, not months. (Financial Investment News via AlphaMaven, October 2026)
  • CalPERS, revisited. The week's biggest allocator story — the ~$640B pension cutting directly held Magnificent Seven shares by 26% in H1 2026 for private-equity diversification — remains the frame for every pacing decision above. No new CalPERS disclosure this weekend; the trade stands. (Financial Times, Oct 23; Edition #023)

THE NUMBER

$38.95B — tokenized real-world assets excluding stablecoins, per rwa.xyz as of October 21, with about 5.27 million holders — nearly double the roughly $20 billion recorded at the end of 2025. Estimates vary by definition: the IMF put public tokenized RWAs at about $65 billion as of July 31 using a broader measure. Either way, the direction is the same — tokenization's asset count is compounding while its liquidity and consent rules are still being negotiated.

MEME CLEANSER

QIAGEN trades €1.49 below EQT's offer, Partners Group's investors get to vote on the fees for the fund they just watched get restructured, and SIFMA wants a universal "no" button for tokenized shares. Private markets spent the week raising record funds and then immediately negotiating the terms of its own success. The toll booth is open — and now everyone wants to renegotiate the toll.

Edition #023

The Week in Alts: The barbell market closed $19.9B in a week

Saturday, October 24, 2026

THE LEAD

Private markets didn't have a slow week — it had a barbell week. Add up the closes reported across this week's editions and you get roughly $19.9B: Blackstone's $8.7B energy-transition fund, Pantheon's $6B secondaries fund, Ara's $2.5B energy fund, Northwind's $1B real-estate credit fund, and a string of oversubscribed mid-market closes — all at or above target, several at hard caps. At the same time, the country's largest public pension told the Financial Times it is trading concentrated Big Tech exposure for private equity, cutting its directly held Magnificent Seven shares by 26% in the first half of 2026.

The two stories are one story. Fundraising is polarizing — mega-managers and proven mid-market names close oversubscribed, while US private-equity fundraising overall still tracks roughly 27% below the 2023 peak (PitchBook via WSJ, Oct 13). The money hasn't left private markets. It has concentrated in the names allocators already trust, and in the strategies — secondaries, credit, energy transition — that solve the exit and income problems first.

Sources: Editions #018–#022; Financial Times via Bloomberg Law/TradingView (Oct 23)

FUND CLOSES

  • Blackstone Energy Transition Partners V — final close at $8.7B, at hard cap and more than 50% larger than its predecessor, with a stated 34% net IRR since 1997 (as of June 30, 2026). Three platforms funded and three acquisitions already done since the investment period began in June. (Blackstone press release, Oct 19; Edition #020)
  • Pantheon Global Secondaries Fund VIII — final close at $6B, 71% above its $3.5B target, Pantheon's largest-ever secondaries fundraise, with significant new commitments from Asia and the Middle East. Follows ~$3.35B of secondaries deployment across 17 deals in 2025. (GlobeNewswire, Oct 21; Edition #021)
  • Ara Partners — closed inaugural Ara Energy Fund I at its $2.5B hard cap ($1.8B fund commitments plus >$700M co-investment), ten months after launch. (PRNewswire, Oct 20; Edition #020)
  • Northwind Group — final close of Northwind Debt Fund III at $1B, double its $500M target and the largest fund in firm history; senior-secured bridge loans across US gateway markets. (Connect CRE, Oct 22; Edition #022)
  • Monomoy Capital Partners — closed inaugural Monomoy Family Fund I at $589M against a $500M external hard cap, three months after launch, with the GP and affiliates committing $89M (~15%); fully allocated within two weeks, all LPs existing relationships. (Business Wire/VCA Online, Oct 20; Edition #020)
  • Partners Group — closed its second rated feeder of the year for its private-credit evergreen strategy at $510M, all from US insurers, bringing 2026 rated-feeder fundraising to $875M. (ad-hoc-news via Bloomberg, Oct 23; Edition #022)
  • Apogem Capital — closed Apogem Private Equity Fund XI at its $580M hard cap plus $17M employee capital for $597M total, anchored by parent New York Life. (PE Professional; Edition #019)
  • Corre Partners — $220M first close for Horizon III opportunistic credit against a $500M target ($650M hard cap); predecessor Horizon II at 24.7% net IRR / 2.4x MOIC. (Morningstar/Business Wire, Oct 8; Edition #019)
  • Janus Henderson — second close of its Shariah-compliant MENA Private Credit Fund IV at $191M toward a $300M target, backed by Saudi and UAE institutions and family offices. (ent.news, Oct 11; Edition #019)

DEALS

  • EQT takes QIAGEN private for $10.8B. EQT agreed to acquire the Dutch molecular-testing company at €46.50 per share — an 8.6% premium — with Abu Dhabi Investment Authority and Canada Pension Plan Investment Board affiliates participating; enterprise value ~$12.1B including ~$1.1B net debt; expected close mid-2027. (Reuters, Oct 21; Edition #021)
  • The $12.4B AI-anxiety buyout day. Thoma Bravo-owned Proofpoint agreed to acquire Varonis Systems for $6.3B ($49.75/share, 52% premium to the June 22 pre-rumor close); GTCR-led consortium agreed to take CCC Intelligent Solutions private for $6.1B ($7.75/share, 44% premium to the unaffected July 9 close). Both targets vertical software bought off AI-disruption discounts. (Proofpoint/Reuters; Insurance Journal, Oct 15–16; Edition #018)
  • EMK Capital — closed a €1.25B single-asset continuation fund for European technical installation group VDK Groep, at hard cap, co-led by LGT Capital Partners. (AltAssets, Oct 19; Edition #020)
  • GenNx360 × Whitsons Culinary Group — closed a $285M single-asset continuation fund led by Churchill, extending an ownership in which the company has tripled in size. (AltAssets, Oct 15; Edition #018)
  • Victory Park Capital + Tradable launch VALTS. The Janus Henderson affiliate and tokenization platform Tradable launched the AltsABF Tokenized Feeder Fund, giving eligible investors on-chain access to VPC's asset-backed credit interval fund — initially on Stellar. Tradable says it has tokenized $3B in private credit to date. (Secured Finance Network, Oct 20; Edition #022)
  • Nasdaq Private Market completed its acquisition of Nasdaq Fund Secondaries, merging direct company shares and secondary fund stakes onto one platform. Jefferies' 2025 review put global secondary volume at ~$233B in 2025, up 53%. (GlobeNewswire, Oct 15; Edition #020)

REGULATION & POLICY

  • Retailization countdown: six weeks and shrinking. No new rulemaking this week since the SEC's September 30 package — but the comment deadlines still stand: December 4 (interval fund modernization + accredited-investor pathways, including the proposed ~$100 FINRA exam valid for 10 years) and December 7 (performance fees in regulated funds). (Stradley Ronon, Oct 13; Intelligize, Oct 7; Editions #019–#022)
  • Form PF wants a private credit line of its own. The SEC and CFTC's proposed streamlined Form PF amendments include a request for comment on adding a dedicated private credit reporting section — the current glossary doesn't even define "private credit." (Chapman, JD Supra, October; Edition #019)
  • Issuers push back on the tokenization sandbox. CoreWeave and Broadcom are challenging tokenized versions of their shares, testing the SEC's five-year Innovation Exemption for Tokenized Securities Venues — including the issuer opt-out and unresolved mechanics of shareholder lists, dividends, splits, and voting. (The Brief, Oct 16; Edition #018)

ALLOCATOR INTEL

  • CalPERS trades the Magnificent Seven for private equity. The ~$640B pension cut the combined number of Magnificent Seven shares it directly held by 26% in H1 2026, trading "large exposure to seven companies for several hundred firms to diversify equity risk," deputy CIO Anton Orlich told the FT. The catch, noted in the same coverage: the S&P 500 returned ~65% over the three years to March 2026 versus ~24% for a Cambridge Associates US private equity index. (Financial Times, Oct 23; Edition #022)
  • Connecticut Retirement Plans and Trust Funds — $1.1B in new private-market commitments, spanning ICG credit, Stonepeak infrastructure, Homestead farmland, ICG Europe mid-market plus a €150M co-invest program, and Penzance real estate. (Dakota, Oct 22; Edition #022)
  • Virginia Retirement System — committed $300M to Castlelake Asset-Based Private Credit IV; predecessor closed at $2.23B last August. (IPE Real Assets, Oct 21; Edition #022)
  • New York State Common Retirement Fund — roughly $565M to US real estate in August, per October comptroller disclosures, including $300M to Artemis Real Estate Partners Fund V. (Connect Money, Oct 19; Edition #019)
  • State of Michigan Retirement System — $765M across seven alternatives funds while rebalancing, even as it signals slower future commitment pacing. (AlternativesWatch via AlphaMaven, Oct 15; Edition #020)
  • The Mansion House scorecard. One year after 17 UK pension providers pledged at least 10% of DC assets to global private markets by 2030, schemes hold £11.1bn in UK unlisted assets — 6.6% of DC growth-phase assets in global private markets, 3.3% in the UK — with £8bn in real estate and just £500mn in domestic private equity. UK Private Capital's Michael Moore: "At the current rate of progress, the Compact target will be missed by a decade or more." (FT, Oct 20; Edition #021)
  • Milwaukee City Employees' Retirement System ($6.7B) is weighing a full 2027 private-equity pause after zero new PE commitments in 2026, per consultant Callan. (Markets Group, Oct 13; Edition #020)

THE NUMBER

$19.9B — the sum of the closes reported across this week's editions (Blackstone $8.7B + Pantheon $6B + Ara $2.5B + Northwind $1B + Monomoy $0.589B + Partners Group $0.51B + Apogem $0.597B, rounded). In a year when overall US buyout fundraising still trails the 2023 peak by ~27%, a single week produced nearly $20B of closes — all at or above target. The barbell is the market.

Private equity spent 2026 telling LPs the IPO window was closed, the M&A window was closed, and exits were "deferred." This week, Pantheon raised $6 billion to buy those deferred exits, Blackstone raised $8.7 billion for the energy transition, and CalPERS decided seven tech stocks were too much concentration. The exit window isn't closed. It's just charging a toll — and this week, the toll booth did record business.
