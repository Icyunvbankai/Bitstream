Ten under-the-radar ways people make money with AI.

Not trading bots, not agencies, not SaaS, not prompt packs. A ranked hunt for the income plays most “make money with AI” coverage never mentions — with real numbers, real sources, and skepticism flags where the proof runs thin.

01

Sell curated datasets to niche teams

READ IN FULL

Skip the SaaS dashboard. Package clean, labeled datasets — public transaction data, open-banking logs, government bulletins — as CSV/Parquet/SQLite and sell download access to teams that need them for modeling. One developer tried selling a dashboard first; nobody bought. Selling the raw data got purchase orders.

$1,200 revenue in the first 30 days on 12 hours of work and $37 in costs — roughly $100/hour. Each new dataset takes ~2 hours to package and sells for $800–$1,500. Example sale: 50,000 anonymized M-Pesa transactions to two Kenyan neobanks.
STARTUP
Near-zero: a domain plus object storage, ~$37/month. Medium difficulty — needs data-wrangling skills.
CATCH
Licensing is messy: one buyer redistributed the file across their whole engineering team. Fix it with single-company, single-region licenses and watermarked files. Regulatory exposure if data isn’t properly anonymized.
Sources: kubaik’s itemized 6-month income-stream test (read in full) · data marketplaces overview
02

Rent out your spare GPU compute

SEARCH-INDEX

Install hosting software from Vast.ai, Salad, or Clore.ai and rent your gaming GPU to AI developers while it idles. Same hardware, wildly different economics than crypto mining.

A September 2026 comparison on identical hardware: an RTX 4090 nets $141–$314/month after electricity on rental platforms, versus $24–$36/month mining — rental is 5–10x better. Payback on a $1,600 card: ~7 months vs ~53 months for mining. Platforms take 15–25%.
STARTUP
$0 if you already own a supported GPU. Easy setup: daemon plus pricing.
CATCH
Earnings depend on uptime/reliability scores and demand; electricity costs; 24/7 load wears hardware. Figures vary widely by card ($150–$800/month per 4090 across sources) — treat as a range, not a guarantee.
Sources: GPU rental vs mining returns, Sept 2026 · Vast.ai figures via Eneba
03

License your AI voice on ElevenLabs

READ IN FULL

Record about two hours of your voice, publish a Professional Voice Clone to ElevenLabs’ marketplace, and earn usage-based royalties ($0.03–$0.20 per 1,000 characters customers generate).

ElevenLabs’ May 2026 update: over $22M paid to creators (double November 2025), across 10,400+ creators in 32 languages. Top-end contributors reportedly earned over $10K/month — explicitly a top-end figure, not an average. No median published; one anecdotal account puts most creators at $80–$320/month.
STARTUP
Creator plan $22/month plus a mic and a quiet room. Easy. Payouts via Stripe Connect, $10 minimum, automatic every 6–8 days.
CATCH
Brutal power law: a listing with no paid use earns $0, and free-tier use pays nothing. 10,400+ competing creators means a discoverability problem. You license your voice for uses you can’t fully pre-approve.
Sources: ElevenLabs $22M payout update, May 2026 (read in full) · ElevenLabs earning guide
04

“Human data” gigs for AI training

READ IN FULL

AI labs are out of human data. Two tiers: entry-level — film daily-life video or record calls for robotics and vision datasets via Micro1, Neon Mobile, or gig apps; expert tier — write prompts, evals, and simulations for frontier labs via Handshake AI, Mercor, and similar platforms.

Entry: $15/hour filming household movement (Micro1), $0.50/minute for phone-call recordings, $80 for 2 hours filming dishes. Expert: OpenAI hired 100+ ex-bankers at $150/hour to build financial models; Handshake AI contractors write professional task simulations at $50/hour general, up to $500/hour for domain experts. Macro demand is real: Micro1 hit a $500M gross run rate in August 2026.
STARTUP
$0 — a phone or laptop. Easy entry; the expert tier is competitive.
CATCH
Contributors often grant irrevocable licenses — your voice and movement data reused indefinitely, with workers reporting they don’t know how data is stored or shared. Task flow is unstable; projects pause without notice.
Sources: PYMNTS / MIT Technology Review: your daily life is now a data product (read in full) · TechCrunch: Micro1’s $500M run rate
05

AI-assisted bug bounties & model red-teaming

UNVERIFIED

Use AI to map APIs at scale and hunt access-control flaws, or red-team AI models for prompt injection, jailbreaks, and data leakage — Google’s AI vulnerability program pays up to $30,000 per AI security bug.

⚠ HEADLINE UNCONFIRMED: researcher “Brutecat” claims an AI-assisted pipeline collected over $500K in Google bounty payouts in under 3 months — self-reported, Google has not confirmed. Verified: Google paid $17.1M across vulnerability programs in 2025. Honest baseline: a beginner’s 90-day log shows 145 hours, 14 reports, 3 paid, $1,050 total (~$7/hour). Industry skew: 35–45% of active hunters earn $0/year.
STARTUP
$0. Hard — needs real security and API skills; AI amplifies expertise but doesn’t replace it.
CATCH
Extreme skew (most earn nothing); duplicates kill payouts on crowded programs; AI-generated reports need manual validation or they’re rejected as noise.
Sources: TechRepublic: the AI bug-bounty pipeline (read in full) · 90-day beginner income log
06

Prediction-market market making

UNVERIFIED

Not directional betting — providing liquidity with limit orders on both sides and earning the spread plus maker rebates. Polymarket introduced taker fees in 2026 specifically to fund maker rebates.

An on-chain study of 2.5M wallets: 84.1% of traders are unprofitable; only 2% ever cleared $1,000 lifetime. But skilled winners and market makers capture 30%+ of all profits. ⚠ One guru source claims bots earn 1–3% monthly — unverified guru math; the mechanism is sound, the return figure is not.
STARTUP
Needs a trading float plus a bot (coding). Medium difficulty, real capital risk.
CATCH
Directional trading is a documented loser’s game; market makers get run over when real news moves prices; regulatory shifts around Polymarket’s US rollout and Kalshi competition.
Sources: How people make money on Polymarket · Polymarket bot strategies (guru-flavored)
07

AI dubbing & subtitle localization for creators

SEARCH-INDEX

Use Whisper plus DeepL or ElevenLabs to translate and dub creators’ videos, or sell human QC on top of YouTube’s auto-dubbing. The buyer economics are solid — which is why creators pay.

A creator earning $5,000/month from 1M English views can add $800–$2,500/month by dubbing their top 20 videos into Hindi and Portuguese; multilingual audiences command 20–40% higher sponsorship CPMs. ⚠ Supply-side rate claims ($15–$40 per video, “$1,200–$1,800/month”) are guru-sourced with no verified dashboards.
STARTUP
Under $10/month in tools. Technically easy; the hard part is client acquisition.
CATCH
YouTube’s 2026 auto-dubbing (27 languages, all users) is commoditizing the low end. The durable niche is human-in-the-loop quality control, not raw machine translation.
Sources: Dubbing as a creator growth lever · Subtitle translation income claims (guru tone)
08

Niche AI newsletters & directories

SEARCH-INDEX

Hyper-narrow AI-assisted newsletters (research with Perplexity, drafts with Claude, distribution on Beehiiv) monetized via direct sponsorships — or niche AI-tool directories with featured listings and affiliate links.

Platform data via secondary sources: niche newsletters with ~500 subscribers regularly earn $500–$1,200/month from direct sponsorships (~$0.50/subscriber/month rule of thumb). ⚠ A viral “$10K/month AI newsletter in 14 days” post has internally inconsistent subscriber numbers — not credible, flagged as guru marketing.
STARTUP
Under $30/month in tools. Technically easy; distribution is the hard part — the first 100 subscribers take 4–8 weeks of community posting.
CATCH
Month one typically earns $0; AI-generated newsletters face spam-filter and trust issues; directories are SEO-dependent and hyper-competitive.
Sources: The AI niche newsletter model · Newsletter business playbook
09

AI-assisted resale flipping

SEARCH-INDEX

Build an eBay valuation bot: vision models identify brands in thrift stores, LLMs write SEO listings and estimate sold comps. Flip cameras, lenses, graded trading cards, and mispriced lots — the edge is “bad-listing arbitrage,” finding sellers who hide identity signals like model suffixes and certificates.

One solo dev’s published research notes report card-flipping margins of 10–40%+ net after fees and shipping, and 40–100% on graded flips and mispriced lots. No verified revenue — build-in-public only.
STARTUP
A laptop plus a small buying float. Easy to moderate (some coding helps).
CATCH
eBay fees (~13%+) eat margins; low-end cards need volume; eBay gates solo API access. As the researcher puts it: success comes from sharp buying more than tech — AI doesn’t replace sourcing skill.
Sources: eBay valuation bot research notes · Making money on eBay
10

Agent-to-agent commerce (x402)

READ IN FULL

Honest answer: not a proven individual income stream yet. The emerging rail where AI agents pay each other micropayments per API call — e.g., your research agent behind a pay-per-query endpoint. The rails are real; the income isn’t.

x402 processed over 230M transactions / $54M volume in about a year. But the average payment is ~$0.04–$0.14, and a September 2026 payments-research report screened $52.7M of x402 commerce and found only 0.6–7.5% was verifiably agents-paying-agents. Verdict: watch-list for 2027, not a 2026 income play.
STARTUP
Low — wrap a FastAPI agent in x402 middleware. High experimentation value.
CATCH
No documented case of an individual earning meaningful income selling agent services over x402. Ticket sizes are cents; mostly hype-cycle infrastructure right now.
Sources: AI agent payments on Base · The missing half of agent commerce
