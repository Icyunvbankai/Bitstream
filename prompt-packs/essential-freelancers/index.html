How to use this pack (60 seconds)

  1. Open a free AI tool — ChatGPT (chatgpt.com), Claude (claude.ai), or Gemini (gemini.google.com). No account tricks, no paid plan needed.
  2. Copy any prompt and replace the [BRACKETED] parts with your details.
  3. Be specific. Your service, your niche, your rates — specifics in, quality out. "Write me a proposal" gets a generic doc. "Write a proposal for a 5-page WordPress site for a dental office, $2,500 budget, 3-week timeline" gets a proposal you can send today.
  4. Treat the first answer as a draft. Reply with "make it shorter," "more confident," or "add a deadline" until it's right.
  5. AI drafts, you decide. AI can write contracts, tax checklists, and invoices — but have an accountant or attorney check anything that affects your money or legal risk.
Rule 1 — Niche beats broad. "Freelance writer" competes with everyone. "Email copywriter for dental practices" gets hired. Every prompt in this pack works better when you name your lane.
Rule 2 — Money prompts need a human backstop. Prompts on taxes, contracts, and insurance are organizers and starting points — verify everything with your accountant or attorney before you act on it.
Rule 3 — Clients buy outcomes, not effort. Say what the client gets ("a website that books appointments"), not what you do ("I build websites"). The AI can't know your wins — feed it your numbers.

1 · Finding Clients & Niching Down

Stop competing with everyone on everything. Pick a lane, say it clearly, and show up where your clients already are.

PROMPT 01

Pick your niche in one session

I do [freelance web design] for anyone who asks. Ask me 8 questions — one at a time — about my favorite past projects, who paid best, and what I enjoy doing. Then recommend 3 specific niches I could own, with the pros and cons of each.
PROMPT 02

One-line service description

Write 5 one-line descriptions of my service. I do [social media management] for [local restaurants], and the result clients get is [a full month of content without lifting a finger]. Each one under 15 words. Make them sound like what I'd say out loud.
PROMPT 03

Find where your clients hang out

My ideal clients are [small business owners who need bookkeeping help]. Tell me exactly where they spend time online and offline: which platforms, which groups, which events, which podcasts. Rank them by where I'm most likely to actually reach them.
PROMPT 04

Portfolio case study builder

Turn this past project into a client-focused case study: [describe the project, the client's problem, what you did, and the result]. Format it as: the problem, what I did, the result with numbers. Keep it under 200 words and make the client the hero, not me.
PROMPT 05

Testimonial request that gets answers

Write a short message I can send to [a recent happy client] asking for a testimonial. Make it easy for them: include 3 fill-in-the-blank questions they can answer in 2 minutes. Warm, grateful, zero pressure.
PROMPT 06

Build a referral system

I do [freelance copywriting] and want more referrals. Design a simple system: when to ask, exactly what to say, and a small thank-you reward that doesn't feel like a bribe. I want this to run on [email].
PROMPT 07

Audit my online presence

Here's what a client sees when they Google me: [paste your profile, website text, or portfolio description]. I want clients who need [branding for startups]. Tell me what's working, what's confusing, and give me a rewritten version that sells the outcome.
PROMPT 08

Content ideas that attract clients

Give me 10 post ideas for [LinkedIn] that would attract [course creators who need video editing]. Each one: a hook line and a one-sentence summary. No generic advice posts — each should prove I know their world.
PROMPT 09

Rewrite portfolio titles to sell outcomes

Here are my current portfolio project titles: [list them]. Rewrite each one to say what the client GOT, not what I made — e.g. "landing page that tripled demo bookings" instead of "landing page design." I serve [coaches and consultants].
PROMPT 10

Competitor teardown

I'm a [freelance virtual assistant] targeting [real estate agents]. Look at how competitors describe their services: [paste 2-3 competitor blurbs]. Tell me what they're all saying, where the gap is, and how I can position myself differently.
PROMPT 11

Pick your one flagship offer

I currently offer [list your services] and it's too many. Ask me what I want to be known for, which work pays best, and which clients are easiest — then recommend ONE flagship offer with a name, a price range, and exactly who it's for.
PROMPT 12

Ideal client profile

Build a detailed profile of my ideal client. I do [bookkeeping] for [service businesses]. Include: who they are, what keeps them up at night, where they look for help, what they've tried before, and what would make them hire me on the spot.
PROMPT 13

Weekly client-getting routine

Build me a weekly routine for finding freelance clients. I have [5 hours a week] for marketing. I do [logo design] for [food brands]. Give me a day-by-day plan with specific actions — not "network more" fluff.

PROMPT 23

PROMPT 24

3 · Pricing & Packages

Undercharging is the freelancer's disease. Do the math, name your number, and say it like you mean it.

PROMPT 26

Hourly to project pricing math

I charge [$75/hour] and a typical [logo design] project takes me [12 hours]. Walk me through converting to project pricing: what to charge, how much buffer to add, and how to present it so the client doesn't ask for the hourly breakdown.
PROMPT 27

Build 3 pricing tiers

Build 3 pricing tiers for my [social media management] service: Starter, Growth, and Premium. For each: what's included, the monthly price, and who it's for. Make the middle tier the obvious best choice. My costs are about [$X per month].
PROMPT 28

Rate increase announcement

I'm raising my rate from [$60/hour] to [$85/hour] starting [next month]. Write a short email to existing clients announcing it. Confident, appreciative, no long explanations. Include one line about what's included at the new rate.
PROMPT 29

Minimum project fee calculator

Help me set a minimum project fee. My monthly expenses are [$3,500], I can handle about [4 projects] per month, and small projects eat [30%] of my admin time. Do the math and recommend a minimum — plus how to say it when someone asks for less.
PROMPT 30

Retainer pricing builder

I do [ongoing blog writing] and want to offer a monthly retainer. My project rate is [$400 per post]. Design 2 retainer options: number of posts, price, rollover policy, and what happens when they need extra. Make it simple enough to explain in one paragraph.
PROMPT 31

Discount script without desperation

A good client asked for a discount on [a $1,500 project] and I want to say yes — once. Write a reply that: agrees to [10%] off, ties it to something specific [fast decision / upfront payment], and makes clear this isn't my normal pricing. No groveling.
PROMPT 32

Price my day rate

I want to offer a day rate for [on-site video shoots]. My target annual income is [$90,000], I can realistically bill [3 days] per week, and [48 weeks] per year. Calculate my day rate and give me the script to quote it confidently.
PROMPT 33

What's my real break-even rate

Calculate my true break-even hourly rate. Monthly costs: [rent, software, insurance, taxes — list amounts]. I can bill about [25 hours] per week (the rest is admin). Show the math step by step so I can redo it when costs change.
PROMPT 34

Rush fee policy

Clients keep asking for "urgent" work. Write a rush fee policy for my [design] business: what counts as rush, the fee structure [25% under 5 days / 50% under 48 hours], and the exact sentence to use when quoting it so it sounds standard, not personal.
PROMPT 35

Per-project vs. hourly for this job

A client wants [a 10-page website] and asked whether I charge hourly or per project. My hourly rate is [$80] and I estimate [40 hours]. Tell me which is better for ME on this job and why, and give me the exact wording to propose it.
PROMPT 36

Raise rates for existing clients

Write an email to my long-term clients raising my [monthly retainer] from [$1,000] to [$1,250] effective [January]. Thank them genuinely, mention the new value I've added this year [faster turnaround, new skills], and give them one easy way to lock the old rate briefly.
PROMPT 37

Name your packages so clients understand them

I offer [3 website packages at $1,500 / $3,000 / $5,500] but clients always ask "what's the difference?" Rename the packages with clear names and write a one-sentence description of each that a non-technical business owner instantly understands. No jargon.

4 · Contracts & Scope

The paperwork that protects your pay and your sanity. AI can draft the words — an attorney checks the shield.

PROMPT 38

Simple scope statement

Write a plain-English scope of work for [a brand identity project: logo, color palette, typography, 2 revision rounds, $2,200]. Cover: exactly what's included, what counts as a revision, the timeline, and what's NOT included. No legal jargon — just clear.
PROMPT 39

Contract checklist before signing

I'm about to sign a freelance contract for [a 3-month content writing engagement]. Give me a checklist of what to look for: payment terms, scope, IP, termination, and red flags. This is an organizer to review with my attorney — not legal advice — so flag anything that needs a professional's eyes.
PROMPT 40

Kill fee clause in plain English

Draft a plain-English kill fee clause for my contracts: if a client cancels after work starts, I keep the [50%] deposit and they pay for completed milestones. Write it in simple language I can paste into my agreement, and note what to ask my attorney about it.
PROMPT 41

Revision limit policy

Write a revision policy I can put in proposals: [2 rounds included, extras at $X/hour]. Define what a "round" actually means, what happens when feedback arrives late, and the sentence to use when a client asks for round 4.
PROMPT 42

IP handoff terms in plain English

Explain intellectual property transfer for freelancers in plain English: when does the client own the work, what about my portfolio rights, and what should I put in writing? Give me 5 bullet points to discuss with my attorney — this is a starting checklist, not legal advice.
PROMPT 43

Scope creep response script

My client keeps adding "just one more thing" to a [website] project that was supposed to be [5 pages]. Write a firm but friendly email that: names the extra requests, explains they're outside the agreed scope, and offers to add them for [$X]. Protect the relationship and the boundary.
PROMPT 44

Late feedback clause idea

Clients sit on drafts for weeks, then demand rush delivery. Draft a contract line covering late client feedback: if feedback takes more than [5 business days], the timeline extends by the same number of days. Plain English, one paragraph, with a note on what to verify with my attorney.
PROMPT 45

Termination clause wording review

Here's the termination clause in a client's contract: [paste it]. Explain in plain English what it actually lets them do, what it lets me do, and what looks unfair. This is a first-pass review — I'll have my attorney confirm before signing.
PROMPT 46

Non-compete red flags for freelancers

A client wants me to sign a non-compete for a [6-month marketing contract]. List the red flags to watch for: duration, geography, scope of work covered, and what would make it reasonable vs. a career-killer. Flag this as something to review with an attorney — not a legal conclusion.
PROMPT 47

Turn a handshake deal into a written agreement

I agreed to [manage social media for a local gym, $800/month, starting next week] over coffee — nothing in writing. Draft a simple one-page agreement covering: services, pay, payment date, term, and how either of us can end it. I'll have an attorney review it before we both sign.
PROMPT 48

Payment terms clause builder

Build a payment terms section for my contracts: [50% deposit, 50% on delivery], payment due within [14 days] of invoice, and a [1.5% monthly] late fee. Plain English, ready to paste, with a reminder to confirm the late-fee part with my attorney.
PROMPT 49

Ownership of source files policy

Write a source-file policy for my [graphic design] contracts: final deliverables included, working files available for an extra [$X] fee. Explain the reasoning in one sentence a client will accept, and note what to check with my attorney about IP transfer.
PROMPT 50

NDA review checklist

A client sent me an NDA to sign before we talk about their project. Here's the text: [paste it]. Give me a plain-English checklist review: what I'm agreeing to, how long it lasts, and anything unusual. This is a first pass — I'll get my attorney's sign-off on anything binding.
