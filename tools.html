FROM EVERYDAY AI

The free everyday wins — no coding, no cost.

EVERYDAY AI

ChatGPT

The free chatbot at chatgpt.com — open it in your browser and just talk to it.

  1. Go to chatgpt.com and create a free account.
  2. Describe what you want in plain words — e.g. “suggest a weeknight dinner with chicken, rice, and whatever vegetables I have.”
  3. Add specifics: budget, constraints, who's it's for. Specificity is the whole trick.
  4. Treat the first answer as a draft — ask it to revise, shorten, or try a different angle.
Open ChatGPT →
EVERYDAY AI

Claude

Anthropic's free chatbot — strong at writing, explaining, and careful reasoning.

  1. Go to claude.ai and sign up free.
  2. Paste in a real task: an email to rewrite, a resume bullet to sharpen, a concept to explain.
  3. Ask it to quiz you or explain the same thing two ways (simple, then advanced).
  4. Pick the best parts of its draft and keep your own voice.
Open Claude →
EVERYDAY AI

Gemini

Google's free chatbot — handy if you already live in Gmail, Docs, and Drive.

  1. Go to gemini.google.com and sign in with your Google account.
  2. Try the voice mode: talk while walking or driving and let it turn the ramble into clean notes.
  3. Use it for trip planning with a hard budget — it flags the pricey parts and what to book first.
  4. Compare its answer against ChatGPT or Claude on important decisions.

Perplexity

The answer engine that cites its sources, so you can verify everything yourself.

  1. Go to perplexity.ai and ask a factual question.
  2. Click the numbered citations and read the actual sources.
  3. Use it for price comparisons and product research where proof matters.
  4. Free tier is enough for everyday use.

Grammarly

Quietly fixes your writing everywhere you type — used by hundreds of millions.

  1. Go to grammarly.com and install the free browser extension.
  2. Write normally — it underlines fixes as you go.
  3. Click a suggestion to accept it; ignore the ones that change your meaning.
  4. Free plan covers the everyday fixes.

FROM FREE AI TOOLS · SEP 24

Four free tools you missed.

Fresh launches, all free, all doing something real.

FREE AI TOOLS · SEP 24

TurboApply

Free ATS resume checker plus a browser extension that scores your fit for a job right on the listing page. Launched Sep 22.

  1. Upload your resume to the ATS checker — it runs the same kind of parsing big-company hiring software uses.
  2. Fix what it flags: keyword density, formatting, and whether your text extracts cleanly.
  3. Ditch multi-column layouts with graphics — they can scramble your work history into gibberish and get you auto-rejected.
  4. Install the browser extension, open a job listing, and check your match score before you apply.

Find it: search TurboApply AI

FREE AI TOOLS · SEP 24

Abacus AI Bot

Open-source AI agent that runs on your own computer and plugs into the messaging apps you already use. 2,000 free credits, no credit card.

  1. Download the desktop app for Windows, Mac, or Linux.
  2. Connect it to the messaging app you already chat in.
  3. Give it personal workflows and multi-step tasks — it runs them as background automations.
  4. When the 2,000 free credits run out, connect your own model API key and keep going.

Find it: search Abacus AI Bot

FREE AI TOOLS · SEP 24

Devin Desktop

AI coding assistant (formerly Windsurf, acquired by Cognition). Free plan includes unlimited tab completions and inline edits.

  1. Download Devin Desktop and open your project.
  2. Code normally — tab completions and inline edits are unlimited on the free plan.
  3. Heavier agent tasks draw from a light quota, and fewer models are available than paid tiers.
  4. Genuinely useful as a daily driver if you're learning to code.

Find it: search Devin Desktop

FREE AI TOOLS · SEP 24

Cline + Ollama

The fully open-source route: Cline, an Apache-licensed coding agent inside VS Code, paired with a local model via Ollama. Ongoing cost: zero.

  1. Install Ollama from ollama.com and pull a code-capable model.
  2. Install the Cline extension in VS Code.
  3. Point Cline at your local Ollama model instead of a paid API.
  4. Your code never leaves your machine and the meter never runs.

AI MONEY · SEP 24

ElevenLabs

Voice licensing marketplace: record about two hours of audio, publish a professional voice clone, earn when creators license it. Creators there have collectively earned $22M+.

  1. Go to elevenlabs.io and create an account.
  2. Record the ~2-hour voice sample following their script guidelines.
  3. Publish your voice to the marketplace with a clear, honest sample.
  4. Know the curve: it's top-heavy — a listing with no paid use earns nothing, so quality and niche matter.
Open ElevenLabs →
AI MONEY · SEP 24

Vast.ai

Rent your idle GPU to AI developers. An RTX 4090 nets roughly $141–314/month after electricity (vs $24–36 mining crypto) — 5–10x better, but earnings depend on uptime and demand.

  1. Go to vast.ai and create a host account.
  2. List your machine with accurate GPU specs.
  3. Set a competitive price and keep uptime high — that's what drives earnings.
  4. Track electricity costs honestly; the spread is the whole game.
Open Vast.ai →

Gumroad

Sell digital products with free listing. The show's recurring 'Do This Today': build one simple product with a free chatbot and list it at $15–29.

AI MONEY · SEP 24

Micro1

Human data gigs for AI training — the labs are out of human data and pay real people. Entry level $15–40/hr; domain specialists writing evaluations earn more.

  1. Apply as a contributor on their site.
  2. Complete evaluations in a domain you actually know — specialists earn the premium rates.
  3. Read the fine print before submitting: contributors often grant irrevocable licenses to voice and movement data.
  4. Treat it as skilled gig work, not passive income.

FROM THE MUSE SERIES

Put Muse to work.

The agent and the connectors that make it useful.

MUSE SERIES

Muse (Meta AI app)

Meta's personal AI agent — the subject of the whole Muse series. Its own email address, phone calls to real businesses, and connectors that plug it into your life.

  1. Get the Meta AI app and set up Muse.
  2. Start with email triage: let it draft replies while you approve them (see 'Inbox Zero, Zero Effort').
  3. Connect one tool at a time — calendar first, then notes and code.
  4. Give it the boring work: bill negotiation drafts, subscription hunting, travel-booking loops.

Find it: search Meta AI app

MUSE SERIES

Muse connectors: Notion · GitHub · 1Password

The plug-ins that make Muse actually useful — notes, code, and passwords, each connected with only the access it needs.

  1. In Muse's settings, open the connectors or integrations section.
  2. Connect Notion for notes and docs (notion.com), GitHub for code (github.com).
  3. Add 1Password (1password.com) so it can fill credentials without you pasting them.
  4. Review permissions per connector — grant the minimum each one needs.
Open Muse connectors: Notion · GitHub · 1Password →
